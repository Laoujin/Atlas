The weekend's geography divides cleanly: Seoul's cultural showpieces — Gyeongbokgung, Bukchon, Gwangjang Market, Seongsu — sit in the historic core north of the Han River, while all 11 Michelin 2 and 3-star restaurants cluster in Gangnam-gu and its immediate neighbors to the south [[1]](https://guide.michelin.com/en/article/michelin-guide-ceremony/all-the-stars-michelin-guide-seoul-busan-2026). That divide is the design principle: immerse in the city's layered history and neighborhoods by day, cross to Gangnam for the marquee dinner.

**The reservation is the critical-path item.** Seoul has exactly one 3-star restaurant — [Mingles](https://www.restaurant-mingles.com/), Chef Mingoo Kang's Korean-contemporary flagship in Cheongdam — retained for a second consecutive year in 2026 [[2]](https://en.sedaily.com/news/2026/03/06/mingles-retains-koreas-only-three-star-michelin-rating-for). It requires 8–12 weeks advance booking; all 10 two-star addresses need 4–8 weeks [[3]](https://seoulkoreaasia.com/how-to-book-michelin-restaurants-in-seoul/). English-language reservations go through [Catchtable](https://www.catchtable.net/exhibition/michelin-guide). Book the table first — which evening it lands on structures everything else.

**June works well for the visit.** Days are long, weather is warm, and rain stays light until the final week [[4]](https://travel-stained.com/korea-in-june-weather-things-to-do/). One friction point: Bukchon Hanok Village's residential alleys enforce a managed quiet zone — tourist entry only 10:00–17:00, with a ₩100,000 fine outside those hours [[5]](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=215658). The Changdeokgung Secret Garden tour is capped and must be pre-booked separately [[6]](https://kculture.com/how-to-book-changdeokgung-secret-garden-jongmyo-online-2026/); both Bukchon and Changdeokgung are in Jongno-gu and walkable together on the same morning, which makes it a natural pairing.

**An unexpected pairing for developers.** The tech events research surfaced a detail that's absent from the leisure-focused children: [COEX](https://www.coexcenter.com/) in Gangnam — host to [Smart Tech Korea](https://en.smarttechkorea.com/Exhibition) (Jun 10–12, 500+ exhibitors) [[7]](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/designing-the-future-with-ai--smart-tech-korea-2026-brings-together-six-shows-shaping-the-next-era-of-smart-connected-industries-302703670.html), [Open Source Summit Korea + MCP Dev Summit](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/open-source-summit-korea/) (Aug 11–14) [[8]](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/open-source-summit-korea/), and [AI Summit Seoul](https://www.aisummitseoul.com/) (Aug 19–21) [[9]](https://www.aisummitseoul.com/) — sits 15 minutes by taxi from Cheongdam and Garosu-gil, where most starred restaurants are concentrated. A developer attending any of these conferences can add the Michelin dinner with minimal logistics friction; post-dinner drinks on Garosu-gil are a 5-minute walk from Mingles, Evett, and Jungsik.

**What this expedition does not resolve:** whether last-minute cancellation slots on Catchtable or Naver Booking are realistic for the popular 2-star tables (lead-time guidance exists but availability patterns don't), and whether the two newest 2026 additions — Sosuheon (new 2-star, sushi, Bugahyeon) [[10]](https://www.postcard.inc/places/sosuheon-seoul-zyeGezgePHo) and Evett (promoted 2024, Australian chef, weekly-rotating menu) [[11]](https://www.broadsheet.com.au/national/food-and-drink/article/evett-seoul-earns-second-michelin-star) — have shorter booking queues than the established names.
