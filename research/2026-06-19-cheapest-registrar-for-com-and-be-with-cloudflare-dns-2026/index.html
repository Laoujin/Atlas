Decision: split the registrar by TLD — neither at-cost registrar sells .be.

→ .com (laoujin.com): move to Cloudflare Registrar, ~US$10.46 / ≈€9.70 at cost[1][2]. You already run DNS on Cloudflare, so its "must use Cloudflare nameservers" rule is a non-issue[3]. Equally good: Porkbun ~US$11.08 / ≈€10.30[5].

→ .be ×4 (sangu.be, ttc-aalst.be, pongit.be, stockoma.be): Cloudflare and Porkbun both refuse .be[1][5]. Cheapest reputable renewals — Strato ≈€4.96, mijn.host ≈€6.99, or Combell ≈€9.99 (Belgian)[6]. Leave one.com, TransIP and Versio — their .be/.com renewals are 2–6× higher[6][7].

The constraint that decides everything

The two cheapest "at-cost" registrars — Cloudflare and Porkbun — do not sell .be. Cloudflare's catalogue omits it[1]; Porkbun's TLD list omits it too (it brokers many ccTLDs via CentralNic but not .be)[5]. .be is run by DNS Belgium and most resellers of it are EU hosting companies. So consolidating all five domains at one registrar means either paying a premium on the .com at a .be host, or accepting a split. The split is cheaper and barely more work — you manage records in Cloudflare via dnscontrol regardless of who the registrar is.

WHOIS privacy: a non-factor for .be, free at the .com winners

For .be, privacy is automatic and free — DNS Belgium hides a private individual's registrant data from WHOIS by GDPR default, and third-party privacy add-ons are explicitly not allowed (because they're unnecessary)[8]. So ignore "privacy" as a .be selection axis entirely. For .com, Cloudflare, Porkbun and Namecheap all include WHOIS privacy free[2][5][13] — don't pay anyone for it.

dnscontrol & nameservers: a near-non-issue

Your records live in Cloudflare and dnscontrol pushes them there via the cloudflareapi provider — that's independent of who registers the domain. The registrar's only DNS job is the nameserver delegation, which you set to Cloudflare once and never touch again. dnscontrol can automate that delegation, but only for registrars it has a registrar provider for — Cloudflare, Namecheap, OVH, Gandi and Porkbun qualify[10][11]; the cheap .be hosts (Strato, mijn.host, Combell, TransIP) do not. Because NS is set-and-forget, this barely matters — set the Cloudflare NS by hand in the .be host's panel and let dnscontrol own the records. For Cloudflare Registrar the NS lock is automatic and is exactly what you already run.

⚠ ttc-aalst.be carries live email (MX/SPF/DMARC). Those are DNS records in Cloudflare, not registrar settings — a registrar move doesn't touch them as long as the nameservers stay pointed at Cloudflare. Transfer the registration, leave the zone alone.

Transfer mechanics

  • .com: an inbound transfer to Cloudflare/Porkbun/Namecheap adds +1 year to the expiry at the at-cost price — effectively a renewal, not a surcharge[13]. Unlock + get the EPP/auth code at one.com/TransIP first. Cloudflare is transfer-only, which fits since the domain already exists[3].
  • .be: DNS Belgium transfers move the registration between resellers; they don't always add a year, so transfer when renewal is due. No privacy step needed[8].
