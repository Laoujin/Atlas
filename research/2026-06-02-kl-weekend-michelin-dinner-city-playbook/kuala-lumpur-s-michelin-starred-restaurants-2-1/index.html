Decision For KL's pinnacle, Dewakan (Malaysia's only 2★, RM870/pp) is unambiguous — book at least a month out.[4] For a strong Saturday-evening alternative: Beta KL (RM550–630++) delivers the most accessible modern-Malaysian storytelling, and Molina (from RM688) offers the sharpest European-Asian contrast. Three of the seven KL stars cluster in TTDI — useful if you want a neighbourhood crawl.[1]

The MICHELIN Guide Kuala Lumpur & Penang 2026 covers 9 starred restaurants across Malaysia; all 7 in KL are listed here.[1] Two restaurants — Akar and Terra Dining — are new additions for 2026, both from TTDI and both rooted in modern Malaysian cuisine.[2]

Two Michelin Stars
★★ Two Stars ♻ Green Star

Dewakan

Chef Darren Teoh  ·  Modern Malaysian  ·  Level 48, Skyviews, Naza Tower, Platinum Park, No. 10 Persiaran KLCC

Malaysia's only two-star restaurant — and its first.[3] Named from dewa (god) + makan (eat), Dewakan serves a 12-course tasting menu built entirely on Malaysian-sourced ingredients — fermented, dry-aged, or foraged in-house. Even the dinnerware is made by local artists. Service takes place 48 floors above KL; the skyline views match the ambition of the food.[4] The restaurant also holds Malaysia's only MICHELIN Green Star, awarded for sustainability practices.[3]

Beta KL

Chef Raymond Tham

"Tour of Malaysia" tasting menu narrates the country's culinary heritage through regional storytelling — blood-cockle "Childhood Weekend" and Musang King durian are signatures.[5] French technique, local soul, cocktail pairings from RM45. One of the more accessible price points and the easiest late-notice reservation in this list.[15]

Thai Fine Dining

Chim by Chef Noom

Chef Noom (founder)  ·  Chef Willy (head chef)

KL's only Thai-cuisine Michelin star. Seasonal multicourse menus change each quarter; current offering: "Naga & the Mekong Legend." Standouts include a 200-year-old tom yum variant and a Hyogo prefecture oyster under pickled chilli granita and caviar.[6] Chef Noom brings Michelin experience from London and Bangkok.[7]

DC by Darren Chin

Chef Darren Chin

Four-floor 1970s TTDI townhouse with four distinct dining modes: Moonbar (cocktails, tapas), DC 1.0 tasting, DC 2.0 private salon, and the Cellar — an 8-seater counter omakase hosted personally by Darren Chin (~RM1,200/pp).[16] In 2025, à la carte was added, opening the kitchen to larger cuts and rustic preparations without a tasting-menu commitment.[8]

European / French-Nordic–Asian

Molina

Chef Guillaume Depoortere  ·  concept: Sidney Schutte

Opened early 2024; starred within 5 months and won the Guide's 2025 Opening of the Year award.[9] 35-cover room on the 51st floor; seafood and vegetables are the kitchen's defining strength. Menus range 5 to 9 courses; Saturday lunch also available.[10] The most explicitly European perspective in KL's starred scene.

Akar

Chef-owner Aidan Low

Named "root" in Malay — Chef Low bridges training in Osaka and Lyon with exclusively Malaysian sourcing. Techniques include cooking in melted beeswax, dry-aging, and claypot searing.[12] Signatures: marble goby in fisherman's broth, mud crab claypot rice, ketupat with silver catfish. Young brigade (avg. under 26) delivers polished, warm service.[11]

Terra Dining

Chef-owner Yu Cheng Chong

Opened 2023 in a TTDI shophouse; starred 2026 for 11 courses of Malaysian produce under French rigour. Lobster bisque carries smoky Tahal oil echoing asam laksa; beurre blanc rides coconut-milk masak lemak.[13] Non-alcoholic tea pairings (with Beca Tea) are a standout alternative to wine.[14]

TTDI Cluster — three stars, one neighbourhood: Akar (109 Jln Aminuddin Baki), Terra Dining (91G Jln Aminuddin Baki), and DC by Darren Chin (44 Persiaran Zaaba) are all within a 5-minute walk of each other in Taman Tun Dr Ismail. A low-key residential feel makes TTDI a pleasant base; the three restaurants account for three of KL's seven Michelin stars.[1]
