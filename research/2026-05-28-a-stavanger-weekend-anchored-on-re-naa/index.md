Three sub-topics, one anchor question collapsed: **RE-NAA is the only Michelin 2-or-above restaurant in Stavanger** — it holds 3★ as of 2024 [[1]](https://business.edgeofnorway.com/news/re-naa-awarded-a-3rd-michelin-star), and the city has zero 2-star restaurants; the next 2-star in Norway is Kontrast in Oslo [[2]](https://www.visitnorway.com/things-to-do/food-and-drink/restaurants/here-are-the-norwegian-michelin-restaurants/). So the dinner picks itself. The non-trivial step is securing it: RE-NAA's 22-seat open-kitchen format books out far in advance [[3]](http://www.restaurantrenaa.no/), which makes the reservation — not the flight or the hike — the gating decision on which weekend the trip happens.

**The hike–dinner sequencing rule is sharper for a 3-star.** The activities plan flags that pairing a Michelin tasting menu with a Preikestolen day is "punishing" after 8–10 hours on your feet [[4]](https://apassionandapassport.com/preikestolen-hike-from-stavanger-norway/). For RE-NAA's ~16-course chef-at-table service [[5]](https://www.eatingoutinstavanger.com/post/fine-dining-has-reached-the-next-level-re-naa) at NOK 4,500 per guest [[3]](http://www.restaurantrenaa.no/), this is non-negotiable: book Saturday or Sunday evening, hike the other day. Late May is specifically the Preikestolen window — trail open, thinning snow, ~5 °C summit, light midweek crowds [[6]](https://preikestolen365.com/hike-preikestolen-off-season/) — while Kjerag's express bus and Lysebotn road aren't open yet [[7]](https://gofjords.com/experiences/hiking/stavanger/kjerag-express-bus-round-trip/).

**A tech-trip variant pivots the calendar but keeps the geography.** [Hello Stavanger](https://www.hellostavanger.no/) (Oct 21–22) is the natural conference anchor [[8]](https://www.hellostavanger.no/), and its venue Tou Scene sits in the same Storhaug / Pedersgata cluster as the city's biggest Nuart stock — including the 32-metre Fintan Magee silo diptych [[9]](https://sandalsand.net/street-art-in-stavanger-norway/) — and the casual-dinner row of Bravo, Hekkan Burger and Bellies. A Tue–Sun "tech + culture" extension walks the same ground twice. **Don't anchor on ONS** (Aug 24–27) by default: 72k visitors saturate hotels city-wide [[10]](https://www.ons.no/), and the parent topic's "lodging is a non-issue" framing breaks for that one week.

**Three booking gotchas span the children.** (1) RE-NAA cannot accommodate vegan diets or guests requiring freedom from milk protein, eggs or citrus — declare at booking, not on arrival [[3]](http://www.restaurantrenaa.no/). (2) [Vinmonopolet](https://www.vinmonopolet.no/) (state monopoly for anything >4.7% ABV) closes Saturdays ~15:00 and all day Sunday — buy wine for the room Friday or order at restaurants [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinmonopolet). (3) All three remaining 2026 conferences run mid-week (Mon–Thu or Wed–Thu) [[8]](https://www.hellostavanger.no/) [[10]](https://www.ons.no/) [[12]](https://www.norwayhealthtech.com/events/the-healthtech-festival-2026-stavanger), so a Fri–Sun add-on is always available.

**Open question left after all three angles ran:** if RE-NAA has no Saturday/Sunday seat on the target weekend, the closest in-city substitute is the 1-star Sabi Omakase — ~20-course omakase counter, the only Stavanger room with comparable chef-at-table intimacy [[2]](https://www.visitnorway.com/things-to-do/food-and-drink/restaurants/here-are-the-norwegian-michelin-restaurants/). The trade against shifting the weekend by two weeks to catch a RE-NAA cancellation wasn't researched here and is the next thing to decide.
