The plan resolves cleanly because Lausanne is small. All three Michelin anchors sit within 7 km of the main station — Pic on the Ouchy lakefront, La Table in the city centre, and the 3-star Hôtel de Ville reachable in ~7 minutes by SBB train [[1]](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Lausanne-Station/Crissier) — and daytime sights cluster in three M2-metro-linked zones (lake, downtown, uphill) along the world's steepest metro at an 11.6 % gradient [[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tro_Lausanne%E2%80%93Ouchy). The restaurant choice therefore drives the day's activity geography more than the itinerary drives the restaurant pick.

**The day-shape constraint that ties it together.** All three Michelin tables run Tue–Sat, so Saturday dinner works at any of them — but **none open Sunday dinner**, and only Pic adds a Sunday lunch from 19 April 2026 [[3]](https://www.anne-sophie-pic.com/en/pic-at-the-beau-rivage-palace) [[4]](https://www.restaurantcrissier.com/en/) [[5]](https://www.lausanne-palace.ch/en/restaurants-bars/la-table-du-lausanne-palace/le-restaurant/). The shape that emerges: Saturday becomes the anchored day (lakefront morning, Lavaux or paddle-steamer afternoon, dinner), Sunday is freer (Plateforme 10, fondue in the old town, cathedral belfry) and ends without a marquee meal. ⚠ A Sunday-anchored weekend narrows the Michelin pick to Pic's Sunday lunch.

**Lavaux belongs on Saturday afternoon.** Route 113 from Saint-Saphorin down to Lutry is ~11.6 km, ~4 h, mostly downhill from 200 m of elevation [[6]](https://swissfamilyfun.com/lavaux-vineyard-hike/), and finishes at a station 8–10 minutes from Lausanne by SBB [[7]](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Lausanne/Cully) — enough margin to shower and make a 19:00 seating at Crissier or Pic. A CGN paddle-steamer Lavaux/Chillon loop runs about three hours [[8]](https://www.cgn.ch/en/la-suisse.html), which is tighter to fit either side of dinner; default to the steamer on a non-Michelin day.

**Free transit removes the friction.** The Lausanne Transport Card, given at hotel check-in and valid the whole stay, covers bus, metro, and regional trains in Mobilis zones 11/12/15/16/18/19 — including the SBB hop to Crissier [[9]](https://www.lausanne-tourisme.ch/en/lausanne-transport-card-and-more/). The activities child's warning that "Lausanne is not an easy walking city" because of three steep hills [[10]](https://tourismattractions.net/switzerland/lausanne-attraction-walkability-guide) is neutralised by M2 and funiculars handling the climbs.

**The IT-calendar mismatch is the honest gap.** The May 29–31 weekend itself has no flagship tech event — only a niche academic Artificial Life workshop at EPFL's Centre Bernoulli [[11]](https://www.epfl.ch/campus/events/). Lausanne's heavy tech weekends are all Q1: AMLD (Feb 10–12) [[12]](https://2026.appliedmldays.org/), Insomni'hack (Mar 16–20) [[13]](https://insomnihack.ch/), HackSummit (Apr 22–23) [[14]](https://www.hacksummit.co/). If pairing a conference with the Michelin anchor is even a soft goal, the trip wants February or April — not this weekend.

The open question: **Crissier vs. Pic for Saturday dinner.** Crissier is the 30-year-old 3-star legacy (Girardet → Rochat → Violier → Giovannini) in a quiet western suburb [[15]](https://www.lausanne-tourisme.ch/en/guide-michelin-switzerland/); Pic is Anne-Sophie Pic's lakefront 2-star with 15 years of tenure at Beau-Rivage Palace and floral-signature cuisine on Lac Léman produce [[3]](https://www.anne-sophie-pic.com/en/pic-at-the-beau-rivage-palace). Pick by what you want the room to feel like, not by the star count.
