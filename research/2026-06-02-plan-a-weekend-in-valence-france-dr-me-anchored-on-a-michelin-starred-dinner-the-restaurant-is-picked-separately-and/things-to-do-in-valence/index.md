> **TL;DR** — Valence itself is a half-day at most: a walking loop of Champ de Mars [[9]](https://www.valence.fr/patrimoine-remarquable/champ-de-mars/), Vieux Valence's côtes [[10]](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieux_Valence), the Maison des Têtes [[4]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/sinspirer/must-sees/maison-des-tetes/) and the Musée de Valence [[5]](https://www.museedevalence.fr/). Spend the other day-and-a-half across the Rhône in **Hermitage wine country** (Tain-l'Hermitage + Tournon, the obvious anchor day) and on **Château de Crussol**, the ruined castle staring back at the city [[27]](https://en.ardeche-guide.com/discover/top-10-explore/castle-crussol/). One real warning: in early June the lavender fields are still green [[65]](https://travelfrancebucketlist.com/provence-lavender-season/) — Drôme provençale lavandin only starts blooming mid-June [[66]](https://routes-lavande.com/en/the-flowering-of-the-lavender/). Plan around that, not for it.

## Geometry of the trip

Valence sits on the Rhône about an hour south of Lyon and just over two hours from Paris by TGV [[73]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence_TGV_station). The historic core is genuinely compact — Champ de Mars, [Parc Jouvet](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/catalog/detail/le-parc-jouvet-150032/), the cathedral and SNCF station all sit inside one walkable rectangle [[85]](https://aroundus.com/p/7431846-champ-de-mars-valence). [Maison Pic](https://www.relaischateaux.com/us/hotel/pic/) is a 15-minute walk south on avenue Victor Hugo [[82]](https://www.relaischateaux.com/us/hotel/pic/). You can do the entire weekend without a car if you base in town and pick day-trips that have a train: Tain-l'Hermitage is 10 minutes by TER, ~27 services a day [[77]](https://www.thetrainline.com/en/train-times/tain-lhermitage-tournon-to-valence-ville).

Pick the full-day Hermitage; **then** pick one of Crest / Grignan / Romans depending on whether you want fortress / literary château / shopping-and-shoes.

## Half-day 1 — A walking loop of central Valence

Start at the **Champ de Mars**, a 3 ha esplanade laid out in 1773 on an alluvial bench of the Rhône, working as a balcony with [Château de Crussol](https://en.ardeche-guide.com/discover/top-10-explore/castle-crussol/) directly across the river [[9]](https://www.valence.fr/patrimoine-remarquable/champ-de-mars/). At its centre is the [Kiosque Peynet](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiosque_Peynet) — the 1890 bandstand by Eugène Poitoux that Raymond Peynet's 1942 "Amoureux" lovers made internationally famous, classified MH 1982 [[8]](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiosque_Peynet). Drop down into **Parc Jouvet**, 7 ha inaugurated in 1905 by President Émile Loubet, labelled *Jardin Remarquable* since 2006 [[7]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/catalog/detail/le-parc-jouvet-150032/).

Climb back into Vieux Valence via the **côtes** — the stepped medieval alleys (Saint-Martin, Saint-Estève, Sainte-Ursule, des Chapeliers, de la Voûte) that Rhône boatmen and muleteers used to reach the upper town [[10]](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieux_Valence). On Grande Rue, the **[Maison des Têtes](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/sinspirer/must-sees/maison-des-tetes/)** (1528–1532, Antoine de Dorne) is the Gothic-to-Renaissance pivot, its facade carved with the Winds, Fortune, Time and allegorical heads [[3]](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_des_T%C3%AAtes_de_Valence); the courtyard is free, no booking [[4]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/sinspirer/must-sees/maison-des-tetes/).

At the cathedral, the **[Pendentif](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/catalog/detail/le-pendentif-121044/)** — a 1548 Renaissance funerary monument for Canon Mistral in the old cloister — is free and was among the very first French monuments listed historic (1840) [[2]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/catalog/detail/le-pendentif-121044/). Plan the **Cathédrale Saint-Apollinaire** around its volunteer-managed hours: Thu 10–12, Sat 8:30–12 & 14–17, Sun 9–11 [[1]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/catalog/detail/valence-2971053/la-cathedrale-saint-apollinaire-121179/). Next door, the **[Musée de Valence – Art et Archéologie](https://www.museedevalence.fr/)** sits in the former bishops' palace, Wed–Sun 10–12:30 / 14–18 (10–18 in Jul–Aug), **closed Mon AND Tue** [[5]](https://www.museedevalence.fr/)[[79]](https://www.museedevalence.fr/en/admission-fees-and-hours); €9/€7 reduced 7 Mar–21 Jun 2026, dropping to €6/€4 from 22 Jun, free under-18 and first Sunday of the month [[6]](https://www.museemusee.com/musees/191-musee-de-valence-art-et-archeologie-valence.html). For a niche bonus, the **Musée des Spahis** at Quartier Baquet covers the 1st Spahis Regiment 1830–1991 — but only Wednesday mornings 9–12, free [[11]](https://www.notrefrance.com/culture/musees/rhone-alpes/drome/valence/musee-des-spahis).

The 19th-century rampart-line is now the **boulevards Bancel/Gambetta**, a Haussmann-style streetscape anchored by Poitoux's 1887 monumental fountain at the Bancel/Maurice-Clerc corner [[12]](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-ville_de_Valence) — worth walking once for the Belle Époque ensemble, not a stop in itself.

## Day 2 — Hermitage: the obvious full-day across the river

The best day-trip is sitting in plain view from your hotel window. Twenty minutes north, **Tain-l'Hermitage** and **Tournon-sur-Rhône** face each other across the river — twenty minutes by car, ten by TER train, ~27 services daily [[77]](https://www.thetrainline.com/en/train-times/tain-lhermitage-tournon-to-valence-ville).

### Walk up the hill

The signature move is to walk the **Hermitage hillside**: ~45 minutes from the Taurobole square in central Tain through the terraced granite vineyards to the **Chapelle Saint-Christophe** at 329 m, with the Drôme valley opening underneath [[15]](https://www.ardeche-hermitage.com/en/focus-on/tain-lhermitage-top-6-visit-ideas/)[[14]](https://www.komoot.com/highlight/3910916). The signposted *Sur les Pas de Gambert, en Hermitage* trail explains the terroir on the climb [[13]](https://www.ardeche-hermitage.com/en/focus-on/hiking-on-the-hermitage-hillside/). Or drive to the **Pierre Aiguille** belvedere and reach the chapel in 10 minutes [[15]](https://www.ardeche-hermitage.com/en/focus-on/tain-lhermitage-top-6-visit-ideas/).

### Chocolate, bridge, château

The **[Cité du Chocolat Valrhona](https://www.citeduchocolat.com/en)** in Tain is the perfect rainy-morning anchor — Mon 10–12 / 13–18:30, Tue–Sat 9–12 / 13–18:30, Sun & holidays 10–12 / 13–17:30 [[16]](https://www.citeduchocolat.com/en). Cross the river on the **Marc Seguin footbridge**, built 1847–1849 and the oldest suspension bridge still in service in France [[17]](https://structurae.net/en/structures/marc-seguin-bridge); the Seguin brothers' even older 1825 bridge here was the world's first wire-cable suspension bridge [[18]](https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/marc-seguin/). On the Tournon side, the **[Château-Musée de Tournon](https://www.ardeche-hermitage.com/en/catalog/detail/tournon-sur-rhone-2972214/chateau-musee-129669/)** is daily 18 Mar–1 Nov 2026; May–June afternoons only (14–18), €5 / €4 reduced [[19]](https://www.ardeche-hermitage.com/en/catalog/detail/tournon-sur-rhone-2972214/chateau-musee-129669/). The **[Train de l'Ardèche](https://www.trainardeche.fr/en/)** steam train leaves Saint-Jean-de-Muzols at 10:15 for the Gorges du Doux, returning 17:00 — full-day commitment, kid-friendly, swallows the rest of your Tournon afternoon [[20]](https://www.trainardeche.fr/en/).

## Outdoor: Crussol and a couple of cols

**Château de Crussol** is the silhouette on the cliff across the Rhône — a ruined castle reached by a moderate ~4.8 km / ~210 m / 1.5–2 h loop [[26]](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/france/ardeche/chateau-de-crussol-et-ses-cretes), or a 20-minute push from the foot-of-castle car park, 40 minutes from the Rhône Crussol tourist office [[27]](https://en.ardeche-guide.com/discover/top-10-explore/castle-crussol/). The ridge is mostly exposed limestone — recommended months are **April–June and September–October**, so an early-June visit is in-window but go early with water and a hat [[28]](https://guidetoeurope.eu/france/attractions/details/chateau-de-crussol).

For a quieter outdoor hour in town, the **Bois des Naix** is a 5 km loop across 12 ha of mature woodland with fitness stations and the Vallée des Fleurs aquatic garden [[32]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/les-voyageurs-vous-parlent/top-10-family-walks-around-valenceromans/) — billed as the 2nd-largest urban park in the former Rhône-Alpes region, with a 20-beacon orienteering course; ⚠ bikes, music and dogs are prohibited [[36]](https://www.drome-cestmanature.com/fiches/le-bois-des-naix/).

For drives into the Vercors edge: **Col de Tourniol** (1,145 m, via Barbières, quiet road, under an hour) [[34]](https://alpes4ever.com/france/drome/les-plus-de-1000-m/col-de-tourniol/); **Col de la Bataille** (1,313 m, ~45 min, panorama of the south Vercors combes) [[35]](https://www.cols-cyclisme.com/vercors/france/col-de-la-bataille-depuis-leoncel-c573.htm); and the further **[Col de Rousset](https://en.vercors-drome.com/sejour-vacances-vercors/162012_route-et-belvedere-du-col-de-rousset/)** (1,254 m tunnel) with twin orientation tables and a chair lift to the upper belvedere (summer + winter) [[33]](https://en.vercors-drome.com/sejour-vacances-vercors/162012_route-et-belvedere-du-col-de-rousset/).

### Cycling the ViaRhôna

The ViaRhôna EV17 long-distance route runs along the river through Valence. The easy ride is the **Tain → Valence** stretch — ~19 km of stabilised greenway through Hermitage vineyards [[31]](https://en.ardeche-guide.com/itinerary/cycling-road-from-tain-lhermitage-to-valence-420014/). For rentals, **[Carbone Zéro](https://www.viarhona.com/pratique/loueurs-reparateurs-de-velo/carbone-zero-magasin-gare-centre-ville)** at 24 rue Denis Papin (next to the gare) does classic and e-bikes and is Accueil Vélo certified [[29]](https://www.viarhona.com/pratique/loueurs-reparateurs-de-velo/carbone-zero-magasin-gare-centre-ville); ViaRhôna-wide e-bike daily rates run €35–55 and high-season pickups should be booked ~1 month ahead [[30]](https://www.francevelotourisme.com/viarhona-a-velo-electrique). For ten-minute hops inside town, **Libélo** bike-share has 68 stations, 431 bikes including e-bikes (VAE), first 30 min free, then €0.50/30 min [[78]](https://valence-info.fr/velo-libre-service-valence/).

## What to eat besides the dinner

The Drôme has four edible icons — Suisse, ravioles, pogne, Picodon — plus the charcuterie tradition. Hit at least two.

**[Suisse de Valence](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/fr/les-voyageurs-vous-parlent/le-suisse-connaissez-vous-cette-specialite-de-valence-dans-la-drome/)** — a soldier-shaped orange-blossom shortbread with candied peel, created to honour the Swiss Guards who escorted Pope Pius VI's body after he died in Valence in 1799; named producers include Nivon and Pâtisserie Giraud in Valence, Maison Guillet in Romans, Pascalis in Bourg-de-Péage [[37]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/fr/les-voyageurs-vous-parlent/le-suisse-connaissez-vous-cette-specialite-de-valence-dans-la-drome/). The reference is **[Maison Nivon](https://nivon.com/nos-specialites/le-suisse-nivon/)**, founded 1856 by Félix Henry Léon Nivon; 250 g ≈ €8.50 [[38]](https://nivon.com/nos-specialites/le-suisse-nivon/).

**Ravioles du Dauphiné** — tiny pasta pillows of Comté, fresh cheese and herbs; Label Rouge + IGP since 2009, production restricted to Romans–Saint-Marcellin [[39]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/les-voyageurs-vous-parlent/famous-ravioles/). For a sit-down go to **[La Ravioline](https://fr.gaultmillau.com/en/restaurants/la-ravioline)** at 18 rue Dauphine — poached ravioles, ravioline burger, even Valrhona-chocolate dessert ravioles, chef Alexandre from Romans [[40]](https://fr.gaultmillau.com/en/restaurants/la-ravioline). For free factory + tasting, the **[Cité de la Raviole](https://www.entrepriseetdecouverte.fr/visite/la-cite-de-la-raviole/)** at Mère Maury in Mours-Saint-Eusèbe (just past Romans) is free [[54]](https://www.entrepriseetdecouverte.fr/visite/la-cite-de-la-raviole/).

**[Pogne de Romans](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/fr/les-voyageurs-vous-parlent/la-pogne-sp%C3%A9cialit%C3%A9-locale-de-romans-dans-la-dr%C3%B4me/)** — crown brioche perfumed with orange blossom, medieval Easter origin [[41]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/fr/les-voyageurs-vous-parlent/la-pogne-sp%C3%A9cialit%C3%A9-locale-de-romans-dans-la-dr%C3%B4me/). The four-generation reference producer is **[Maison Pascalis](https://boutique.maison-pascalis.com/en/blogs/news/c-est-quoi-la-pogne-de-romans)** in Bourg-de-Péage, founded 1892 [[42]](https://boutique.maison-pascalis.com/en/blogs/news/c-est-quoi-la-pogne-de-romans).

**[Picodon AOP](https://www.picodon-aop.fr/)** — small firm raw-goat cheese from Drôme/Ardèche, AOP since 1983, name from Provençal *picaoudou* "small spicy cheese" [[43]](https://www.picodon-aop.fr/)[[44]](https://www.avignon-et-provence.com/en/local-products/picodon-goats-cheese-drome). It sits naturally on the Drôme charcuterie board alongside the **caillette de Chabeuil** [[50]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/be-inspired/flavours-terroir/local-specialities/).

### Markets

The big one is **Saturday morning on [Place des Clercs](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/catalog/detail/valence-2971053/marche-du-samedi-place-des-clercs-426595/)**: 7am–2pm year-round, ~100 stalls spilling toward the cathedral [[47]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/catalog/detail/valence-2971053/marche-du-samedi-place-des-clercs-426595/). Other days rotate: Mon Danton, Tue Europe, Wed Paix, Thu Clercs, Fri Faventines, plus the **Place Saint-Jean covered hall** Tue 16–19 [[48]](https://www.atrium-hotel.fr/en/2022/04/07/markets-valence/). ⚠ **Halle Terroir** — the new gourmet food hall in the former Jean-Bouin hall in the basse-ville (one restaurant + 19 food merchants) — was targeted for October 2025 but slipped ~3 months because of protected martins nesting in the roof; check the live opening status before banking on it [[49]](https://www.cheriefmvalleedurhone.fr/valence-cest-parti-pour-le-chantier-des-halles-gourmandes/).

## Day-trips further afield

You only get one of these on a weekend. Pick on theme.

**Romans-sur-Isère** (~25 min) — for shoes and ravioles. The **[Musée international de la Chaussure](https://www.museedelachaussure.fr/english)** holds 16,500 historic shoes spanning 4,000 years in a former Visitation convent [[51]](https://www.museedelachaussure.fr/english). **[Marques Avenue Romans](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/sinspirer/must-sees/marques-avenue/)** packs 80+ outlet boutiques in a renovated barracks: 30% off year-round, up to 70% in sales [[53]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/sinspirer/must-sees/marques-avenue/). Worth adding the free Cité de la Raviole tasting [[54]](https://www.entrepriseetdecouverte.fr/visite/la-cite-de-la-raviole/).

**Crest** (~29 min) — for vertical drama. The **[Tour de Crest](https://www.tourdecrest.fr/en/)** is the tallest medieval keep in France at 52 m, open daily 10–19 May–Sept [[55]](https://www.tourdecrest.fr/en/); built in the 13th century, a state prison 17th–mid 19th [[56]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_Crest).

**Mirmande** (~31 min) — for a Plus-Beau-Village lunch. Stone hilltop village above the Rhône, revived by cubist André Lhote's summer school [[60]](https://www.les-plus-beaux-villages-de-france.org/fr/nos-villages/mirmande/).

**Grignan** (~59 min) — for Madame de Sévigné. **2026 is the official Année Sévigné** marking 400 years since her birth; the **[Château de Grignan](https://www.chateaudegrignan.fr/2026-annee-sevigne/)** runs a full event programme including a 30 Apr–3 May historical-reenactment weekend [[58]](https://www.chateaudegrignan.fr/2026-annee-sevigne/). If you have any literary inclination, this is the day-trip in 2026.

**Forêt de Saoû** (~45 min) — for a hike. A 2,500 ha "most beautiful perched syncline in Europe", topped by **Les Trois Becs** at 1,589 m [[62]](https://www.drome-tourism.com/fiches/foret-de-saou/). Full Trois Becs loop ≈ 10 km / 1,130 m / 6 h; the short Mont Veyou version ≈ 6 km / 3.5 h / 660 m [[63]](https://www.visorando.com/en/walk-les-trois-becs-foret-de-saou-en-drome/). ⚠ Sections close **15 May–15 July** to protect chamois during calving — your early-June visit is partially impacted, so check the current zone map before driving out [[64]](https://www.campingmornans.com/en/actu/foret-de-saou).

**Nyons** (~1 h) — for olives. **[La Maison des Huiles d'Olive](https://www.dromeprovencale.fr/en/patrimoine-culturel/la-maison-des-huiles-dolive-et-olives-de-france/)** runs tastings and a permanent exhibition, open 18 Mar–23 Dec 2026 [[67]](https://www.dromeprovencale.fr/en/patrimoine-culturel/la-maison-des-huiles-dolive-et-olives-de-france/); the **[Vignolis](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g1497613-d7209444-Reviews-Vignolis_Cooperative_du_Nyonsais-Nyons_Drome_Auvergne_Rhone_Alpes.html)** cooperative (founded 1923) does mill tours [[68]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g1497613-d7209444-Reviews-Vignolis_Cooperative_du_Nyonsais-Nyons_Drome_Auvergne_Rhone_Alpes.html).

**Wine geeks** can push further to **Suze-la-Rousse**, whose château houses the [Université du Vin](https://universite-du-vin.com/) (founded 1978, 100+ courses for pros and amateurs) [[70]](https://universite-du-vin.com/)[[71]](https://www.avignon-et-provence.com/en/monuments/university-wine-suze-rousse). **Romantics** push to **Le Poët-Laval**, a 12th-c. Hospitaller commandery classed Plus Beau Village in the Jabron valley [[72]](https://www.les-plus-beaux-villages-de-france.org/fr/nos-villages/le-poet-laval/).

### Lavender reality check

If you came hoping for purple fields: you're early. **Drôme provençale lavandin starts blooming mid-June in the plain (Grignan, Tricastin) and runs to mid-July at altitude** [[66]](https://routes-lavande.com/en/the-flowering-of-the-lavender/). In early June the fields are still green [[65]](https://travelfrancebucketlist.com/provence-lavender-season/). Grignan in early June is the château, not the fields.

## Suggested two-day shape

A defensible shape if you arrive Friday evening and leave Sunday evening:

- **Sat AM**: Saturday market on Place des Clercs [[47]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/catalog/detail/valence-2971053/marche-du-samedi-place-des-clercs-426595/) → Vieux Valence walking loop → Maison des Têtes courtyard [[4]](https://www.valence-romans-tourisme.com/en/sinspirer/must-sees/maison-des-tetes/) → Musée de Valence [[5]](https://www.museedevalence.fr/) → Champ de Mars / Parc Jouvet [[9]](https://www.valence.fr/patrimoine-remarquable/champ-de-mars/).
- **Sat PM**: Cross to Crussol, hike up before it gets hot (early-June still OK) [[28]](https://guidetoeurope.eu/france/attractions/details/chateau-de-crussol). Suisse + ravioles tasting somewhere on the way back.
- **Sat evening**: Michelin dinner.
- **Sun**: Hermitage day. TER to Tain [[77]](https://www.thetrainline.com/en/train-times/tain-lhermitage-tournon-to-valence-ville). Cité du Chocolat [[16]](https://www.citeduchocolat.com/en) → Hermitage hill walk [[15]](https://www.ardeche-hermitage.com/en/focus-on/tain-lhermitage-top-6-visit-ideas/) → tasting at Cave de Tain (book) [[23]](https://www.cavedetain.com/en/) → Marc Seguin footbridge [[17]](https://structurae.net/en/structures/marc-seguin-bridge) → Tournon château [[19]](https://www.ardeche-hermitage.com/en/catalog/detail/tournon-sur-rhone-2972214/chateau-musee-129669/). Back to Valence by early evening.

Swap **Sun = Hermitage** for **Sun = Grignan** if you want the Année-Sévigné year [[58]](https://www.chateaudegrignan.fr/2026-annee-sevigne/), or **Sun = Crest** if you want the keep [[55]](https://www.tourdecrest.fr/en/) — but if it's your first time, Hermitage wins. The vineyards are walking distance from the train and the Michelin meal will sit better on the way home with a glass of Crozes than after a day at the outlet mall.
