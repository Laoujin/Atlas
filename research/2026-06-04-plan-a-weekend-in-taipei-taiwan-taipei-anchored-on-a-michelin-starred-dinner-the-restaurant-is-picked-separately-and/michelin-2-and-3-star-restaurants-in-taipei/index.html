Pick for your anchor dinner: Le Palais for definitive Cantonese prestige — 8 consecutive three-star years, set menus from NT$4,880 (lowest 3-star entry in Taipei) [4]. Taïrroir if you want the world's first 3-star Taiwanese restaurant, where Chef Kai Ho weaves local terroir through French technique (~NT$9,000 tasting) [8]. At 2-star: logy (NT$4,750, Japanese-Asian fusion) offers the best creativity-to-price ratio [13]; L'Atelier de Joël Robuchon (from NT$2,380) is the easiest to book online in English [11].

Taipei has 9 restaurants in the two- or three-star tier of the 2025 Michelin Guide Taiwan [1] (8th edition): 2 three-star and 7 two-star. Three restaurants earned their second star for the first time in 2025 [2]. The 2026 guide uses monthly rolling additions; star tiers are from the last annual ceremony (August 2025) [3].

Le Palais 頤宮中餐廳

Cantonese Datong District
Location: 17F, Palais de Chine Hotel, 3 Chengde Rd Sec.1 (near Taipei Main Station)
Price: Set menus NT$4,880–NT$24,880 +10% service; dim sum à la carte from NT$380

Character: Taipei's most storied three-star. Classic Cantonese executed at the highest level — har gow, roast duck (order ahead for whole bird), braised abalone, bird's nest. Grand hotel booths with private room options. Widest price range of any starred restaurant here.

Booking Notes

  • Book 4–8 weeks ahead for logy, Taïrroir, and Mudan — all fill quickly and seats are limited. [13] [9]
  • English-friendly online booking: L'Atelier via inline.app [12], Taïrroir via tairroir.com, Molino via OpenTable, logy via logy.tw.
  • Eika and Yu Kapo use local platforms (AutoReserve, Facebook) — less intuitive for non-Mandarin speakers; confirm by phone or have your hotel assist. [23]
  • Le Palais offers the widest price window: dim sum à la carte starts at NT$380, making a lighter lunch possible; full set menus are the starred experience. [6]
  • Mudan: only two sittings per day (12:30 and 18:30); walk-ins not possible. [18]
