The walk-to-dinner constraint collapses the lodging question harder than it first looks. L'Oustau de Baumanière (3★ since 2020 under Glenn Viel [[1]](https://www.baumaniere.com/en/actualites/our-3rd-star-shines-again/)) sits in a private vallon below the medieval village; the only zero-walk option is the Baumanière estate itself, where the rooms and the table share an address [[2]](https://www.baumaniere.com/en/accommodation/). The cheaper neighbours — La Riboto de Taven (3 troglodyte rooms in the same Vallon de la Fontaine [[3]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g1819081-d310356-Reviews-Taven_Residences-Les_Baux_de_Provence_Bouches_du_Rhone_Provence_Alpes_Cote_d_Azur.html)) and Hostellerie de la Reine-Jeanne (4 rooms in the village above [[4]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g1819081-d219938-Reviews-Hostellerie_de_la_Reine_Jeanne-Les_Baux_de_Provence_Bouches_du_Rhone_Provence_Alpes_Co.html)) are the only properties for which "walk back after a 3-hour tasting menu, in the dark" is honestly true. Hôtel Benvengudo deserves the loudest cross-cut: it has a Route d'Arles address ~1.4 km away on the same road, and the character sub-topic admires it as a restored mas [[5]](https://benvengudo.com/en/) — but recent guests say the D78F stretch has no proper footpath and the property is "not walkable" without a car [[6]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g1819081-d219939-Reviews-Hotel_Benvengudo-Les_Baux_de_Provence_Bouches_du_Rhone_Provence_Alpes_Cote_d_Azur.html). If the user's rule is hard, Benvengudo (and Domaine de Manville at 25–30 min on foot [[7]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g1819081-d5895678-Reviews-Domaine_De_Manville-Les_Baux_de_Provence_Bouches_du_Rhone_Provence_Alpes_Cote_d_Azur.html)) become "sleep there, taxi to dinner" — a different deal.

Two non-obvious dependencies link the lodging and activity sub-topics. **First**, the special-character shortlist quietly doubles the Michelin-meal count: Domaine de Manville's L'Aupiho (Lieven Van Aken) [[8]](https://guide.michelin.com/us/en/provence-alpes-cote-dazur/les-baux-de-provence/restaurant/l-aupiho) and Hôtel de Tourrel's La Table [[9]](https://guide.michelin.com/us/en/hotels-stays/saint-remy-de-provence/hotel-de-tourrel-7920) each carry one star — a Friday-night room-and-board pairing that turns the weekend into two Michelin meals without driving for either. **Second**, every village that surfaced as a lodging is also on the day-trip map: Le Paradou (Le Hameau des Baux) sits on the way to the Cornille olive cooperative in Maussane [[10]](https://www.moulin-cornille.com/en/); Saint-Rémy (Tourrel, Château des Alpilles, Mas de l'Amarine) **is** the Wednesday-market and Van-Gogh / Glanum day [[11]](https://provence-alpes-cotedazur.com/en/things-to-do/events/all-events/weekly-provencal-market-of-saint-remy-de-provence-saint-remy-de-provence-en-2867828/); the AOP Les Baux-de-Provence wine appellation has, since the 2023 vintage, required 100% organic viticulture across all producers [[12]](https://oray-wine.com/en/a-trip-to-the-aoc-baux-de-provence-the-stronghold-of-organic-biodynamic-winemaking/), so any Friday-afternoon cellar visit slots into the same itinerary you were already planning.

Three seasonal hazards bind all four sub-topics into the same calendar. The restaurant closes roughly 26 January – 6 March each year [[13]](https://www.baumaniere.com/en/infos-contact/), Mas de l'Oulivié is shut through 26 March 2026 [[14]](https://www.masdeloulivie.com/en/le-mas-de-loulivie/the-facilities/), and Hôtel de Tourrel only reopens mid-March [[15]](https://www.designhotels.com/hotels/france/provence/saint-remy-de-provence/hotel-de-tourrel/) — so winter weekends collapse the menu to Manville, Hameau, Château des Alpilles or Benvengudo and rule out L'Oustau itself. From 1 June onward the Bouches-du-Rhône prefecture closes the Alpilles massif by daily 18:00 decree for fire risk [[16]](https://www.parc-alpilles.fr/actualite/acces-aux-massifs-forestiers/), so Sunday's Mont Gaussier hike is a check-the-map-the-night-before plan rather than a booking. And the Mistral wind — 50 km/h average, gusting to 100 km/h, often multi-day [[17]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_(wind)) — is the off-switch on ridge walks at any time of year.

The IT-conferences side-question changes the trip's framing in one direction only: there is no DevFest, Agile Tour, JS/Python/Go conference or hackathon in Avignon, Arles or the Alpilles in 2026 — the nearest real developer conference is WAX at Marseille's Orange Vélodrome on 1 October 2026, a 90 km drive [[18]](https://www.waxconf.fr/). Anchor the trip on the dinner, not on a conference.

Open question worth answering before booking: at L'Oustau, does a room reservation actually unlock or accelerate a Saturday-evening table booking, or are the two queues independent? The children all assume independent queues — book the dinner first, then chase a room — but the property is a Relais & Châteaux estate where the table and the bed share an operator, and that's the one piece of dinner-mechanics the children couldn't verify without calling.
