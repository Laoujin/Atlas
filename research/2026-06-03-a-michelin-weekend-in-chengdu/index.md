The dinner is the fixed point; everything else orbits it. Chengdu has exactly two Michelin 2-star restaurants and no 3-stars after four guide editions [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Michelin-starred_restaurants_in_Chengdu). They represent opposite philosophies: **Yu Zhi Lan** is an unmarked 18-seat house in Qingyang, owner-chef Lan Guijun's ~20-course modern Sichuan degustation at ~600–2,380 RMB [[2]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g297463-d7211656-Reviews-Yu_ZhiLan-Chengdu_Sichuan.html); **Xin Rong Ji** is a high-rise financial-district operation bringing Taizhou coastal seafood to a spice-forward city, Asia's 50 Best–ranked [[3]](https://www.enprimeurclub.com/restaurants/xin-rong-ji-chengdu-restaurant) at a comparable price.

That choice also shapes routing. Yu Zhi Lan sits at Changfa Street, Qingyang [[4]](https://chengdu-expat.com/places/yu-zhi-lan-chengdu/) — roughly 2 km from the Wuhou Shrine–Jinli cluster [[5]](https://us.trip.com/travel-guide/attraction/chengdu/chengdu-wuhou-shrine-museum-76341/), so a day flows cleanly: panda base at 7:30 AM opening [[6]](https://m.panda.org.cn/en/service/ticket/), Wuhou Shrine + Jinli lanterns in the afternoon, Yu Zhi Lan for dinner. Xin Rong Ji is in Wuhou District's financial center, a taxi ride from anywhere with no neighbourhood pairing implied.

**Booking sequence matters more than itinerary.** Yu Zhi Lan books 1–2 weeks ahead with a 50% non-refundable deposit [[2]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g297463-d7211656-Reviews-Yu_ZhiLan-Chengdu_Sichuan.html) and likely requires a Mandarin speaker by phone. WeChat Pay or Alipay setup should come first — foreign-card binding takes 24–72 hours under late-2025 regulations [[7]](https://hiddenchinatravel.com/alipay-for-foreigners-china/). Panda base tickets open 14 days ahead and sell out; book them the same day you confirm the restaurant [[6]](https://m.panda.org.cn/en/service/ticket/).

**Two active closures to route around**: Jinsha Site Museum is shut through April 2027 [[8]](https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202511/10/WS69119ffa498e3685503373d7/jinsha-site-museum-set-to-close-temporarily-for-upgrade.html); Dujiangyan's panda volunteer base closed April 23, 2026 [[9]](https://www.chinadiscovery.com/panda-tours/panda-volunteer-reference.html). The Chengdu Museum (free, open to 20:30 Fri–Sat [[10]](https://www.cdmuseum.com/en/zhinan/)) replaces Jinsha for ancient-Shu history. Day trips to Leshan Giant Buddha or the Dujiangyan irrigation site remain viable — the closure only removes panda volunteering, not the UNESCO canal.

The reported calendar places April (C3 Security Apr 17–18 [[11]](https://www.asiainfo.com/en_us/content_5119.html); China Network Audio-Visual Conference Apr 14–16 [[12]](https://www.nrta.gov.cn/art/2026/4/15/art_112_73087.html)) and July (electronics expos at Century City) as the peak conference windows. If the trip overlaps either, the events are daytime-only and leave evenings free for the dinner.

Yu Zhi Lan has been the Chengdu culinary pilgrimage since Bourdain filmed it in 2016 [[4]](https://chengdu-expat.com/places/yu-zhi-lan-chengdu/) and holds a World's 50 Best Discovery listing [[13]](https://www.theworlds50best.com/discovery/Establishments/China/Chengdu/Yu-Zhi-Lan.html) — but it demands friction: Mandarin phone call, non-refundable deposit, unmarked door. Is that singular Sichuan experience worth the booking overhead, or does Xin Rong Ji's English-friendly reservation process and flexible à la carte format serve the trip better?
