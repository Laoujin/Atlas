> **TL;DR:** KK's strangeness comes in distinct flavours, and the best ones spread out from the city. In town: a wooden malaria-memorial clock tower that outlived WWII [[14]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/atkinson-clock-tower/), a plane-crash monument on its exact crash spot [[17]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/double-six-gallery/), and a sea-gypsy pearl market [[26]](https://www.penang-traveltips.com/malaysia/sabah/kota-kinabalu/filipino-market.htm). Day-trip orbit: a house of 42 real headhunter skulls [[3]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/monsopiad-village-and-house-of-skulls/), an upside-down kampung house [[8]](https://rumahterbalik.com/rumah-terbalik/), live wriggling grubs you eat head-first [[32]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sago-worm-butod/), a wild-river fish massage [[40]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/tagal-sungai-moroli-kampung-luanti-fish-massage/), and the original *Survivor* island with a bubbling mud volcano [[42]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/pulau-tiga-survivor-island/). For a semi-adventurous couple with a car (or a driver), almost all of it is half-day-trip-able. Rate used throughout: ~RM4.69 = €1 (early June 2026) [[57]](https://www.exchange-rates.org/exchange-rate-history/eur-myr-2026).

Each find below is tagged **[where it is]** and **(touristy)** ↔ **(offbeat)**. No two are the same kind of strange.

## Macabre & eccentric museums

**House of Skulls, Monsopiad Cultural Village** — *[Penampang, 15 min from KK] (offbeat-ish, low crowds)*. A heritage village run by the literal descendants of Monsopiad, an 18th-century Kadazan headhunter, displaying **42 genuine human skulls** of his vanquished enemies strung from the rafters [[3]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/monsopiad-village-and-house-of-skulls/)[[4]](https://www.amazingborneo.com/package/monosopiad-cultural-village-tour-home-of-the-headhunters). The skulls are tied to ritual, not gore — kept as protection and spiritual balance — and a 300-year-old monolith (*Gintutun do Mohoing*) stands in the yard [[3]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/monsopiad-village-and-house-of-skulls/). Time it for late April and you can sit in on the Magavau thanksgiving rite in the skull house itself [[3]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/monsopiad-village-and-house-of-skulls/).

**Sabah State Museum** — *[KK city, Bukit Istana Lama] (touristy but quiet)*. The main building is a giant concrete [Rungus longhouse](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/malaysia/malaysian-borneo-sabah/kota-kinabalu/attractions/sabah-museum/a/poi-sig/480202/356955) [[1]](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/malaysia/malaysian-borneo-sabah/kota-kinabalu/attractions/sabah-museum/a/poi-sig/480202/356955) whose roof beams mimic dancers' outstretched arms. Inside: a whale skeleton hung overhead and a cabinet of headhunting skulls — an odd one-two of natural history and the macabre [[2]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g298307-d1897763-Reviews-Sabah_State_Museum-Kota_Kinabalu_Kota_Kinabalu_District_West_Coast_Division_Sabah.html).

**Mari Mari Cultural Village** — *[Inanam, ~25 min from KK] (touristy)*. A living open-air museum of five tribes where guides demonstrate bamboo fire-starting, blowpipe-making and traditional tattooing, and skulls in the Dusun and Murut houses flag a century of headhunting [[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari_Mari_Cultural_Village)[[6]](https://marimariculturalvillage.my/). The polished, touristy counterpoint to Monsopiad's raw one.

## Quirky buildings & monuments

**Rumah Terbalik (Upside Down House)** — *[Tamparuli, 30 min from KK] (touristy, very Instagram)*. A fully furnished Sabah kampung house — kitchen, beds, veranda, car in the carport — built **completely upside down**, with you walking on what should be the ceiling [[7]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/upside-down-house-rumah-terbalik/). It's in the Malaysia Book of Records as the country's first (2012) [[8]](https://rumahterbalik.com/rumah-terbalik/), and sits beside a 3D Wonders Museum billed as Borneo's first [[9]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/rumah-terbalik-3d-wonders-museum/). The interior tour is a brisk 5-10 min and photos inside are banned [[7]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/upside-down-house-rumah-terbalik/).

**Atkinson Clock Tower** — *[KK city, below Signal Hill] (offbeat, often overlooked)*. KK's oldest standing structure: an all-wood tower of merbau hardwood, built in 1905 by a grieving English mother for her son Francis Atkinson, the first district officer, dead of malaria at 28 [[14]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/atkinson-clock-tower/). It famously survived the WWII flattening of the city that took almost everything else [[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atkinson_Clock_Tower). As of late 2025 it's the trailhead of a new 500 m elevated forest walkway toward Signal Hill; expect RM10 / €2.10 entry (RM20 / €4.30 foreigners) once 2026 fees kick in [[16]](https://www.theborneopost.com/2025/11/15/atkinson-clock-tower-a-timeless-symbol-of-unity-and-progress/)[[57]](https://www.exchange-rates.org/exchange-rate-history/eur-myr-2026).

**Double Six Monument** — *[Sembulan, KK city] (offbeat, sombre)*. A memorial built on the **exact spot** where a Sabah Air Nomad crashed on 6 June 1976, killing chief minister Tun Fuad Stephens and several state ministers [[17]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/double-six-gallery/)[[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Six_Monument). The adjacent gallery displays actual wreckage debris, and the crash still feeds whispered conspiracy theories among Sabahans [[17]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/double-six-gallery/)[[19]](https://www.aviation.my/flights/1976-sabah-air-gaf-nomad-crash-double-six-tragedy/). A genuinely strange, eerie civic site most tourists never reach.

**Tip of Borneo (Tanjung Simpang Mengayau)** — *[Kudat day-trip, ~215 km / 3.5 h] (touristy at the lookout, remote getting there)*. A bronze globe marks the northernmost point of Borneo, where the South China and Sulu seas collide [[20]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/the-tip-of-borneo-tanjung-simpang-mengayau/). The name means "junction to the battle" — this headland was a pirate watch-point [[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tip_of_Borneo). Entry is free [[22]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/the-tip-of-borneo-tanjung-simpang-mengayau-in-kudat/). A long haul, best paired with an overnight.

## Abandoned & haunted

**Signal Hill colonial ruins** — *[KK city, Signal Hill ridge] (offbeat, urbex)*. Abandoned colonial-era mansions overgrown above the city, documented by local urbex YouTubers for their graffiti and skyline views [[11]](https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=oEodkTKYfp0); derelict houses around KK periodically go viral as "creepy" finds [[10]](http://dailyexpress.com.my/news/277526/creepy-abandoned-house-draws-attention/). Not an official attraction — go with respect and care.

**Kinarut Mansion** — *[Kinarut, ~30 min south] (offbeat, decayed)*. A rare 1910-14 stone house (most of North Borneo built in wood), now a roofless ruin that features on Malaysia's "haunted homes" lists, with the usual reports of apparitions and voices after dark [[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinarut_Mansion)[[13]](https://www.starproperty.my/news/haunted-homes-around-malaysia/115497).

## Water villages & strange markets

**Mengkabong stilt village** — *[Tuaran, ~45 min from KK] (offbeat, a working community)*. A Bajau "sea gypsy" village built out over an estuary, houses linked by rickety plank walks, reachable by river kayak [[23]](https://cityseeker.com/kota-kinabalu/192344-mengkabong-water-village). The Bajau are Sabah's second-largest group and live genuinely between land and sea [[24]](https://terra-cultura.com/en/the-bajau-of-kota-kinabalu-a-story-between-land-and-sea/) — though rising seas and erosion are slowly eating the village, forcing constant rebuilding [[25]](https://www.penang-traveltips.com/malaysia/sabah/mengkabong-water-village.htm). Visit quietly; this is a home, not a set.

**Filipino Market (Pasar Filipino)** — *[KK waterfront] (touristy but characterful)*. A warren of stalls run by Sulu-island immigrants, the city's best source of cheap **Sabah pearls** (freshwater and seawater, made into jewellery on the spot) and Bajau/Suluk handicraft [[26]](https://www.penang-traveltips.com/malaysia/sabah/kota-kinabalu/filipino-market.htm)[[27]](https://travel.earth/filipino-market-in-kota-kinabalu-sabah/). Haggle hard.

**Tamu Besar, Kota Belud** — *[Kota Belud, ~75 km / 1.5 h] (offbeat, very local)*. Sabah's biggest open-air *tamu* — a rotating market tradition unique to the state [[28]](https://sabahtourism.com/activity/tamu-weekly-market-in-sabah/). The annual late-October *Tamu Besar* layers on **buffalo auctions, buffalo racing**, and a beauty-queen crowning [[29]](https://www.sabahtravel.com/articles/post/visit-the-biggest-market-in-sabah-tamu-besar-kota-belud). Its showpiece is a parade of Bajau horsemen — Sabah's "Cowboys of the East" — galloping in silver-studded ceremonial dress [[30]](https://sabahguide.com/ethnic-groups/bajau/)[[31]](https://sabahtourism.com/article/the-charms-of-tamu-besar-kota-belud/). A weekly tamu runs other days too if your dates miss the big one.

## Weird food

**Butod (live sago grubs)** — *[markets & tamu; Sago Festival, Kuala Penyu] (offbeat, dare-food)*. Fat sago-palm-weevil larvae, the size of a finger, eaten **alive** — you pinch the head, pop the body in, and the creamy gut "bursts" on the tongue; raw they taste of sweet coconut milk, fried like crispy fish skin [[32]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sago-worm-butod/). A recognised local test of nerve [[33]](https://www.expatgo.com/my/2017/01/19/delicacies-sabah-sarawak/). Around RM40-45/kg (~€9) [[32]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sago-worm-butod/); the annual Sago Festival (Pesta Rumbia) in Kuala Penyu does a sago buffet [[32]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sago-worm-butod/).

**Hinava (raw fish)** — *[KK restaurants, e.g. Kadazan eateries] (offbeat, milder dare)*. The Kadazan-Dusun "ceviche": raw fish cured in lime, ginger, shallots and chilli — bright, sour, an easier adventurous bite than butod [[34]](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2017/01/15/next-time-you-are-in-sabah-try-its-traditional-cuisine/1292893).

## Unusual natural phenomena

**Synchronous fireflies** — *[Klias / Garama rivers, ~2 h south] (touristy tour, real wonder)*. Thousands of fireflies mass on mangrove "display trees" and flash *in unison*, described locally as a "summer Christmas tree" [[35]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/the-fireflies-of-sabah/). Go on a moonless night, 7-10pm, for the full effect [[36]](https://www.sabahcarrentaltour.com/travel/destinations/where-to-see-fireflies-in-sabah-top-spots-and-travel-tips/).

**Rafflesia** — *[Tambunan / Crocker Range, ~1.5-2 h] (offbeat, luck-dependent)*. The world's largest single flower, up to a metre across, that **smells of rotting meat** and blooms only a few days [[37]](https://www.sabahtravel.com/tour/tambunan-rafflesia-reserve-tour). The Tambunan reserve is the spot — but call ahead; a sighting is pure timing.

**Sky Mirror, Dream Beach (Bongawan)** — *[Bongawan, ~1.5 h south] (offbeat)*. At certain tides a flat sheet of beach turns into a mirror that doubles the sky, à la Bolivia's salt flats — usually combined with a firefly cruise [[38]](https://www.world-tourism.org/kota-kinabalu-fireflies-sky-mirror-dream-beach-bongawan/).

**Proboscis monkeys** — *[Klias / Weston wetlands, ~2 h] (touristy cruise)*. Borneo-endemic monkeys nicknamed "Dutchman" (*Monyet Belanda*) for the male's absurd pendulous nose and potbelly — among the strangest-looking primates alive, seen on the same Klias cruises as the fireflies [[39]](https://www.amazingborneo.com/package/klias-wildlife-sunset-river-cruise-proboscis-monkey-fireflies).

## Strange experiences & day-trip oddities

**Tagal fish massage, Kampung Luanti** — *[Ranau, ~2.5 h east] (offbeat, very local)*. Step into a clear river stretch and wild *mahseer* fish swarm to nibble your legs — a "fish spa" in a real river, not a tank [[40]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/tagal-sungai-moroli-kampung-luanti-fish-massage/). It works because of *tagal*, a Kadazan-Dusun communal no-fishing pact that lets the river's fish recover [[41]](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/03/30/the-tagal-way-to-save-nature). Easy to bolt onto a Kundasang day.

**Pulau Tiga — "Survivor Island"** — *[off Kuala Penyu, ~2 h + boat] (offbeat, faded fame)*. The island that hosted the very first US *Survivor* (2000) [[42]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/pulau-tiga-survivor-island/). Its oddity is a **bubbling cold mud volcano** you wallow in, plus neighbouring Snake Island, crawling with banded sea kraits [[43]](https://www.clladventureborneo.com/pulau-tiga-sabah/). ⚠ The mud volcano was temporarily closed in mid-2026 due to drought — check before you commit [[43]](https://www.clladventureborneo.com/pulau-tiga-sabah/).

**Desa Cattle Dairy Farm** — *[Kundasang, ~100 km / 2.5-3 h] (touristy, surreal)*. Holstein cows on misty green pastures below Mount Kinabalu — Sabah's tongue-in-cheek **"Little New Zealand"**, a genuinely jarring Alpine-dairy scene in equatorial Borneo [[44]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/desa-cattle-dairy-farm/). Feed calves, drink fresh milk; RM20 (~€4.30) non-Malaysian, three timed sessions a day [[45]](https://explorekundasang.com/desa-dairy-farm-kundasang-guide/)[[57]](https://www.exchange-rates.org/exchange-rate-history/eur-myr-2026).

**Tuaran Crocodile Farm** — *[Tuaran, ~30 min] (offbeat, B-grade fun)*. Sabah's largest croc farm (est. 1986), home to **over a thousand** crocodiles with feeding shows — pleasingly old-school and uncrowded [[46]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/tuaran-crocodile-farm/)[[47]](https://www.clladventureborneo.com/tuaran-crocodile-farm/). Pairs neatly with the Mengkabong stilt village nearby.

**Kokol Hill "Elf House"** — *[Kokol, ~40 min / 800 m up] (offbeat, photo spot)*. A whimsical Hobbit-style cottage ("Elf House") at Kokol Haven, perched above the city with Mount Kinabalu and coastline views [[48]](https://airial.travel/attractions/malaysia/kota-kinabalu/kokol-hill-viewpoint-uhTs6u3K). The hill is also Sabah's main **tandem-paragliding** launch — semi-adventurous, no permit needed [[49]](https://paraglidinginmalaysia.com/paragliding-kota-kinabalu-kokol-hill).

## Folklore you can stand on

**Jambatan Tamparuli (the curse bridge)** — *[Tamparuli, 30 min — next to the Upside Down House] (offbeat, story-rich)*. Sabah's most famous bridge, over the Tuaran river, carries a tragic legend: the bridge kept collapsing until a Dusun maiden, Solungkoi, cursed it to never stand unless the blood of her British lover Robinson spilled in the river — and the story ends in his suicide [[50]](https://www.amazingborneo.com/travel/the-story-behind-jambatan-tamparuli-a-sabahan-legend-of-love-duty-and-sacrifice). It's so embedded in local memory it became a beloved Kadazan folk song [[51]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/jambatan-tamparuli-the-most-famous-bridge-of-sabah/).

## Festival & custom quirks (if your dates align)

**Kaamatan harvest festival** — *[Penampang / statewide, all May] (touristy at KDCA, deeply local elsewhere)*. The festival date is literally **chosen by a priestess** (*bobohizan*), who also leads the *Magavau* — a moonlit procession into the rice fields to recover the wandering rice spirit [[52]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaamatan). It climaxes 30-31 May with the *Unduk Ngadau* pageant, whose winner embodies Huminodun, the maiden who in myth sacrificed herself so rice could grow [[53]](https://sabahguide.com/kaamatan/)[[54]](https://www.buro247.my/lifestyle/news/pesta-kaamatan-sabah-harvest-festival-facts.html). A first-visit couple in May should not miss it.

**Murut Lansaran & tapai** — *[interior; demonstrated at Mari Mari] (offbeat)*. The Murut's *Lansaran* is a **giant communal trampoline** — a rope-slung platform that bounces dancers 3-4 m into the air, traditionally to snatch a prize hung overhead [[55]](https://sabahguide.com/ethnic-groups/murut/). Their *tapai* rice wine is drunk communally from one jar through long bamboo straws, and refusing a sip is an insult [[56]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sabah-wine-tapai-lihing/). You can see both staged at Mari Mari without going to the deep interior.

---

**How to use this:** the highest-strangeness-per-minute cluster is the **Tamparuli–Tuaran axis** (Upside Down House + curse bridge + crocodile farm + Mengkabong stilt village), all inside ~45 min of KK and easily one self-driven half-day. For the eerie, do Monsopiad + Double Six in the city. For the genuinely weird-nature day, point east to Kundasang and add the Luanti fish massage. Most need a rental car or a hired driver — public transport to these is thin.
