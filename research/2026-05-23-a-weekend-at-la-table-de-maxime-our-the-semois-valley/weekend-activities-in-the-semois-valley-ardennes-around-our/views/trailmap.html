Field Sheet S-55 · Ardennes / Province de Luxembourg

Semois Valley around Our

A late-May weekend, charted: three viewpoints, one river, one fortress town, and a Sunday loop that starts at the village church.

SHEET S-55
EDITION May 2026
SCALE ~1 : 95 000
DEPTH Expedition
CITED 81 sources
RECOMMENDED
ROUTE

Build the weekend around three anchors: Saturday 10:00 kayak Poupehan → Frahan (4 km, 1 h, €25) with Semois Kayaks [19], then the meander arc to the Tombeau du Géant belvedere at Botassart [31]. Saturday afternoon Château de Bouillon — arrive 11:00 for the 11:30 falconry [26]. Sunday 09:00 the 11.5 km Au fil de l'Our signed loop from the village church [1]. Late May puts the river at 15–60 m³/s — ideal for sporty paddlers [22].

Numbered Viewpoints

From map · field-tested in late side-light
1

Tombeau du Géant

Botassart · 20 min drive

The hill the Semois winds around on three sides — classified "exceptional heritage of Wallonia" and one of Belgium's most photographed landscapes [31]. Free parking; the belvedere is minutes from the lot [32].

⚠ Snack bar & toilets July–Aug only · free access 24/7 otherwise.

2

Panorama de Frahan

Rochehaut · 25 min drive

300 m of asphalt road with balustrade and free binoculars [36]. Jewel of Wallonia since 2015. The river-loop hamlet sits 130 m below; reach it on foot via the 9.5 km Frahan loop from Laviot [38].

Best in late side-light · pair with Rochehaut lunch.

3 BELVÉDÈRE
DE BOUILLON

Belvédère & Pic du Diable

Bouillon · on the "12 viewpoints" loop

Twelve-viewpoint Bouillon loop: 14.62 km, +665 m, ~6 h [8]. Chains Belvédère → Pic du Diable → Tombeau du Géant → Rocher du Pendu → Ramonette. Pic du Diable alone is a 18.7 km / 5h23 stand-alone [9].

Ranger-guided walks Apr–Oct · ~60 scheduled in 2026 [16].

5 JAMBON DE LA
SEMOIS

Jambon de la Semois

Membre · Corten-steel platform

Best reached by water — the Alle → Membre family descent ends at this Corten-steel cantilever above the meander, after 3 hours past beaver-dam sites and grey herons [50].

The official MdT Bouillon list omits any "Roche à 7 Heures" — that's a Monthermé Meuse site, not local [10][11].

6 ROCHE AUX
ÉPERVIERS

Roche aux Éperviers

Bouillon · stairs & railings

Fitted belvedere with stairs and safety rail above what tourism docs call "the longest meander created by the Semois," overlooking Botassart and Poupehan woods [12].

Pairs naturally with the 12-viewpoints walk.

Château de Bouillon

€7 adult · 4.4/5 across 1,565 reviews

Belgium's largest preserved medieval fortress. Visitor pattern: secret passages, river panorama and falconry win; complaints are the uphill walk and weather-cancelled bird shows [28].

Buy the City Pass+ €16/€12 if combining castle + Medieval Experience + Musée Ducal + Odyssée de Lumière [27][69]. Musée Ducal alone is the weakest link (3.7/5) [29].

Laforêt

Beaux Villages de Wallonie · 1955

One of the rare southern Ardenne villages to retain its original character — slate roofs and the seasonal Pont de Claies woven from hornbeam branches [41].

Échelles de Rochehaut

Suffer-fest · 4.7 km · 1h30

Metal-ladder scramble up schist cliffs from Poupehan via GR 16 [6]. For the regional benchmark version, chain it with the Crêtes de Frahan ridge — 578 AllTrails reviews [7].

Cycling · from Our's doorstep

VTC / e-MTB for the D+

Loops passing Our

Two signed Points-Noeuds loops from the Bouillon tourism office explicitly pass through Our: Nature et Paysages 3 (29 km, 480 m D+) and Villages d'Ardenne 2 (23 km, 310 m D+) — PN 59 sits right by the village [53].

Province-wide Points-Noeuds mesh: ~2,500 km / 530 numbered nodes / 44 communes / 85% paved [52]. Regional route W7 fully signed Sainte-Ode → Libramont → Bertrix → Paliseul → Bouillon — point-to-point spine from Our [56].

Gravel: six signed Pays de Bouillon loops including Bertrix–Paliseul 35 km [59].

MTB headline · Bike Trail Center Bouillon

42 km enduro · ~1,200 m D+ · linking Cordemois, Moulin de l'Épine, Poupehan, Frahan, Rochehaut, Botassart, Sensenruth · plus five short loops graded blue/red/black like ski slopes + two kids' lines (ages 5–10) [57].

Belgium's first officially-sanctioned trail-building project — sub-50 cm natural-terrain width with berms & jumps from fallen branches [58].
