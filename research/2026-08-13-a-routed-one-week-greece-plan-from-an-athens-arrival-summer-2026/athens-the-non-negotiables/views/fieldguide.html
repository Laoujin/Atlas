§ 01

Standing orders

Compulsory · everything else optional

Acropolis, 08:00

€30 · timed slot · book ~2 weeks out

Booking a slot is mandatory year-round; the ticket is valid from 15 minutes before to 15 minutes after it. Buy on hhticket.gr — the ministry's own platform, no booking fee.[1][37]

Acropolis Museum

€20 · Friday to 22:00

Rock first, museum after — you see where each sculpture stood before you meet it indoors. Friday's late close puts the Parthenon Gallery in sunset light with the floodlit temple through the glass.[5][38]

Ancient Agora

€20 · site + museum

Best value on the ticket sheet after the Acropolis, and the best-preserved Greek temple still standing is in it.[11][37]

⚑ Trap 01 — the ticket that no longer exists

The €30 five-day combined ticket is dead, killed by the April 2025 reform. Those same seven sites now cost roughly €105 bought individually.[1][37]

⚑ Trap 02 — the slot that can be cancelled

The Culture Ministry closes the Acropolis roughly 12:00–17:00 on heatwave days and did it again during the 18–22 July 2026 heatwave. An 11:00 or 13:00 August booking is the one that gets taken from you.[19][18]

€30 covers the rock and its slopes, so the Theatre of Dionysus, the Asklepieion and the walk past the Odeon are already paid for. Last entry 19:30, tapering from 1 September. Closed only 1 Jan, 25 Mar, 1 May, Easter Sunday, 25–26 Dec — 15 August is a normal open day.[10] No shade up there, and the marble is polished slick: water, hat, shoes with grip.[17]

Acropolis Museum

Mandatory · Friday evening is the best version of it
Ticket€20 / €10
On site1.5–2.5 h

Monday closes at 17:00, Friday runs to 22:00.[5] The draws are the five original Caryatids and the Parthenon Gallery, rotated 23° so it aligns with the real building outside the glass.[38] Its second-floor café terrace faces the Acropolis head-on and can be used without a museum ticket — the cheapest good view in the city.[28]

One ticket covers the site and its museum in the Stoa of Attalos; last entry 19:00, half an hour earlier than the other state sites.[11] Shade, trees and the Hephaisteion — the best-preserved Greek temple anywhere — make this the one big site that is bearable later in the morning.[37]

§ 03

Heat & crowd protocol

The day, hour by hour

The Acropolis has a daily cap of 20,000 visitors, down from 23,000, split into hourly quotas — 3,000 in the 08:00 hour, 2,000 in the 09:00 hour, tapering after.[20] Roughly half the site's daily traffic arrives between 08:00 and noon, most of it organised groups and cruise passengers landing from about 10:00.[17]

12:00 → 17:00

⚠ The heat rule has no published threshold

The Culture Ministry decides day by day and announces in the morning. In July 2025 it shut the site until 17:00 during a severe heatwave.[18] During the 18–22 July 2026 heatwave — 40–43 °C — it closed roughly midday to 17:00 again. Warnings come through EMY and the 112 civil-protection alert system, so keep the phone on.[19]

"Skip the line" is half a promise. The timed ticket books your gate, not your place in the mandatory security screening, which still runs 15–30 minutes at peak. The old hack — entering at the south-east Dionysus gate instead of the west Propylaea gate — has stopped working; both queues are now comparable.[33][17]

§ 04

Site register & ticket math

The combined ticket is dead

Greece's April 2025 reform put the Acropolis in a category of its own at €30, abolished seasonal winter pricing and tiered everything else at €20/€15/€10/€5 by visitor volume.[2] It also killed the €30 five-day combo.[1]

Walk past · €20 saved

Temple of Olympian Zeus

All fifteen standing columns are visible from the perimeter fence. Twenty minutes, no shade, and the price doubled from €8 in the reform.[37][14]

Marginal · €10

Hadrian's Library

Thirty minutes inside, and the façade reads perfectly well from the street in Monastiraki. Buy it only if you are already holding a Roman Agora ticket.[15]

Marginal · €12

Panathenaic Stadium

Open 08:00–19:00 March to October, audio guide included. Forty-five minutes, and it is mostly for the marble.[24]

Third-party bundles exist — Acropolis + Museum + National Archaeological around €83, a five-site archaeological combo around €98, an Athens Museum Pass around €79 — but they only pay off if you were already going to visit everything inside them.[1]

☾ The one summer freebie

Friday 28 August 2026 — August Full Moon night. 126 sites and museums open free from about 19:30 to about 23:00, with extra events on 26–27 and 29–30 August.[36]

◷ Free days, and who is always free

State sites are free on 6 March, 18 April, 18 May, the last September weekend and 28 October — no summer dates, and you still queue for a €0 ticket.[35] EU citizens under 25 are always free with ID.[16]

§ 05

2026 amendments to the rock

What changed since the guidebook you bought
New · 15 Jun 2026

West façade, scaffolding-free

The Acropolis Restoration Service completed the Parthenon's west pediment and struck the frame permanently — the first clear west face in about 220 years.[32]

Shut · 3 years+

Odeon of Herodes Atticus

June 2026 was the farewell season; restoration works started in July 2026 and run at least three years. No Herodeon concert this summer — you look down into it from the slope walk and that is it.[8][9]

Shut · since May 2025

Kerameikos

Due to reopen in July 2026 after a €7.5m infrastructure rebuild, but the ephorate's notice still reads "till further announcement". Do not build a day around it.[4][3]

Out of order

Lift & site toilets

The accessibility lift is out of order per a ministry notice dated 13 August 2026, and the toilets inside the site are shut for renovation — use the ones by the central entrance before going up.[10]

New · Mar 2026

Free Digital Tour Guide app

The Ministry of Culture launched its own guide app in March 2026. Worth having instead of a paid audio guide.[15]

If money is no object

€5,000 after-hours

Private visits for up to five people at 07:00 or 20:00 on Tue/Fri/Sat. Eighty were already booked by the start of the 2026 season.[25]

§ 08

Viewpoints

Filopappou for the Parthenon · Lycabettus for Athens
Free · 24/7

Filopappou

Fifteen to twenty minutes' walk from the Acropolis; the head-on Parthenon shot at sunset. Sensible shoes, and don't stay after dark.[29]

Free · at the gate

Areopagus

Closest view of all, right by the west Acropolis gate — but the rock is polished to glass and it is mobbed at sunset.[29]

Funicular €10 return

Lycabettus

Free on foot (~45 min) or €10 return / €7 one-way on the funicular from Kolonaki, every 30 minutes 09:00–02:30. The 360° city-to-Piraeus panorama; the Acropolis is distant from here.[30]

Filopappou is the better Acropolis view; Lycabettus is the better Athens view. Doing both on one trip is not redundant — doing both at sunset is.

§ 09

Do not

Overrated, skippable, or a trap
✕

Temple of Olympian Zeus at €20

Fifteen columns, no shade, twenty minutes — and the whole thing is visible from the perimeter fence for nothing. Walk past it towards Hadrian's Arch instead.[37]

✕

The hop-on hop-off bus

Central sights sit inside a 20-minute walking radius and the metro is €1.20 a ride. The bus only earns its ~€20 for mobility limits, small children, extreme heat or the Piraeus run.[26]

✕

A midday Acropolis slot in July or August

Worst crowds, worst light, worst heat — and the slot most likely to be cancelled outright by a ministry closure.[19][17]

✕

Third-party "skip-the-line" upsells

hhticket.gr charges no booking fee, and no ticket type bypasses the security screening.[37][33]

✕

Plaka's main drag at 14:00

Same lanes, four times the crowd, restaurant touts. The neighbourhood is genuinely lovely at 08:00 and after 19:00.[23]

✕

Planning a Herodeon concert

Gone from July 2026 for at least three years.[9][8]

✕

Building a day around Kerameikos

Still closed at the time of writing, with no reopening date.[4][3]

✕

Second-tier museums on a two-day trip

Byzantine, National Gallery, War Museum, Numismatic — all fine, none worth a morning against the two headliners.[31]
