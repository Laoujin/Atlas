> **Decision.** Two and a half days in Athens — the 08:00 Acropolis slot [[1]](http://odysseus.culture.gr/h/3/eh355.jsp?obj_id=2384), the Acropolis Museum [[2]](https://www.theacropolismuseum.gr/en/plan-your-visit) and the Ancient Agora [[3]](http://odysseus.culture.gr/h/3/eh355.jsp?obj_id=2485) — then **one** base for the remaining four nights, not two. Naxos is the default island [[4]](https://santorinidave.com/naxos); a Nafplio-based Peloponnese loop is the better call if you want ruins and a car [[5]](https://community.ricksteves.com/travel-forum/greece/peloponesse-road-trip-help-4-5-days-4-nights).

**The single constraint that reorders everything is the wind.** The meltemi blows June–September and peaks at 7–8 Beaufort in July–August [[6]](https://www.davestravelpages.com/what-are-the-meltemi-winds-in-greece/), which is simultaneously the reason the Aegean islands sat at 28–30 °C during the July 2026 heatwave while central Greece hit 43.5 °C and the eastern Peloponnese 41–42 °C [[7]](https://blog.wego.com/greece-heatwave/), and the reason high-speed catamarans get cancelled before the big Blue Star hulls do [[8]](https://teleporio.com/blog/meltemi-wind-greece-ferry-guide/). Comfort and connectivity trade against each other directly: the mainland tail never gets cancelled but bakes; the island tail is cool but the boat is a coin-flip. Neither angle can be read without the other.

**Athens is smaller than a week suggests.** The April 2025 ticketing reform put the Acropolis in its own €30 band and killed the combined ticket — buying the seven ex-combo sites separately now runs about €105 [[9]](https://www.artvisitguide.com/archaeological-sites-in-athens-a-travelers-guide-to-ancient-ruins/) — while Kerameikos has been shut since May 2025 [[10]](https://efaathculture.gr/en/urgent-notification-kerameikos/) and the Odeon of Herodes Atticus closed in July 2026 for a multi-year restoration [[11]](https://greekreporter.com/2026/02/22/greece-approves-restoration-odeon-herodes-atticus/). Fewer sites are worth paying for than a first-timer assumes. Two compensations this specific summer: the Parthenon's west scaffolding came down permanently on 15 June 2026 [[12]](https://www.ysma.gr/en/news/parthenon-west-pediment-complete/), and the August Full Moon on Friday 28 August opens 126 sites free until 23:00 [[13]](https://news.gtp.gr/2026/08/07/august-full-moon-2026-126-cultural-sites-across-greece-to-open-with-free-admission/).

**The transfer arithmetic kills two-island plans.** Seven days minus an arrival and a departure day leaves five; each island change consumes most of one, and Cycladic guides want three nights per island minimum [[14]](https://santorinidave.com/cyclades-islands). Only the Saronic escapes this — Piraeus–Hydra is 1h05–2h with 6–8 daily high-speed runs, and Hydra–Poros is 25–40 minutes [[15]](https://www.greeka.com/saronic/hydra/ferries/). That produces an unresolved overlap between two angles: the Saronic is simultaneously the best single day trip from an Athens base [[16]](https://www.athenstravelguides.com/posts/athens-to-hydra-guide/) and the only cluster where a genuine two-island tail works. You cannot do both — pick one.

**The calendar is the live risk, not the budget.** Peak-season guidance is to book 2–3 months ahead, 3–4 for August cabins and vehicles [[17]](https://www.go-ferry.com/trends-and-insights/greek-ferries), and 15 August is Greece's biggest domestic travel holiday, when ports pack out and ferries sell out outright [[18]](https://www.cycladicspaces.com.au/blog/panagia-in-greece-how-to-experience-august-15-like-a-local). Booking now for this month means the Naxos recommendation is only as good as its remaining availability — and the mainland tail, at €80–150 a room against €150–280 on Santorini or Mykonos [[19]](https://greektriplanner.me/blog/how-much-does-a-trip-to-greece-cost), is the fallback that stays bookable late.

So the open question is not *which island* but *how much ferry risk you are willing to hold*: if the answer is none, the car and the Peloponnese win the week by default.
