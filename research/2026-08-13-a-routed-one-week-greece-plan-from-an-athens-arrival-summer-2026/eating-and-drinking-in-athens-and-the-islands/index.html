TL;DR — Athens rewards the cheap end hardest: a €3.50 souvlaki at Kostas[4] beats most €40 dinners. Spend the money once, on a mezedopoleio in Koukaki or Pangrati (€15–25/head)[5], and treat a starred meal as optional — the Athens Michelin selection is mid-refresh, with the 2026 list (now including Santorini and Thessaloniki) not out until the second half of 2026.[2] After Athens, ferry to Sifnos if food is the point.[9]

Street food, and where it actually is

Kostas (Plaka)

Pork souvlaki €3.50, cash only, closes 3–4pm when the meat runs out.[4]

O Thanasis (Monastiraki)

Kebab since 1964 on the Mitropoleos grill row; ~€4, expect a queue.[4]

Elvis (Psyrri)

Late-night skewers — chicken, lamb kebab, pork belly, sausage — still going at 3am.[4]

Kosta & Lefteris o Politis

50 Best's March 2026 Athens picks: "pillowy pita, filled with seared pork or beef"; Lefteris' paprika kebabs since 1951, under €3.[1]

Varvakios Agora (Athinas St, since 1886) is the free anchor for a morning: 100+ butcher stalls, 150 fish stalls, Mon–Sat 07:00–18:00, with cheap cook-house tavernas and the Sokratous St tripe-soup joints inside.[6]

Rooftops: pay for the view once

Good rooftop cocktails run €14–18, €22 where the name carries it — "either worth every cent or a hostage situation with a view".[7] A for Athens frames the Acropolis off Monastiraki Square at €12–15; 360 Cocktail Bar is €10–13 with the same view; GB Roof Garden is €22+ cocktails and €45–75 mains, and buys you three unhurried hours. Railing tables are first-come — be up there by 6pm.[7] For a cocktail bar that isn't a view tax, Baba au Rum is on the World's 50 Best Bars list.[1]

Is a starred night worth a seventh of the trip?

Only if tasting menus are your thing. Delta is Greece's sole two-star[13], inside the SNFCC in Kallithea — a 10–14 course, farm-sourced, hyper-sustainable menu, so it eats the whole evening and the taxi.[14][1] One-stars sit at €95–125 — Soil (Pangrati), CTC's 11-course blind tasting (Kerameikos), The Zillers (Acropolis view), Spondi[3][12] — cheap by international star standards.[3] ⚠ The refreshed Athens list lands only in H2 2026, so August rankings are stale.[2]

Norms

Dinner starts 21:00 or later; round up or add 5–10%, 10–15% upscale, cash preferred.[8] No service charge to hunt for.

Islands

Sifnos is the food island: clay-pot revithada, birthplace of Nikolaos Tselementes, highest chef-per-capita density in Greece.[9] Naxos is produce and dairy — small creamy potatoes, aged graviera, ximomizithra, kopanisti.[10][11] Santorini means fava, domatokeftedes and Assyrtiko — and joins the Michelin map this year.[11][2]

If you only get five meals

  1. Souvlaki standing up at Kostas, before 3pm.[4]
  2. Cook-house lunch inside Varvakios Agora.[6]
  3. Long mezedopoleio dinner in Koukaki (O Lolos), 21:30.[1]
  4. One rooftop sunset — 360 if you're price-sensitive, GB Roof Garden if you want the chair.[7]
  5. Island night: revithada on Sifnos, or a Pangrati one-star if you never leave Athens.[9][3]
