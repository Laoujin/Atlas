The core planning dependency runs in one direction: pick the dinner first, then build the weekend around it.

SF's 10 Michelin-starred restaurants cluster into five neighbourhood bands — SoMa, Hayes Valley, Mission, Cow Hollow, and Jackson Square — and each band generates a different daytime itinerary for the hours before the reservation. [1]

A SoMa dinner sits five blocks from SFMOMA and an easy walk to the Embarcadero. Atelier Crenn in Cow Hollow is walking distance from the Palace of Fine Arts and Crissy Field. Kiln in Hayes Valley is three blocks from Davies Symphony Hall.

The sharpest contradiction the research surfaces: SF's fine-dining scene is globally competitive — Californios is the first Mexican restaurant in the US to earn two Michelin stars [1] — yet the best free activities sit within 20 minutes of every starred restaurant.

Editor's Choice
"The only three-star you can book on four weeks' notice."
Benu is SF's default pinnacle pick. Corey Lee's Korean-Californian tasting menu ($425 + 22% service) has held three stars since 2014 [7] — but crucially, it releases reservations on Tock 30 days out, [2] making it the only route to a three-star table without booking months ahead. Atelier Crenn and Quince fill in that window and require far more lead time.

Logistics
The Booking Sequence
First · Book Now
The Restaurant
Three-star tables at Atelier Crenn and Quince fill months ahead. Benu is the exception — releases 30 days out via Tock. [2]
Same Sprint
Alcatraz Ferry
Tickets release 90 days out via Alcatraz City Cruises (only NPS-authorized operator). Sells out 60–75 days ahead in summer. [19]
After Anchors Set
Hotel & Activities
The neighborhood dictates the daytime itinerary. Plan backward from the reservation — SFMOMA for SoMa, Palace of Fine Arts for Cow Hollow.
Before You Finalize
Check 2026 Guide
Five Bay Area restaurants were added to the 2026 Michelin Guide in March 2026 with no star designations yet — the starred map may shift. [8]
Know Before You Go — Conference Calendar
The conference overlay is a double-edged condition. If your weekend falls during a major conference, hotel rates spike citywide and SoMa-adjacent restaurants see heavier demand. The upside: conference mornings leave Golden Gate Park and museum galleries relatively empty. Conferences are not a reason to avoid SF — but they make early bookings more urgent.
