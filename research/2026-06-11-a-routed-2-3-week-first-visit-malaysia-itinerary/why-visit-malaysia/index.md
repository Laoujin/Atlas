> **Why Malaysia, and why first.** It is the single easiest, most rewarding way into Southeast Asia: one country that hands you the continent's best-rated street food [[1]](https://worldofbuzz.com/penang-crowned-as-best-street-food-city-in-asia/), wild great apes you can watch from a riverboat [[8]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sepilok-orangutan-rehabilitation-centre/), the planet's oldest rainforest [[14]](https://seasia.co/2026/01/19/malaysias-taman-negara-is-older-than-the-amazon), two living UNESCO heritage cities [[18]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1223/) and turquoise reef islands [[15]](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Perhentian_Islands) — all in the most English-fluent country in Asia [[21]](https://seasia.co/2025/11/22/malaysia-secures-top-spot-in-asias-english-proficiency-2025), visa-free for 90 days [[22]](https://worldtraveldocs.com/entry-requirements/malaysia.html), on a strong euro [[23]](https://www.exchange-rates.org/converter/myr-eur). Thailand is more trampled, Vietnam cheaper but single-culture, Singapore sterile-and-pricey; Malaysia is the one that does *everything* at once and makes it effortless [[26]](https://www.indietraveller.co/south-east-asia-where-to-travel/).

Ruthlessly, the five reasons that earn the flight.

## 1. The three-culture food crossroads — the best eating in Asia

Malay, Chinese, Indian and Peranakan kitchens layered onto one street, for €1–3 a plate. **Penang / George Town was crowned Asia's best street-food city, ahead of Bangkok, Singapore, Hanoi and Chiang Mai** [[1]](https://worldofbuzz.com/penang-crowned-as-best-street-food-city-in-asia/); CNN has called it the continent's greatest [[2]](https://edition.cnn.com/travel/article/george-town-penang-greatest-street-food-city/index.html). The named legends do the persuading:

- **Char koay teow** — smoky, charcoal-wok'd at Michelin-listed Siam Road, Penang [[3]](https://guide.michelin.com/my/en/pulau-pinang/my-george-town/restaurant/siam-road-char-koay-teow).
- **Assam laksa** — sour mackerel-tamarind noodles at the foot of Kek Lok Si in Air Itam [[4]](https://smartdory.com/2026/04/air-itam-laksa-penang/).
- **Satay celup** — a communal peanut-sauce hotpot, Malacca's own [[5]](https://www.airial.travel/restaurants/malaysia/melaka/restaurant-ban-lee-siang-kfnMinZF).
- **Sarawak laksa** — Anthony Bourdain's "breakfast of the gods," sold out by lunch at Choon Hui, Kuching [[6]](https://sabaheats.com/choon-hui-coffee-shop-famous-sarawak-laksa/).
- **Jalan Alor** — a whole KL evening of hawker stalls under RM20 (€4) [[7]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g298570-d1943478-Reviews-Jalan_Alor-Kuala_Lumpur_Wilayah_Persekutuan.html).

The differentiator: Thailand and Vietnam each do one national cuisine brilliantly; Malaysia stacks three on the same hawker bench, every day.

## 2. Wild Borneo — great apes, proboscis monkeys, world-class reefs

The headline no neighbour can match. **Borneo (with Sumatra) is the only place on Earth with wild orangutans** — watch them swing in to feed at Sepilok in Sabah [[8]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sepilok-orangutan-rehabilitation-centre/) or Semenggoh near Kuching (RM10, near-guaranteed) [[9]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g19755212-d1503816-Reviews-Semenggoh_Wildlife_Centre-Siburan_Sarawak.html). A dawn cruise on the **Kinabatangan** stacks the odds for pygmy elephants, hornbills and the bulbous-nosed **proboscis monkey** [[10]](https://girlonazebra.com/kinabatangan-river-cruise/), as does a day at **Bako** [[11]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g298309-d10810369-Reviews-Bako_National_Park-Kuching_Sarawak.html). Below the water, **Sipadan** rates among the world's very best dive sites — 3,000+ fish species and the silver tornado of barracuda at Barracuda Point [[12]](https://www.islands.com/1691081/sipadan-island-worlds-best-rated-diving-destinations-underwater-beauty/)[[13]](https://www.bluewaterdivetravel.com/destination/sipadan-diving). Bali has surf and Komodo, but only Malaysian Borneo bundles wild great apes, river safari and a top-five reef in one accessible state.

## 3. Two ancient wildernesses, one country

Peninsular Malaysia and Borneo are effectively two trips under one flag, currency and visa.

- **Taman Negara** is a ~130-million-year-old rainforest — older than the Amazon — crossed by longboat, not road [[14]](https://seasia.co/2026/01/19/malaysias-taman-negara-is-older-than-the-amazon).
- The **Perhentian Islands** are car-free jungle islands ringed by turquoise water, blacktip reef sharks and green turtles you snorkel from the beach [[15]](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Perhentian_Islands)[[16]](https://www.snorkeling-report.com/spot/snorkeling-shark-point-perhentian/).
- **Mount Kinabalu**, Malaysia's natural UNESCO site, looms over Sabah — summit optional, foothills enough [[17]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1012/).

## 4. Living UNESCO heritage cities

**George Town and Malacca were jointly inscribed in 2008** for 500 years of East–West trade still alive in their shophouses, clan houses and temples — not a museum but a working streetscape [[18]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1223/)[[19]](https://whc.unesco.org/en/canopy/georgetown-2/). The modern counterpoint is KL's **Petronas Twin Towers** skyline, an easy timed-entry icon to bookend the heritage [[20]](https://www.petronastwintowers.com.my/plan-your-visit/admission-ticketing/).
