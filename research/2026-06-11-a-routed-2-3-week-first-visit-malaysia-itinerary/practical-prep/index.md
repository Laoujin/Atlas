> **TL;DR** — Bring a **Wise/Revolut card** (link it to **Grab**) plus a cash buffer; ATMs are everywhere and most charge no own fee, but always *decline* on-screen currency conversion [[1]](https://atmfeesaver.com/cash-currency-exchange-atms-in-malaysia/)[[2]](https://www.you.co/sg/blog/malaysia-atm-withdrawal/). A Belgian passport may not yet unlock **TNG eWallet** (the local QR wallet), so don't rely on it [[4]](https://www.touchngo.com.my/consumer/international-tourists). For data, an **eSIM** skips Malaysia's Feb-2026 SIM-registration paperwork; a local **CelcomDigi** prepaid (~€5/40 GB) has the best reach into Borneo and the islands [[7]](https://esimprime.com/blogs/best-local-esim/malaysia-esim-2026-honest-review-of-the-best-plans-providers)[[8]](https://malaysia4u.com/sim-card-guide)[[10]](https://sparkroam.com/malaysia-mobile-network-coverage/). See a travel clinic 4-6 weeks out for **Hep A/typhoid** (routine) and **malaria advice for rural Borneo** [[12]](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/malaysia)[[13]](https://patient.info/travel-and-vaccinations/borneomalaysia). Dress modestly at mosques, eat/give with the right hand [[16]](https://www.roughguides.com/malaysia/customs-etiquette/). Malaysia is generally safe; the only real geographic flag is the **islands/dive sites off east Sabah (Sandakan-Tawau)** — mostly off your route bar Sipadan-area diving [[24]](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia). Realistic mid-range couple budget: **€80-130/day**, ~**€1,300-2,500** for 2-3 weeks excl. flights [[26]](https://southeastasiabackpacker.com/cost-of-travel/malaysia/)[[27]](https://www.sandinmyshoe.com/malaysia/malaysia-travel-budget/).

*EUR conversions throughout use ~RM4.9/€ and ~$1.08/€ (mid-2026 approximation — check the live rate).*

## Money — ringgit, cards, ATMs, Grab, e-wallets

**Cash vs card.** Cards (Visa/Mastercard, contactless) work in malls, hotels, mid/upper restaurants and supermarkets, but **hawker stalls, small kopitiams, rural guesthouses, island boats and most of Borneo's interior are cash-only** — keep RM50-100 on hand [[1]](https://atmfeesaver.com/cash-currency-exchange-atms-in-malaysia/). The ringgit (MYR/RM) is a closed currency; get the bulk of your cash from ATMs in-country rather than buying euros-to-ringgit at home.

**ATMs & fees.** Maybank, CIMB, Public Bank and HongLeong ATMs accept foreign Visa/Mastercard widely; **most Malaysian banks add no own withdrawal fee**, though a few levy ~RM10-12 [[1]](https://atmfeesaver.com/cash-currency-exchange-atms-in-malaysia/)[[2]](https://www.you.co/sg/blog/malaysia-atm-withdrawal/). The real cost trap is **Dynamic Currency Conversion** — when the ATM offers to "charge in EUR," always pick *Decline / charge in MYR* so your own bank does the conversion [[1]](https://atmfeesaver.com/cash-currency-exchange-atms-in-malaysia/)[[2]](https://www.you.co/sg/blog/malaysia-atm-withdrawal/). A **Wise or Revolut** card avoids the ~2-3% foreign-transaction markup most Belgian bank cards charge on overseas spend [[3]](https://wise.com/my/blog/credit-card-overseas-transaction-fee).

**Grab** is the default ride-hailing app nationwide (KL, Penang, KK, Kuching) and sidesteps taxi haggling; link a card and pay cashless — confirm the licence plate matches the app before getting in [[23]](https://www.godigit.com/international-travel-insurance/tourist-scams/tourist-scams-in-malaysia).

**TNG eWallet (Touch 'n Go) — can a Belgian use it?** Partly. TNG's official tourist page now lists ~20 countries/regions (incl. France and Germany); registration needs a passport/ID + selfie verification and a **WhatsApp OTP** to your foreign number, and you top up with an international credit/debit card [[4]](https://www.touchngo.com.my/consumer/international-tourists). The rollout began with the 9 ASEAN states for Visit Malaysia 2026 and is expanding outward [[5]](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/2025/05/10/malaysia-expands-tng-ewallet-access-for-asean-tourists-ahead-of-visit-malaysia-2026/176199)[[6]](https://fintechnews.my/50639/payments-remittance-malaysia/tng-ewallet-international-tourists/) — **Belgium is not confirmed on the eligible list**, so check the app before counting on it. It's a convenience (DuitNow QR at small merchants, parking, some transport), not a necessity: a card + cash + Grab covers everything.

## Connectivity — eSIM vs local prepaid

**Registration shift (2026):** since Feb 2026, **physical prepaid SIMs require your original passport + a biometric photo + a Malaysian address** at point of sale (MCMC rule); an **eSIM bypasses all of this** — buy online, scan a QR, connected on landing [[7]](https://esimprime.com/blogs/best-local-esim/malaysia-esim-2026-honest-review-of-the-best-plans-providers). For a 3-week trip across remote bases, a sensible combo is a **CelcomDigi local prepaid** (best rural reach) for the heavy data, optionally an eSIM as instant-arrival backup.

**Remote-base reality.** All three carriers share the same DNB 5G core, so the differentiator is rural 4G footprint, where **CelcomDigi leads** — recommended for Borneo, Langkawi and the Perhentians [[10]](https://sparkroam.com/malaysia-mobile-network-coverage/). On Borneo's Route 22 (Kota Kinabalu → Sandakan → Sukau on the Kinabatangan), service rarely dropped for more than a minute, **but the Tip of Borneo and stretches of the Kinabatangan river itself are patchy-to-dead** [[11]](https://whatsdavedoing.com/buying-sim-card-malaysia/). Expect **weak/no signal** in deep jungle lodges (Taman Negara interior, parts of the Kinabatangan), thin coverage on the **Perhentians** (mostly resort Wi-Fi), and decent signal in Cameron Highlands towns. Budget ~**€5-12** total for data for two people over three weeks (one or two RM25 plans) [[8]](https://malaysia4u.com/sim-card-guide).

## Health

See a **travel clinic / Belgian travel-health service 4-6 weeks before departure** [[14]](https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/health-alert-millions-of-visitors-to-malaysia-in-2026-must-get-vaccinated-rising-tb-dengue-and-malaria-risks-you-cant-ignore/).

**Vaccines.** Be current on **routine** shots (MMR, Tdap, polio, flu) [[12]](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/malaysia). CDC recommends **Hepatitis A** and **Typhoid** for most travelers (food/water), **Hepatitis B**, and case-by-case **Japanese Encephalitis** (extended time in rural/forested areas) and **Rabies** (remote areas, animal contact) [[12]](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/malaysia). No yellow-fever requirement unless arriving from an endemic country.

**Malaria.** Low on the peninsula — **no transmission in Kuala Lumpur, Penang Island or George Town** [[12]](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/malaysia). The relevant risk is ***P. knowlesi* (monkey malaria) in rural, forested areas, notably interior/rural Borneo** — discuss chemoprophylaxis (atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine or tafenoquine) with your doctor specifically for **Kinabatangan / inland Sabah** stays [[12]](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/malaysia)[[13]](https://patient.info/travel-and-vaccinations/borneomalaysia). Urban Borneo (Kota Kinabalu, Kuching, Sandakan town) is low risk.

**Dengue** is the bigger everyday mosquito threat — nationwide, **day-biting** *Aedes*, with elevated 2026 case counts; there is no practical traveler vaccine (the dengue jab targets prior-infection cases), so **bite-avoidance is the defence** [[14]](https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/health-alert-millions-of-visitors-to-malaysia-in-2026-must-get-vaccinated-rising-tb-dengue-and-malaria-risks-you-cant-ignore/). Use **DEET/picaridin repellent day and night, long sleeves at dusk, and a net/AC in jungle lodges** [[12]](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/destinations/traveler/none/malaysia).

**Water & food.** Tap water is chlorinated but **drink bottled/filtered or boiled water**; hawker food is generally safe if hot and freshly cooked and busy — the usual peel-it/cook-it caution applies [[13]](https://patient.info/travel-and-vaccinations/borneomalaysia).

**Insurance.** Take comprehensive **travel insurance covering medical evacuation** — essential for remote Borneo and **mandatory-grade for any scuba diving** (Sipadan/Perhentians); confirm the policy covers diving depths and chamber/medevac [[15]](https://malaysia.diplomatie.belgium.be/en/travelling-malaysia).

## Etiquette — a Muslim-majority, multicultural country

Malaysia is **Malay-Muslim majority alongside large Chinese and Indian communities** — norms shift by venue and state, so read the room [[16]](https://www.roughguides.com/malaysia/customs-etiquette/).

- **Dress:** smart-casual is fine in cities and resorts; cover shoulders and knees at religious sites and in conservative states (Kelantan, Terengganu) [[16]](https://www.roughguides.com/malaysia/customs-etiquette/).
- **Mosque visits:** non-Muslims welcome **outside prayer times** (avoid Friday midday); **remove shoes**, dress modestly — **women cover arms, legs and hair**, men wear long trousers; robes/headscarves are usually lent at the door [[16]](https://www.roughguides.com/malaysia/customs-etiquette/)[[17]](https://uaktravel.com/how-to-dress-when-visiting-mosques-in-malaysia-mosque-dress).
- **Right hand:** eat, pass items and give/receive with the **right hand** (the left is considered unclean); **remove shoes** entering homes and mosques [[16]](https://www.roughguides.com/malaysia/customs-etiquette/).
- **Ramadan** (note: Ramadan 2026 falls ~Feb-Mar, so likely *before* a mid-2026 trip, but if overlapping): **avoid eating, drinking or smoking in public during daylight** out of respect; dress extra-conservatively [[18]](https://www.trustedmalaysia.com/cultural-etiquette-tips-for-tourist-malaysia/).
- **Public affection** beyond hand-holding is frowned on, especially in conservative areas [[18]](https://www.trustedmalaysia.com/cultural-etiquette-tips-for-tourist-malaysia/).

**Alcohol** is **legal and available** (non-Muslim restaurants, Chinese kopitiams, bars, supermarkets, hotels) but **not sold by Muslim-owned eateries** and is restricted in Kelantan/Terengganu [[16]](https://www.roughguides.com/malaysia/customs-etiquette/). It's **expensive** — heavy excise duty (raised again +10% from Nov 2025 under Budget 2026) means a beer runs **~RM6-13/can in supermarkets and RM15-25 in bars** (~€1.20-5) [[19]](https://calculatormalaysia.com/alcohol/beer-price-malaysia/)[[20]](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2025/10/10/cigarette-and-alcohol-excise-duties-up-from-nov-1-under-budget-2026-as-govt-promotes-healthier-living/194158). Drink in licensed venues, not openly on the street.

## Safety & scams

Malaysia is **generally safe** for tourists; the main risks are **petty theft and bag-snatching** (motorbike snatch-thefts in cities) and scams, not violent crime [[15]](https://malaysia.diplomatie.belgium.be/en/travelling-malaysia)[[22]](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/malaysia-travel-advisory.html).

| Scam | How it works | Defence | Source |
|----------------------|------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|--------|
| Taxi overcharging    | Refuses meter, flat "tourist" rate, long routes| **Use Grab** for a fixed up-front fare  | [[23]][s] |
| Fake airport "Grab"  | Touts pose as your driver at arrivals          | Match licence plate; use official pickup| [[23]][s] |
| ATM skimming         | Card reader + hidden camera on the machine      | Use ATMs **inside bank lobbies**, cover PIN | [[23]][s] |
| Fake guides / tours  | "Budget tour" booked on social media never shows / no real guide | Book through reputable operators | [[23]][s] |

[s]: https://www.godigit.com/international-travel-insurance/tourist-scams/tourist-scams-in-malaysia

**East-Sabah ESSZONE (the one geographic flag).** The Eastern Sabah Security Zone covers the east-coast districts (Kudat, Sandakan, Lahad Datu, Semporna, Tawau) under historic kidnap-for-ransom concern [[21]](https://sabahtourism.com/advisory/). Current advisories:

- **UK FCDO:** advises against **all but essential travel to all islands and dive sites off the coast of eastern Sabah, Sandakan to Tawau** (incl. Lankayan) — **mainland Sabah not affected** [[24]](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia).
- **US State Dept:** **Level 2 (increased caution)** for **islands and maritime areas** off east Sabah; mainland east-Sabah lowered to **Level 1** [[22]](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/malaysia-travel-advisory.html).
- Security has improved markedly — **no kidnap-for-ransom incident since Jan 2020**, several countries relaxed warnings in 2025-26, and Sipadan/Mabul/Kapalai have permanent police-military outposts plus a coastal night curfew [[25]](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/01/26/foreign-minister-more-countries-lift-level-3-travel-warnings-on-eastern-sabah/206818)[[21]](https://sabahtourism.com/advisory/).

**Route relevance:** a Kota Kinabalu / Sandakan / Kinabatangan / Kuching itinerary sits **west and north of the flagged maritime zone** — the only overlap is if you add **Sipadan-area diving (Semporna)**. If you do, go with a licensed dive operator, expect jetty checkpoints, and read the live advisory before booking [[21]](https://sabahtourism.com/advisory/)[[24]](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/malaysia).

## Daily budget — per couple, in EUR

Malaysia is cheap by European standards; **Borneo runs 20-400% pricier than the peninsula** (flights, park fees, lodge transfers), so weight the back half of the trip higher [[26]](https://southeastasiabackpacker.com/cost-of-travel/malaysia/).
