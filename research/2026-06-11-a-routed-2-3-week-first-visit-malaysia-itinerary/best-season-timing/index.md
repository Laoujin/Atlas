> **Verdict — go late March to May, sweet spot April–May.** It is the only stretch where the Perhentians have reopened [[3]](https://www.backpackingbella.com/beach-life-coral-bay-perhentian-islands/) and Borneo has dried out [[20]](https://www.odynovotours.com/malaysia/best-time-to-visit.html) while the Jun–Oct haze season hasn't started [[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_haze) — the west coast pays only with tolerable inter-monsoon afternoon storms [[1]](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca/). **Runner-up: June–early July** — peak island seas plus Kuching's music festival [[19]](https://rwmf.net/), but rising haze risk [[16]](https://www.indoneo.com/earth/southeast-asia-el-nino-haze-2026-forecast/).

## The crux: Malaysia has no single dry season

Two monsoons split the country, so the three legs of this trip never share a "best" month. MetMalaysia's official split: the **Northeast Monsoon (Nov–Mar)** dumps heavy rain on the **east coast peninsula, west Sarawak (Kuching), and east Sabah (Sandakan)**, while the **Southwest Monsoon (late May–Sep)** is drier nationwide; the gaps are **inter-monsoon** transitions (Apr–May, Oct–Nov) with light winds and afternoon thunderstorms [[1]](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca/). The upshot: when one coast is washed out the other is fine [[21]](https://www.selectiveasia.com/malaysia-holidays/weather) [[6]](https://www.enchantingtravels.com/destinations/asia/malaysia/best-time-to-visit-malaysia/) — but a trip hitting *both coasts plus Borneo* has to thread the overlap.

The hard constraint is the islands. The **Perhentians effectively shut Nov–Feb**: ferries and most resorts stop, reopening around March [[3]](https://www.backpackingbella.com/beach-life-coral-bay-perhentian-islands/) [[4]](https://www.airtraveler.club/intel/malaysia-east-coast-monsoon-warning-november-february/) [[5]](https://4stogo.com/blog/posts/monsoon-season-closures-and-accessibility). That single fact removes the entire Nov–Feb half of the year — exactly the half when the west coast is at its dry-season best [[20]](https://www.odynovotours.com/malaysia/best-time-to-visit.html). So the optimal window can never be the west coast's own optimum; it must be the **shoulder where the east is open and Borneo is dry**.

## Year-round constants

- **Heat & humidity.** Lowlands sit at ~23–33°C with humidity 80%+ all year; only the highlands (Cameron) and Kinabatangan nights cool off [[2]](https://www.intrepidtravel.com/us/malaysia/weather-in-malaysia). Plan around **brief, heavy afternoon/equatorial thunderstorms** in any month — they clear fast [[1]](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca/) [[2]](https://www.intrepidtravel.com/us/malaysia/weather-in-malaysia). Even the "dry" Southwest Monsoon still drops ~100–150mm/month on the peninsula [[1]](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/fenomena-cuaca/).
- **Haze (Jun–Oct).** Indonesian peat/forest fires send transboundary smoke across Malaysia and Borneo in the dry season, worst in strong El Niño years (1997, 2015, 2019, 2023) [[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_haze). **2026 is flagged as the worst risk since 2015**: El Niño ~80% likely by July plus a positive Indian Ocean Dipole in Jul–Aug, with haze peaking **Aug–Sep** and echoing 1997 [[16]](https://www.indoneo.com/earth/southeast-asia-el-nino-haze-2026-forecast/). Early-2026 signals already showed: a large Pengerang (Johor) peat fire in late January [[18]](https://batamnewsasia.com/2026/01/28/haze-threat-in-southern-malaysia-johor-peat-fire-forces-evacuations-impacts-singapore-air-quality/) and a regional haze alarm by late March [[17]](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/03/31/fire-hot-spots-and-scant-rain-raise-haze-alarm-in-south-east-asia). This is the strongest argument for finishing before August.

## Festivals — 2026 dates

- **Ramadan / Hari Raya Aidilfitri** (movable): Ramadan begins ~20 Feb; **Hari Raya ~20–22 Mar 2026**, with a 4-day long weekend and a nationwide *balik kampung* travel surge — packed transport and some closures around those dates [[14]](https://www.eskimo.travel/en/blog/malaysia-public-holidays-2026). A trip starting **early April lands just after the Raya rush** — cleaner than mid-March.
- **Chinese New Year** 17–18 Feb 2026, **Thaipusam** 2 Feb (Penang's Batu Caves-style processions), **Deepavali** 8 Nov [[13]](https://www.timeanddate.com/holidays/malaysia/2026). All three fall in the monsoon/haze months anyway — seek them only on a different trip.
- **[Rainforest World Music Festival](https://rwmf.net/)** — **26–28 June 2026**, Sarawak Cultural Village, Kuching [[19]](https://rwmf.net/). The one festival that aligns with good Borneo weather; it's the reason to stretch the window into late June.

## Decision

**Best: late March–May (core April–May).** Compromises: Perhentian seas aren't yet flat-calm in early April (shoulder), the west coast gets inter-monsoon afternoon storms, and Borneo wildlife is just ramping toward its May–Aug peak. In exchange you dodge both island closures *and* the haze. Aim post-Hari Raya (after ~22 Mar) to skip the travel crush [[14]](https://www.eskimo.travel/en/blog/malaysia-public-holidays-2026).

**Runner-up: June–early July.** Peak Perhentian conditions [[3]](https://www.backpackingbella.com/beach-life-coral-bay-perhentian-islands/), Borneo wildlife at its best [[8]](https://www.amazingborneo.com/sabah/tours/kinabatangan-river-sukau-bilit), and RWMF [[19]](https://rwmf.net/). Compromises: the 2026 haze risk is building by then [[16]](https://www.indoneo.com/earth/southeast-asia-el-nino-haze-2026-forecast/), plus peak-season island crowds and prices [[3]](https://www.backpackingbella.com/beach-life-coral-bay-perhentian-islands/).

**Routing note for the itinerary leg:** within the window any order works, but if dates slip toward June, **front-load Borneo (Sabah → Sarawak) before haze builds and save the Perhentians for their June calm** [[16]](https://www.indoneo.com/earth/southeast-asia-el-nino-haze-2026-forecast/) [[3]](https://www.backpackingbella.com/beach-life-coral-bay-perhentian-islands/). **Avoid outright: Nov–Feb** (islands shut, Kuching and Sandakan at their wettest) [[4]](https://www.airtraveler.club/intel/malaysia-east-coast-monsoon-warning-november-february/) [[10]](https://www.climatestotravel.com/climate/malaysia/kuching) [[22]](https://www.intrepidtravel.com/en/malaysia/best-time-to-visit-malaysia), and **Aug–Sep** (haze peak in an El Niño year) [[16]](https://www.indoneo.com/earth/southeast-asia-el-nino-haze-2026-forecast/).
