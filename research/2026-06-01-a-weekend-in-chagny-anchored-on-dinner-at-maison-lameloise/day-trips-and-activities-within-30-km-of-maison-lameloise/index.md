> **Decision.** Chagny is the geographic pivot of Burgundy — Côte de Beaune to the north, Côte Chalonnaise to the south — so almost every "best of" Burgundy day-trip target fits inside 30 km. If you have one full day, give it to **Beaune** (16 km / 32 min)[[77]](https://www.viamichelin.com/web/Routes/Route-Chagny-71150-Saone_et_Loire-France-to-Beaune-21200-Cote_d_Or-France): Hospices in the morning, négociant cellar in the afternoon, food market on Saturday. If you have a half-day, drive the **Voie des Vignes** from Santenay through Pommard and Meursault — 23 km of vineyard road and bike path between the climats[[37]](https://en.francevelotourisme.com/cycle-route/le-tour-de-bourgogne-by-bike/beaune-santenay). Sunday-quiet day: **Chagny's own Sunday market**[[76]](https://www.valleedelagastronomie.com/en/be-inspired-gourmet-experience/chagny-sunday-market), then a Beaune cellar that's still open (Patriarche, Bouchard Aîné, Marché aux Vins or Jaffelin)[[75]](https://community.ricksteves.com/travel-forum/france/burgundy-on-a-sunday). And before any of this — be back, showered, and hungry by 19:45 for an 8 pm Lameloise dinner; tasting menus run roughly five hours[[73]](https://www.thienlan.me/2014/12/04/la-lameloise-3-star-restaurant-chagny-burgundy-france/).

Three famous targets are **beyond 30 km** and don't belong on a Chagny day-trip list: Château du Clos de Vougeot (open daily, guided tour €15)[[30]](https://www.closdevougeot.fr/) sits in the Côte de Nuits ~55-60 km north-east[[31]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clos_de_Vougeot), Abbaye de Cîteaux is ~45-50 km away[[32]](https://citeaux-abbaye.org/venez-nous-vous-accueillons/visiter-l-abbaye), and Château de Cormatin sits ~41 km south-south-west into the Mâconnais[[81]](https://www.francethisway.com/places/a/chagny-saone-et-loire.php). ## 1. Beaune (16 km — the obvious anchor)

### Hospices de Beaune (Hôtel-Dieu)

The Rogier van der Weyden *Last Judgement* altarpiece and the glazed polychrome roof. Daily 9:00–19:30 continuously from April through mid-November (off-season 9:00–12:30 + 14:00–18:30), last admission 1 h before close[[11]](https://booking-hoteldieu.hospices-de-beaune.com/ticketing-individuals.html). **Open every Sunday, every day of the year**[[74]](https://booking-hoteldieu.hospices-de-beaune.com/faq.html). Self-guided audio tour €9.50 adult / €6.25 reduced / free under 6[[12]](https://sg.trip.com/travel-guide/attraction/beaune/htel-dieu-museum-hospices-de-beaune-89743/).

### The other Beaune stops

[Moutarderie Edmond Fallot](https://www.beaune-tourism.com/to-see-and-do/cultural-heritage/la-moutarderie-fallot-parcours-decouvertes-beaune-en-3860767/) — Parcours Découvertes guided trail, daily 10:00 / 11:30 / 15:00 / 16:30, €12 adult / €9 child[[22]](https://www.beaune-tourism.com/to-see-and-do/cultural-heritage/la-moutarderie-fallot-parcours-decouvertes-beaune-en-3860767/). 5-min walk south of the ramparts. [Collégiale Notre-Dame](https://www.beaune-tourism.com/to-see-and-do/cultural-heritage/collegiale-notre-dame-beaune-en-3860937/) — free, daily 8:00–18:00; the 15th-c. Tournai *Life of the Virgin* tapestries are unveiled April–October for €3 guided access[[36]](https://www.beaune-tourism.com/to-see-and-do/cultural-heritage/collegiale-notre-dame-beaune-en-3860937/). The **Saturday food market** sprawls 7:00–13:00 across Place de la Halle, Place Fleury and avenue de la République with about 180 stalls — produce, cheese, charcuterie, organic, plus a March-November flea market on Place Carnot[[23]](https://www.beaune-tourism.com/organize-your-stay/events/marche-de-beaune-beaune-en-3860373/).

Givry's flagship event is the early-April **Marché aux Vins** (30th edition ran 5–7 April), a one-weekend pour-around with the village's producers[[70]](https://www.givry-bourgogne.fr/30eme-edition-du-marche-aux-vins-5-6-7-avril/).

### Chalon-sur-Saône (~20 km / 25 min)

A real city, not a wine-tourist set piece, and the only place inside the radius for a proper old town and a flagship museum. Cathédrale Saint-Vincent on its eponymous square dates 1090–1520, with 15th-c. half-timbered houses around it and a 19th-c. neo-gothic west façade[[58]](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cath%C3%A9drale_Saint-Vincent_de_Chalon-sur-Sa%C3%B4ne). The **[Musée Nicéphore Niépce](https://www.museeniepce.com/)** on Quai des Messageries is *entrée libre* — 3 million images and 10,000+ cameras, including Niépce's original *Chambre de la Découverte*, the first photographic camera[[52]](https://www.museeniepce.com/)[[53]](https://www.bourgogne-tourisme.com/musees/musee-nicephore-niepce-3). Niépce made the world's first surviving photograph (*View from the Window at Le Gras*, ~1826) 5 km away at Saint-Loup-de-Varennes[[54]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/View_from_the_Window_at_Le_Gras); France is staging a year-long 2026 bicentenary anchored there[[55]](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/photography/france-set-to-stage-a-year-long-celebration-to-mark-200-years-of-photography-in-2026). Museum hours: Sep–Jun 9:30–11:45 + 14:00–17:45, Jul–Aug 10:00–13:00 + 14:00–18:00, closed Tuesdays[[52]](https://www.museeniepce.com/).

The **Sunday morning market** sprawls from Place Saint-Vincent to Place de Beaune — Bresse poultry, Charolais beef, snails, cheeses, charcuterie — and the crowds can be so thick it's hard to move[[56]](https://www.achalon.com/en/homepage-tourism/discover-grand-chalon/burgundian-gastronomy/immerse-yourself-in-the-hustle-and-bustle-of-the-markets/)[[57]](https://eggandbacon.co.uk/chalon-sur-saone/). There's also a Friday morning food market and an organic market on Wednesdays[[56]](https://www.achalon.com/en/homepage-tourism/discover-grand-chalon/burgundian-gastronomy/immerse-yourself-in-the-hustle-and-bustle-of-the-markets/). Outside town, **Parc Saint-Nicolas** has 115 ha with a 3 km rose-history trail and 600+ rose varieties[[59]](https://www.destination-saone-et-loire.fr/fr/patrimoine-culturel/parc-de-loisirs-saint-nicolas.html).

## 4. Châteaux and historic monuments

The honest count of working, visit-worthy castles inside the radius is shorter than the brochures suggest.

**[Château de Rully](https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/sit/chateau-de-rully)** (5 km) — privately owned medieval château, the family runs guided interior tours of the ground and first floors paired with a tasting of three Rully wines and gougères in either the medieval kitchen or a 15th-c. cellar; €10 adult, free under 12, cash only[[27]](https://www.chateauderully.com/prices.html)[[28]](https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/sit/chateau-de-rully). The closest castle worth your time.

**[Château de Couches](https://www.chateaudecouches.com/fr_FR/)** (~15 km west) — an 11th-c. fortress with the *Marguerite de Bourgogne* legend; audio-guided or self-guided visits at a flat €8, plus a tasting cellar and gourmet restaurant on site[[26]](https://www.chateaudecouches.com/informations/tarifs-et-horaires/)[[69]](https://www.chateaudecouches.com/fr_FR/). 2026 themed events: Journées du Patrimoine (19–20 Sep), Halloween (21 Oct – 1 Nov), Noël au Château (19–30 Dec)[[26]](https://www.chateaudecouches.com/informations/tarifs-et-horaires/).

**[Château de Sully](https://www.chateauxenbourgognedusud.com/en/sully-en/)** (~25 km south-west) — "Fontainebleau of Burgundy", birthplace of *Maréchal de MacMahon* (not Vauban, despite frequent online confusion). Open daily 4 Apr – 1 Nov 2026, 9:30–17:00; guided château + park €10.20 adult / €4.50 child / €29 family[[33]](https://www.chateauxenbourgognedusud.com/en/sully-en/).

**[Château de Pommard](https://www.chateaudepommard.com/experiences)** (~20 km) — see the wine table above; the visit *is* the tasting, with the courtyard and cellars part of the experience[[5]](https://www.chateaudepommard.com/experiences).

⚠ **Château de La Rochepot — closed in 2026.** It sits ~17 km / 17 min beyond Beaune via the Hautes-Côtes[[78]](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Beaune/La-Rochepot), but the Cour d'appel de Metz formally confiscated the property from former owner Dmitri Malinovsky on 12 March 2026 and AGRASC is running emergency restoration ahead of resale; the local Nolay tourist office confirms it can only be photographed from outside[[24]](https://www.francebleu.fr/bourgogne-franche-comte/cote-d-or-21/la-rochepot/chateau-de-la-rochepot-en-cote-d-or-la-confiscation-officiellement-prononcee-par-la-cour-d-appel-de-metz-7914100)[[25]](https://www.nolay.com/en/activites/la-rochepot-castle/). Don't drive out specifically for it. The polychrome-roof shot from the road below is still free.

In **Chagny** itself the [Tour Bajole](https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/sit/la-tour-bajole) — a 12th-c. building that was dependent on the priory of Saint-Georges — *is the Maison Lameloise building*, visible only from outside[[34]](https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/sit/la-tour-bajole). A guided "Discovering Chagny" tour by the tourist office runs Tuesdays 15:00 June–September, covering the 12th–13th-c. Saint-Martin church and Romanesque bell tower plus the 1715 apothecary[[35]](https://www.beaune-tourisme.fr/sejourner/agenda/a-la-decouverte-de-chagny-chagny-fr-4118175/).

## 5. Outdoor — cycling, ballooning, walking, water

### Cycling: the rare two-corridor town

Chagny sits at the junction of two flagship routes. The **Voie des Vignes** runs 23 km Beaune → Pommard → Meursault → Chassagne → Santenay, easy difficulty, 149 m total ascent[[37]](https://en.francevelotourisme.com/cycle-route/le-tour-de-bourgogne-by-bike/beaune-santenay). At Santenay it merges into the Canal du Centre towpath of **EuroVelo 6**, giving a flat 24 km to Chalon-sur-Saône, with Chagny's canal port sitting roughly halfway[[38]](https://en.francevelotourisme.com/cycle-route/entre-rhin-et-loire-a-velo-eurovelo-6/chalon-sur-saone-santenay).

### Canal du Centre boating

From Saint-Léger-sur-Dheune (~15 km west), [CrisBoat](https://www.crisboat.co.uk/our-destinations-in-france/burgundy/the-canal-du-centre/saint-leger/) offers no-licence self-drive cruisers including a 3-night round trip to Chagny (26 km of canal)[[42]](https://www.crisboat.co.uk/our-destinations-in-france/burgundy/the-canal-du-centre/saint-leger/). ### Walking the Climats

The **Chemin des Grands Crus** (GR de Pays) runs 87 km Dijon → Beaune → Santenay through the 1,247 parcels that make up the UNESCO Climats, signposted yellow-and-red; it splits cleanly into 3–5 day-stage sections served by trains and Transco buses[[47]](https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/on-foot-by-bike-on-horseback-or-by-boat/hiking-some-of-the-great-trails/the-grands-crus-hiking-trail/)[[49]](https://www.gr-infos.com/en/grp-grands-crus.htm). Beaune Tourism markets the southern segment through Pommard, Meursault, Chassagne-Montrachet and Santenay as the headline vineyard walk — all inside the 30 km radius[[48]](https://www.beaune-tourism.com/explore/nature-hiking/the-chemin-des-grands-crus/).

The single best short walk in the radius is **Mont de Sène / Montagne des Trois Croix** above Santenay: 10.7 km loop, 358 m of ascent, departing from place du Jet d'Eau (223 m) in the village centre and climbing through vineyards and a falaise section above the Bois de la Fée to a 521 m summit with three 18th-century stone crosses and two orientation tables looking over the Côte de Beaune, the Saône valley toward the Jura, the Clunisois and the Morvan[[80]](https://www.altituderando.com/Mont-de-Sene-ou-Montagne-des-Trois-Croix-521m-par-Santenay). Moderate half-day, not a one-hour stroll; pairs naturally with a Santenay tasting stop.

## 7. Saturday timing for an 8 pm dinner at Lameloise

Lameloise serves lunch 12:00–13:00 and dinner 19:30–21:00, Thursday through Monday; closed Tuesday and Wednesday[[71]](https://www.beaune-tourism.com/organize-your-stay/enjoy-and-savour/restaurants/maison-lameloise-chagny-en-3860889/)[[72]](https://restaurantguru.com/Maison-Lameloise-Chagny). A tasting menu runs roughly five hours — one diner reported their meal lasting 20:00 → 01:00[[73]](https://www.thienlan.me/2014/12/04/la-lameloise-3-star-restaurant-chagny-burgundy-france/). Plan backwards from a marathon, not from a meal.

- **Before 14:00:** active sightseeing — Beaune Hospices + market, or a vineyard walk, or a balloon flight (book the morning slot, ~1.5 h flight + aperitif).
- **14:00–16:00:** light lunch *not* a big sit-down. Standard advice for a long Michelin tasting is to keep calories low all day — many people skip lunch and stick to coffee and a small snack[[79]](https://www.traverse-blog.com/beginner-guide-michelin-star-restaurants/).
- **16:30:** back in Chagny.
- **17:00–19:00:** shower, nap, dress.
- **19:45:** walk to 36 Place d'Armes. The Tour Bajole *is* the building[[34]](https://www.burgundy-tourism.com/sit/la-tour-bajole).

Both patterns leave Lameloise dinner as the keystone and treat Sunday morning as a slow start, in line with how Burgundy actually opens on Sundays.
