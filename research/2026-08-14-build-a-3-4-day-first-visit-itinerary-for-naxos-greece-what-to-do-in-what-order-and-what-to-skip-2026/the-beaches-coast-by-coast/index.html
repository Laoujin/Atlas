Decision. A 3–4 day visit is fully served by the west-coast strip between Chora and Alyko: Agios Prokopios / Agia Anna for the organised day with tavernas and a 15-minute bus[3], Plaka for space[5], and one car day for Mikri Vigla → Kastraki → Alyko, which is the whole southwest in a single loop[27].

Wind rule that decides your morning. The west coast is sheltered from the northerly Etesian/meltemi; the south-facing coast takes it head-on, and at Mikri Vigla "by 2pm, the water is choppy, the sand is blowing"[1]. Swim early, sit down to lunch, don't fight it.

Skip on a short trip. The east coast (Moutsouna, Psili Ammos, Azalas) and Panermos/Kalantos: 37–54 km each way over a narrow winding mountain road with no sunbeds and often no food[25][19]. Rina Cave is worth it only as a boat trip, never as a drive[20].

How the coastline reads

One geometric fact drives every beach decision on Naxos. "The west coast runs south from Naxos town in an almost unbroken line of fine golden sand, sheltered from the Etesian winds that blow hard from the north during summer. The south coast faces the open sea and takes the full force of those winds."[1]

In practice the shelter degrades as you go south. Agios Georgios, Agios Prokopios and Agia Anna sit tucked behind the Stelida headland and stay calm[2]. By Plaka the coast is open enough to be listed as "windy in meltemi season"[2]. By Mikri Vigla the wind is the product being sold[8]. South of the Alyko peninsula the coast turns east and gets quiet again — Agiassos "is protected by strong winds whereas the shallow waters are safe for children"[18].

Wind sports: where and with whom

Everything concentrates on the Mikri Vigla / Orkos stretch, 12–17 km south of Chora[2][11]. The headland splits the bay into two beaches 250 m apart: Parthena on the north side is the sports bay, Limanaki on the south is the swimming one[6]. Wind is side-onshore and "the wind will bring you back to the beach" if things go wrong, which is why the spot is beginner-tolerant despite the strength[11]. Water is flat to choppy; the venturi between Naxos and Paros plus afternoon thermals mean you should "bring small kite sizes"[12].

Season: kite-usable wind is quoted as June–September at its most reliable[12], with schools claiming "more than 200 windy days per year"[8]. Mid-August is peak meltemi — good for riders, poor for anyone hoping to read a book on Parthena[1].

Car, ATV or bus

Bus covers the money strip. Line 1 (Town→Plaka) runs 07:30 to 01:00 with a 10–35 minute ride and €1.80–2.60 fares, hitting Agios Prokopios, Agia Anna, Plaka and Mikri Vigla[27]; roughly every 30 minutes in summer[28], and it's the island's busiest route[29]. Buy the ticket before boarding — drivers don't sell them[28].

Line 3 (Town→Pyrgaki) reaches Kastraki, Glyfada, Alyko and Pyrgaki but only five times a day over 15–55 minutes[27] — treated as unreliable for Alyko and Pyrgaki in practice[28]. That is the line between "bus day" and "car day".

2WD is enough. Naxos roads are well maintained[31] and Alyko is reached on "a paved road that's easy to follow" ending in a dirt/sand car park[15]. Even Psili Ammos on the east coast is "asphalt road all the way", just narrow and winding after Apiranthos[24]. ⚠ The real constraint isn't the vehicle, it's the contract: "Most rental contracts strictly forbid driving on dirt paths, rocky tracks, or dunes, and doing so can void your insurance instantly"[30]. That rules out the Azalas approach — 15 minutes of dirt from Atsachas[26] — unless you have it in writing. An ATV buys you narrow tracks and easy parking but no legal dirt access and a real accident rate[31]. Cars run about €25–40/day in shoulder season[30].

What to pair on one day

Bus day — Agios Prokopios + Agia Anna + Plaka

All three sit on Line 1[27] and are connected by a continuous shoreline: it's a 20–30 minute walk between Agios Prokopios and Agia Anna or Plaka[3]. Swim at Prokopios, lunch in Agia Anna, walk south into Plaka's emptier half for the afternoon. On a gusty day, hold at the north-western public stretch of Prokopios, which is the sheltered one[3].

Car day — Mikri Vigla + Kastraki + Alyko (+ Pyrgaki)

A single 18 km run down one paved road[15]. Morning swim at Limanaki/Sahara before the thermal builds[7], walk the headland to watch Parthena when it does[6], then Alyko for the juniper forest across the Kouroupia peninsula[17] and the unfinished 1960s hotel whose concrete shells are now covered in murals[16]. Hawaii Beach is a 5–10 minute walk from the main cove[15]. Bring food and shade — there are no sunbeds and one food truck[15]. Kastraki on the way back for sunset behind Paros[4].

Boat day (optional 4th) — Rina Cave

Rina is in the Kalandos area of the far south and is reachable only by boat[20]. Options: a 2-hour traditional boat from Panermos[21] (~€20 pp reported[19], but Panermos itself is a 54 km drive[19]), a full-day sail from Naxos port combining Rina with the Small Cyclades[22], or a guided sea-kayak day trip[23]. On 3–4 days, take the boat that leaves from Chora — driving to Panermos to catch a 2-hour boat burns the whole day twice over.

Wasted trips on a 3–4 day visit

The east coast (Moutsouna, Azalas, Psili Ammos). 36–44 km each way via Chalki–Filoti–Apiranthos on a road that is "narrow winding after Apiranthos"[25][24], with no sunbeds anywhere[25], Azalas now mostly pebble behind a dirt road[26], and Psili Ammos ending in a 150 m walk-in[24]. It costs a full day to reach beaches that are emptier but not better than Alyko. Worth it only if you were already driving the Apiranthos mountain route for the villages.

Panermos and Kalantos as a drive. 54 km from Chora with a single monopoly cafeteria[19]. Reach that coastline by boat instead[22].

Agiassos. Genuinely calm, shallow and child-safe[18], but at 23 km with no bus and no facilities[18] it duplicates what Pyrgaki (19 km, with tavernas[4]) already gives you 4 km earlier[18].

Agios Georgios as a beach day. Walkable from Chora and the best shallow water for toddlers[4], but it's the most crowded stretch on the island, packed with day-trippers[1]. Use it for a pre-dinner swim, not a day.
