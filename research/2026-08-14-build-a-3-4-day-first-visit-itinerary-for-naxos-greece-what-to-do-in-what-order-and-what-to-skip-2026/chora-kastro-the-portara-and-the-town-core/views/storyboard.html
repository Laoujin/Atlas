Director's note

Give Chora one full day and two evenings, entirely on foot — the old-town streets are too narrow to drive and parking is nonexistent [4]. Shoot it in order: Old Market lanes before 09:00 [20], Kastro mid-morning [4], a swim at Agios Georgios after lunch [21], the waterfront after 18:00, and the Portara for the last hour of light [4]. Two scenes were rewritten for 2026 — see the revision band below.

★ Revised pages · 2026 shooting script Two museum scenes have changed — read before you plan a morning

Archaeological Museum

Scene moved — free temporary exhibition only

The building is closed inside the "Islet of Museums in the Castle of Chora" renovation. Since 15 May 2021, and for the duration of the works, a Temporary Exhibition runs on the second floor of the Saint Ursula Cultural Center in the former Ursulines monastery [8]. The permanent home — a five-storey 17th-century Venetian mansion, the old School of Commerce [25] where Kazantzakis studied [26] — is worth a look from outside even shut.

09:00 – 14:00 Closed Tuesday Free entrance Inside the Kastro

Mitropolis Site Museum

Scene cut — do not build a morning around it

Greece's first on-site museum of its type, created 1999, nominally €5 — but the ministry's own listing now carries the notice "The museum will be closed from 01.08.2025" [9], and Greeka lists it as permanently closed [10]. The Glezos Tower is slated to become the largest Byzantine museum in the Cyclades — restoration is ongoing and it is not open [5].

Closed from 01.08.2025 Listed permanently closed No reopening date

Bourgos · the Old Market

Ext. narrow lanes — early morning

The Greek quarter spreading around and below the castle walls; the name is the Italian borgo [18]. A maze of lanes, shops and tavernas — marble underfoot, arches overhead, staircases into courtyards into more staircases [20].

Light & crowdsShops open around 10:00, so before 09:00 you get the lanes to yourself; weekday mornings are the calm window and late afternoon the prettiest light [19]. Wear real shoes, not sandals [20].
Free≈1 hShops skew pricey [19]

Grotta + Zoodochos Pigi

Ext. north shore — two setups, one stop

Part of the Mycenaean-era capital, around 1300 BC — agora and settlement, with some structures now submerged in the sea, viewed from above on the north side of the harbour below Mitropoleos Square [17]. The Orthodox Metropolis church, Zoodochos Pigi (1787), stands right beside it [16].

Director's callFifteen minutes and a photo, not an excursion — foundations seen from a railing. Do not budget a "site visit" here.
Free15 minOpen-air
↳ Back south, then uphill 5–10 min climb from the waterfront [5]

Enter the Kastro · Trani Porta

Ext. castle gate — mid-morning

Marco Sanudo founded it in 1207 as the seat of his Duchy of the Archipelago: pentagonal, built partly from recycled ancient acropolis marble and blocks from the Portara temple itself [5]. Of three original gates two survive — Trani Porta (the "Great Gate", north-west) and Paraporti (south) [6]; the north-western entrance still carries its original wooden gate [26].

Access noteThis is a lived-in neighbourhood, not a monument site, and entry is free [5]. If the climb is a problem there is a free lift up to the Avaton 1739 café [5].
Free1.5–2 h for the whole quarterAlways open
↳ Inside the walls the museum shuts at 14:00 — take it first [8]

The Kastro loop · Glezos (Crispi) Tower

Ext./Int. castle quarter — tracking shot

The Glezos Tower, also called the Crispi Tower, is the only survivor of the twelve that once guarded the perimeter — and the only circular one [5]. Walk the quarter the way the municipality lays it out rather than wandering: Trani Porta → Della Rocca Barozzi tower → Tower of Crispi → Catholic Cathedral → Panagia Theoskepasti → Capuchin monastery → Archaeological Museum → Capella Kazatza → Ursuline school [7].

Light & crowdsThe lanes are narrow enough to stay shaded through the middle of the morning — this is the frame to shoot when the open-air ones would be blown out.
FreeLiving neighbourhoodTower still under restoration [5]

Venetian Museum (Della Rocca-Barozzi)

Int. family mansion — guided, 40 min

The single museum in Chora that reliably delivers in 2026. A family mansion beside the castle's north gate [25], shown by a staff member — furniture, clothing and heirlooms [11]. Around forty minutes, bilingual English/Greek, about €4 [12].

Two time slotsDaily 10:00–15:00 and 19:00–22:00 [11]. Take the morning slot — the evening one collides with dinner, unless you are coming back for the Domus Festival (see the night unit).

Lunch break · the covered lanes

Ext. Bourgos — midday

The dead hour of the day, and the one the old town is best at: arches, marble, staircases into courtyards into more staircases [20]. Eat here rather than on the waterfront, then get out of the sun.

Light & crowdsEverything open-air is at its worst now. Everything covered — the market lanes, the castle alleys — is at its best. Plan the beach for after this, not during it.
≈1 hPeak heat
↳ South along the waterfront road 1 km, flat, 15 min [21]

Agios Georgios · the town beach

Ext. shallow bay — afternoon

About 1 km from the port and the town centre, a flat 15-minute walk along the waterfront road, Blue Flag, shallow and calm [21] — five minutes south of the port by the shortest line [20]. Sand is free; sunbeds are paid [21].

Director's callDo not give this a whole day — save that for Plaka or Agia Anna and use this one as the pre-dinner swim. It is 25–30 minutes on foot from here to the Portara [4], which makes beach → shower → sunset one clean sequence.
Free sand2 h+Blue Flag
↳ Back north to town, shower, out again promenade after 18:00 [4]

Waterfront promenade

Ext. harbour front — golden hour approach

The seafront strip is also the town's utility belt — bus station, banks, luggage storage and car-rental offices all sit along it [18], which is exactly why basing in Chora removes the need for a vehicle.

Light & crowdsAfter 18:00 the promenade is the correct place to be [4]: the heat drops, the ferries come in, and you are already pointed at the causeway.
FreeWalk it slowly
↳ Onto the causeway ~5 min from town [27]

The causeway to the Portara

Ext. Palatia islet — approach

Four marble blocks of roughly 20 tons each, all that was ever built of a temple of Apollo begun around 530 BC by the tyrant Lygdamis; the islet of Palatia is joined to town by a paved footpath [1]. Ten minutes total from the harbour front — five across the causeway, five up the hill [3]. Open-air, no gate, 24/7, free [2].

WardrobeShoes with grip. When the sea is choppy, waves break near the path and it turns slippery — heels and flip-flops are a bad idea [3].
FreeOpen 24/7Slippery causeway
↳ You are already there hold position for the light

Sunset through the Portara

Ext. Palatia — magic hour, wrap

The frame the whole day is built around. It is also the most crowded thing in Chora: it gets very busy around sunset in summer, and dawn is the answer if you want it quiet [3].

Wrap noteThere is no light on the road, so the walk back after dark needs a phone torch [27].

Night unit

after wrap · two evenings

Evening one · bar-hopping the seafront

Nightlife is concentrated in Chora along the seafront and the old-town lanes, and bar-hopping is entirely walkable [24]. Named options in town: 520 in a 1700s building with an Aegean view [23] — a rooftop terrace in the old market looking over the port and the Kastro [20]; The Naxos Apothecary near the port facing the Portara; Oinochoros for small-winery Greek bottles; On The Rocks on the Chora beachfront; Prime if you want the loud end [23].

All on footSeafront + old town

Evening two · Domus Festival

Classical, jazz, choral, Byzantine and traditional music in the Venetian Museum's courtyard or basement, April through October [13][14]. Book in advance at the museum (22850 22387); the ticket includes a glass of local wine or kitron [13]. The museum's own 19:00–22:00 evening opening [11] doubles as the way in.

April – OctoberBook aheadWine or kitron included

Survey

Naxos beaches, coast by coast

The west-coast strip south of Chora carries a 3–4 day trip; the east coast and the remote south are day-eaters you should skip unless a boat or a specific obsession justifies them.

Expedition

The Naxos interior

Two driving loops and one dawn climb cover the whole interior: the Tragaea in a day, Zas plus Apeiranthos in a second, the emery north as an optional third.

Survey

Eating and drinking Naxian

Naxos is the one Cycladic island that feeds itself — two PDO cheeses, a PGI potato, a PGI citron liqueur — and the eating is inland, not on the beach strip.

Recon

Where to base yourself

For a 3–4 night first visit, base in Chora — port, buses, dinner and a beach are all on foot; the beach strip wins only for a pure swim-and-sleep trip.

Recon

Getting there, and whether you need a car

Take the fast ferry from Piraeus, rent a car for 2 of your 3–4 days, and let the bus cover the beach days.

Recon

Day trips off Naxos

On a 3–4 day first visit, no off-island ferry day trip beats Naxos itself; the only day-boat worth booking never asks you to change islands.
