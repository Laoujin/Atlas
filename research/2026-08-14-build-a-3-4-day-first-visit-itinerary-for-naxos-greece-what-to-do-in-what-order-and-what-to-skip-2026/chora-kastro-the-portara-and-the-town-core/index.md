> **Decision.** Give Chora one full day and two evenings, entirely on foot — no car, no scooter, since the old-town streets are too narrow to drive and parking is nonexistent [[4]](https://explorenaxosparos.com/naxos-town-chora/). Run it in this order: Old Market lanes before 09:00 [[20]](https://www.thecommonwanderer.com/blog/naxos-chora-guide), Kastro mid-morning [[4]](https://explorenaxosparos.com/naxos-town-chora/), swim at Agios Georgios after lunch (1 km, flat, 15 min from the port) [[21]](https://naxaki.com/agios-georgios-beach-naxos/), waterfront promenade after 18:00, Portara for the last hour of light [[4]](https://explorenaxosparos.com/naxos-town-chora/). Two 2026 catches: the Archaeological Museum is closed for renovation and showing a free temporary exhibition in the Ursulines building [[8]](https://archaeologicalmuseums.gr/en/museum/5df34af3deca5e2d79e8c19a), and the Mitropolis site museum is closed [[9]](https://archaeologicalmuseums.gr/en/museum/5df34af3deca5e2d79e8c1a2)[[10]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/naxos-mitropolis-museum/).

Whole-town budget: allow at least half a day, and the town rewards slow exploration rather than a tick-list [[4]](https://explorenaxosparos.com/naxos-town-chora/); a guided Kastro-plus-Portara walk is quoted at about 2.5 hours [[5]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/castle-chora-naxos/), which is a fair floor for doing it yourself.

## Kastro: what you're actually looking at

Marco Sanudo founded it in 1207 as the seat of his Duchy of the Archipelago, pentagonal, built partly from recycled ancient acropolis marble and blocks from the Portara temple [[5]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/castle-chora-naxos/). Three gates originally; two survive — **Trani Porta** ("Great Gate", north-west) and **Paraporti** (south) [[6]](https://www.naxos.gr/kastro-choras/?lang=en), and the north-western entrance still carries its original wooden gate [[26]](https://cycladen.be/Chora%20NaxouText.htm).

Walk it as the municipality lays it out: in via Trani Porta → Della Rocca Barozzi tower (right) → Tower of Crispi → Catholic Cathedral → Panagia Theoskepasti → Capuchin monastery → Archaeological Museum → Capella Kazatza → Ursuline school [[7]](https://www.naxos.gr/a-tour-of-the-kastro-castle-district-in-the-old-town/?lang=en). The **Glezos (Crispi) Tower** is the only survivor of the twelve that once guarded the perimeter, and the only circular one [[5]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/castle-chora-naxos/).

⚠ Kastro is a lived-in neighbourhood, not a monument site, and entry is free [[5]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/castle-chora-naxos/). If the climb is a problem there's a free lift up to the Avaton 1739 café [[5]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/castle-chora-naxos/).

## The 2026 museum situation

- **Archaeological Museum** — the building is closed inside the "Islet of Museums in the Castle of Chora" renovation. Since 15 May 2021 and for the duration of the works, a *Temporary Exhibition* runs on the second floor of the Saint Ursula Cultural Center in the former Ursulines monastery: 09:00–14:00, closed Tuesday, **free entrance** [[8]](https://archaeologicalmuseums.gr/en/museum/5df34af3deca5e2d79e8c19a). The permanent home is a five-storey 17th-century Venetian mansion, the old School of Commerce [[25]](https://www.naxos.net/museums/) where Kazantzakis studied [[26]](https://cycladen.be/Chora%20NaxouText.htm) — worth a look from outside even shut.
- **Mitropolis Site Museum** (Metropolis Square) — Greece's first on-site museum of its type, created 1999, nominally €5, but the ministry's own listing carries the notice "The museum will be closed from 01.08.2025" [[9]](https://archaeologicalmuseums.gr/en/museum/5df34af3deca5e2d79e8c1a2) and Greeka lists it as permanently closed [[10]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/naxos-mitropolis-museum/). Don't build a morning around it.
- **Glezos Tower** — restoration ongoing; it is *slated* to house the largest Byzantine museum in the Cyclades, not open yet [[5]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/castle-chora-naxos/).
- **Venetian Museum (Della Rocca-Barozzi)** — the one museum that reliably delivers in 2026. Family mansion at the Kastro's north gate [[25]](https://www.naxos.net/museums/), staff-led walk-through of furniture, clothing and heirlooms [[11]](https://www.naxos.gr/venetian-museum/?lang=en), ~40 minutes, bilingual EN/GR [[12]](https://www.triphobo.com/places/naxos-greece/venetian-museum).

## Portara and sunset

Four marble blocks of roughly 20 tons each, all that was built of a temple of Apollo begun around 530 BC by the tyrant Lygdamis; the islet of Palatia is joined to town by a paved footpath [[1]](https://www.naxos.gr/the-temple-of-apollo-portara/?lang=en). Ten minutes total from the harbour front: five across the causeway, five up the hill [[3]](https://explorenaxosparos.com/portara-naxos-temple-of-apollo/).

August 2026 sunset times [[22]](https://www.generalblue.com/sunrise-sunset-in-naxos-greece): 14 Aug 20:12, 20 Aug 20:04, 31 Aug 19:49. Be on the rock ~45 min before.

⚠ It gets very crowded around sunset in summer; go at dawn if you want it quiet [[3]](https://explorenaxosparos.com/portara-naxos-temple-of-apollo/). Wear shoes with grip — the causeway turns slippery when the sea is choppy, and heels/flip-flops are a bad idea [[3]](https://explorenaxosparos.com/portara-naxos-temple-of-apollo/). There is no light on the road, so the walk back after dark needs a phone torch [[27]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/portara-naxos/).

## Bourgos, Grotta, the beach

**Bourgos** is the Greek quarter spreading around and below the castle walls — the name is Italian *borgo* [[18]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/villages/town/). Its Old Market is a maze of narrow lanes, shops and tavernas; shops skew pricey, and weekday mornings are the calm window [[19]](https://www.airial.travel/attractions/greece/naxos/bourgos-old-market-quarter-mJMc48kv). Marble underfoot, arches overhead, staircases into courtyards into more staircases — wear real shoes [[20]](https://www.thecommonwanderer.com/blog/naxos-chora-guide).

**Grotta** sits on the north side of the harbour below Mitropoleos Square: part of the Mycenaean-era capital, ~1300 BC, agora and settlement, with some structures now submerged in the sea [[17]](https://www.naxos.gr/archaeological-site-of-grotta/?lang=en). Fifteen minutes and a photo, not an excursion. The Orthodox Metropolis church, Zoodochos Pigi (1787), stands right beside it [[16]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/sightseeing/naxos-orthodox-cathedral/).

**Agios Georgios** is the town beach: 1 km from the port, a flat 15-minute walk along the waterfront road, Blue Flag, shallow and calm [[21]](https://naxaki.com/agios-georgios-beach-naxos/) — five minutes south of the port by the shortest line [[20]](https://www.thecommonwanderer.com/blog/naxos-chora-guide). Morning secures position before peak heat; late afternoon gets the light [[21]](https://naxaki.com/agios-georgios-beach-naxos/). It's 25–30 minutes on foot from there to the Portara [[4]](https://explorenaxosparos.com/naxos-town-chora/), which makes beach → shower → sunset a clean sequence.

## Evenings

Nightlife is concentrated in Chora along the seafront and the old-town lanes, and bar-hopping is entirely walkable [[24]](https://travel-greece.org/nightlife-naxos-island-2026). Named options in town: **520** in a 1700s building with an Aegean view [[23]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/eat-drink/bars/), a rooftop terrace in the old market looking over the port and the Kastro [[20]](https://www.thecommonwanderer.com/blog/naxos-chora-guide); **The Naxos Apothecary** near the port facing the Portara; **Oinochoros** for small-winery Greek bottles; **On The Rocks** on the Chora beachfront; **Prime** if you want the loud end [[23]](https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/naxos/eat-drink/bars/).

For one cultured evening, the **Domus Festival** runs in the Venetian Museum's courtyard/basement from April to October — classical, jazz, choral, Byzantine and traditional music [[13]](https://roughguides.com/destinations/europe/greece/the-cyclades/naxos/naxos-music-festival)[[14]](https://www.naxos.gr/domus-festival-at-the-venetian-museum/?lang=en). Book in advance at the museum (22850 22387); the ticket includes a glass of local wine or kitron [[13]](https://roughguides.com/destinations/europe/greece/the-cyclades/naxos/naxos-music-festival). The museum's own evening opening, 19:00–22:00 [[11]](https://www.naxos.gr/venetian-museum/?lang=en), doubles as the way in.
