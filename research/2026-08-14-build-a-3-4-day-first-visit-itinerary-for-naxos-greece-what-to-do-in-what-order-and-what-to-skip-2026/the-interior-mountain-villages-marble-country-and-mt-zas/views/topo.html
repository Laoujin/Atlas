Sheet 03 Naxos · Cyclades · Aegean Interior sheet · 430 km² Contour interval 100 m Edition 2026

Marble country, plotted by altitude

Everything worth driving to on Naxos sits between sea level and 1,003 m, and altitude is the only axis that orders it honestly. Two closed driving loops and one dawn climb cover the whole sheet. Here it is surveyed — every place at its true height, every monument pinned with its hours and its price, and the two calendar traps drawn rather than footnoted.

§ 01

Survey notes

The decision, before the detail
Loop A

Tragaea · ~65 km · half a day of driving

Chora → Sangri/Temple of Demeter → Halki → Moni → Filoti. Highest payoff per kilometre on the island: a re-erected Archaic Ionic temple in marble, 08:30–15:30 daily except Tuesday, €5 [2], plus a 6th-century church with pre-iconoclastic frescoes [54] inside an olive plain holding 200+ Byzantine churches [6]. All asphalt, 25–35 min per leg from Chora [1].

Dawn

Zas from Agia Marina · ~6 km return · 3–4 h

~450 m of gain to a 1,003 m summit, the highest point in the Cyclades [3] [4]. Not the 12.8 km / 5 h 20 Filoti circuit [30] unless you have a spare morning and cool weather. Pair it with Apeiranthos afterwards.

Loop B

Emery north · ~110 km · a full day

Apeiranthos → Koronos → Apollonas → back down the west coast. Only if you have a fourth day. It buys the ropeway remains [8], stepped Koronos [13] and the 10.7 m kouros [48].

Cut

The Apollonas kouros as an errand, and Cheimarros Tower

2.5 h round trip for a 10-minute stop [49]; and a 14 km dead-end drive plus a walk for a tower you can only look at from outside [52].

§ 02

The altitude profile

Every named place, at its true height

Naxos has an interior because Mt Zas traps moisture the other Cyclades never see [5] [4]. Plot the villages by altitude and the sheet reads as one continuous climb: emery ports at the waterline, the olive plain at 280 m, the quarries at 400 m, the emery villages between 500 and 600 m, and the marble village just under 700 m.

Horizontal axis is rank, not distance. Altitudes: Halki 280 m [10] · Kinidaros 400 m [7] · Photodotis 500 m [55] · Koronos 540 m [13] · Skado 580 m [14] · Zas Cave 630 m [4] · Apeiranthos 650–700 m [12] · summit 1,003 m [4].

Datum note. The summit trig point reads 1,001 m on the ground [32], the municipality publishes 1,003 m [4] and some operators 1,004 m [42]. That is survey-datum noise, not a second peak.

Mt Zas · highest point in the Cyclades

The dawn climb, and nothing else that day before it

Measured on the ground, Filoti → summit is 4.34 km with about 3 h 25 of walking, of which village-to-chapel is 40 min and chapel-to-summit 1 h 09 [32]. From the Agia Marina chapel it is ~6 km return with ~450 m of gain in 3–4 h [3] — reviewers report about an hour up [33]. The top is a plain trig point; the confusion is below it, where cairns are the only marker and an unmarked gap in a wall misleads people [33].

Apeiranthos · the marble village · pop. 773

Four museums in a village of under 800 people

28 km north-east of Chora, Cretan-descended in dialect and tradition, first documented by Buondelmonti in 1420 [12]. Two of the four museums carry the story: the Archaeological Collection (Early Bronze Age marble figurines and the incised Aroni plaques, 08:30–15:30, closed Tuesday, €5) [27] and the Geological Museum, which explains the marble and emery you have been driving past, free, May–September 11:00–16:00 [28]. The folk-art and natural-history collections are the two to drop if you are time-boxed.

Zas Cave · Filoti flank

A real excavation, not a myth stop

Chambers up to 11 m with stalactite formations [4]. Explored in 1962, systematically excavated 1985–86 and 1994, occupied from the Late Neolithic to Early Cycladic III, with the finds now in the Naxos Archaeological Museum [34]. The absence of crop-processing debris points to seasonal herding rather than settlement, and a nearby rock cutting reads Zeus Melosios — Zeus as protector of sheep [34]. Inside it runs roughly 15 °C cooler than outside; bring a torch [3].

Skado · 38 km from Chora · ~120 people

A 15-minute stop, not a destination

Amphitheatric on the slopes of Mt Koronos, 2.5 km north of Koronos [14]. Its inhabitants still hold shares in the emery mines they worked [15] — which is the whole reason to slow down here rather than park.

Koronos · 28 km from Chora

The most atmospheric village on the island, and the least visited of the big four

Seven neighbourhoods stitched together by whitewashed steps and covered archways around the central Platsa square, with an eco-museum, a folklore museum and three car parks [13]. Emery made it and emery unmade it: ~3,000 residents before WWII, then the mines closed under occupation and 414 villagers died of hunger [13].

Kinidaros · the working quarries

Marble is still being cut, 2,500 years on

Naxian marble was quarried before 550 BC and exported to Olympia and the Athenian Acropolis [5]. Six quarries operate near Kinidaros, 15 km from Chora at 400 m; the largest, Bolimbas, is visible straight from the road, and the ancient Flerio workings sit 7 km away [7]. This is the cheapest 20 minutes on the sheet — it is a roadside stop, not a site.

Halki (Chalkio) · former capital · anchor of Loop A

Three things inside five minutes' walk

16 km / 30 min from Chora, with free parking throughout and the Venetian tower lot the easiest [10].

Vallindras distillery — workshop from 1862, citron liqueur from 1896, first exported 1928, fifth generation still running it; kitron is one of only three Greek spirits with PDO status [22] [23]. Three grades: green 30%, white 33%, yellow 36% ABV, the white being the local pour [24]. Budget ten minutes — reviewers consistently describe a small room with minimal signage [25].

Fish & Olive — Katharina Bolesch and Alexander Reichardt's ceramics and jewellery gallery on the square; the 2026 exhibition Cycladic Whispers runs 10 May to mid-October, marking their 25th year on Naxos [26]. Apr–Oct 10:00–14:00 and 18:00–21:00 [10].

Panagia Protothroni — 7th century or earlier, Apr–Oct 08:00–20:00, free; Agios Georgios Diasoritis 500 m away is 11th century but opens only 1 July – 31 August, 11:00–13:30 [10] [35].

Moutsouna & Apollonas · the two industrial ports

Where the mountain sent its cargo

Moutsouna was the emery terminus. The aerial ropeway was built 1926–29, ran 8,720 m from Stavrolagada on 72 pylons and moved ~340,000 tonnes between 1929 and 1982; the pylons, wagons and loading stations still stand as a free open-air industrial monument, signposted off the Koronos–Lionas road [8]. Fish tavernas on the waterfront [16]. ⚠ Getting there means 650 m of descent from Apeiranthos on a road described as one of the steepest in Greece [9].

Apollonas was the ancient marble export port, 36 km from Chora, with a sandy beach, tavernas, parking and the 10.7 m kouros just outside the village entrance [17] [49].

§ 04

The loops, traced

Closed circuits · asphalt unless flagged

Two loops are the two-day core. Everything else on the sheet is a spur off one of them, and the spurs are where trips get eaten.

Loop A

Tragaea — the highest payoff per kilometre on the island

~65 km1.5–2 h net drivingAll asphalt

Buys you: the Temple of Demeter [2], Bazeos Tower [19], the kitron distillery [10] and five Byzantine churches on Trail 4 [35]. Legs are 25–30 min Chora–Halki and 30–35 min Chora–Filoti [1].

Loop B

Zas at dawn, then Apeiranthos

~75 km2 h net drivingLast 2 km single-track

The chapel lot is small and fills [33], which is a second argument for the early start alongside the heat [58]. Apeiranthos is 45–50 min from Chora [1].

Loop C

The emery north — a fourth day, or nothing

~110 km4–5 h net drivingAsphalt throughout

The ropeway installations at Stavrolagada, Pezoulia and Pigi are signposted from the Koronos–Lionas road and cost nothing [8]. Apollonas is 1 h 15 from Chora on the fast route [1] [49].

Spur D · Moutsouna

~22 km return · 1 h + lunch

Asphalt but "one of the steepest in Greece": 650 m down to sea level in tight hairpins [9] [16]. Fish tavernas and the ropeway's sea terminus at the bottom.

Spur E · Melanes / Flerio

~35 km · 1 h

Slots into a spare 90 minutes on your way in or out of Chora — it does not need its own day. Two colossal kouroi, a quarry sanctuary and the modern quarries [44] [47].

Spur F · Cheimarros

28 km return · 1.5 h + walk

Dead-end provincial road, 14 km south of Filoti, for a 4th-century BC marble tower still ~15 m tall [52] [53]. Great if you want an empty half-day; poor use of a three-day trip.

Road

Road reality, 2026

Village-to-village asphalt is well maintained, but many secondary roads are dirt or rock [9]. The last stretch to the Aria Spring trailhead is narrow, without guardrails and with almost nowhere to turn around [31]. Rental contracts routinely exclude damage on unpaved surfaces — which is exactly the Cheimarros and Photodotis approaches [9] [55]. Do not drive the mountains after dark: no street lighting, livestock on the road, and signs sometimes only in Greek [9]. Parking is free and plentiful in the villages [10] [13].

Loop A wants both Gyroulas and Bazeos in the same day — and there is no day of the week on which one of them is not shut. On a Monday you get the temple and lose the tower; Tuesday loses the temple, Yria and the Apeiranthos collection at once. The Geological Museum publishes no weekly closure day [28]; Drosiani in practice depends on the caretaker being there [6].

Clash 2 · the state collections shut at 15:30 [2], August runs ~30 °C with 35–36 °C spells [58], and the Ministry of Culture's 2026 heat measure shut Knossos 13:00–17:00 at 43 °C [57] and the Acropolis noon–17:00 [59]. Since Gyroulas and Yria close at 15:30 anyway, arriving before 11:00 means you never meet the problem. Start the Zas climb by 07:15–07:30 — reviewers report heat already building by 08:30 [33].

Sangri · Loop A

Temple of Demeter, Gyroulas

The interior's must-see

08:30–15:30Closed Tue€5 incl. museum

Built c. 530 BC in Naxian marble, an almost square 13.29 × 12.73 m, one of the earliest Ionic temples anywhere [50]. Dismantled for a 6th-century basilica, partially re-erected in 1994 from roughly 1,600 surviving fragments, site museum opened August 2001 [50]. Card payment not reliably available [2].

14 km S of Filoti

Cheimarros Tower

Late 4th century BC · unstaffed

UnfencedFreeSkip on 3 days

Originally ~20 m tall on a 9.2 m base with four internal storeys linked by marble stairs, inside a 35 m square enclosure [52]. § 09

The kouroi, to scale

Three colossal statues abandoned where they broke

Three unfinished Archaic figures, abandoned in or beside the quarries that produced them. They are the single most distinctive thing in the Naxian interior, and they are free. Solid bars are the lower published figure; the hatched extension is the higher one, where sources disagree.
