Decision

Naxos is the only Cycladic island with real agricultural self-sufficiency — two PDO cheeses, a PGI potato and a PGI liqueur[2][3][8] — so the best eating is inland, not on the Agios Prokopios–Plaka beach strip. Rent the car and drive to Axiotissa (Kastraki) and one mountain taverna in Apeiranthos or Kinidaros[23]. Book exactly two things ahead: Axiotissa (3–4 days out in high season, mandatory)[4] and one producer visit — the Promponas kitron distillery tour (€65, 90 min)[15] or the Perivoli farm cooking class (€100, 4h15)[16]. Carry home arseniko and graviera, not kitron — the cheese is the thing you can't buy elsewhere. If your dates touch 15 August, the Filoti panigiri is the single best food night on the island[5].

**On the cooperative.** Founded 1926, EAS Naxos today spans 26 member co-ops and ~3,274 farmers and breeders, processes ~12,000 t of milk a year (>70% of the island's production), and cheeses milk within 8 hours of collection; the Glinado dairy dates to 1985[10]. It also holds the Patata Naxou PGI alongside Graviera Naxou PDO[11]. Skip trying to tour the plant; buy from the seafront shop instead, or go to a farm dairy: **Kalavros** near Agiassos makes PDO arseniko, **Pittaras** in Melanes makes aged cheeses[28]. **Honey.** Nature & Art is based in Halki and keeps hives at Moni and Kinidaros, producing thyme, heather and flower honey[20]. Naxia Gi, also Halki, covers honey, bee products, spices and herbs[21]. Halki is the practical one-stop for taking product home: distillery, honey, herbs and the galaktoboureko in one square. ## When to eat what - **Potatoes.** Spring crop is sown mid-Feb to early March and harvested late May to the first ten days of July[9]; a second planting goes in Aug–Sep[8]. June is peak. A Potato Festival runs at Agia Anna, usually July or August[9]. - **Xinomizithra.** Eat within days of production — it's a spring/early-summer goat-milk cheese by nature[1]. - **Zamponi.** Cured over winter after the north-wind slaughter, so summer bought stock is the previous season's[22]. - **Panigiria.** July through September, plus the odd one in October; the Panagia festival in August runs nine days[29]. ### 2026 panigiria calendar[5]

What's on the table at a panigiri: lamb on the spit, baked fish, fava, local cheeses (kefalotyri and arseniko) and Naxos wine in carafes; a €5–10 per-person church collection typically covers it[5]. For 15 August in Filoti: arrive after 23:00, the core runs midnight to 04:00, and sleep in Chora, Apollonas or Halki — Filoti rooms fill a year ahead. KTEL runs special buses; taxis must be pre-booked[5]. ## Booking rules of thumb Most Naxos restaurants don't take reservations at all, and for most you don't need one[24]. The exceptions are small rooms, sunset terraces and the destination tavernas: **Axiotissa 3–4 days out, mandatory in high season**[4]; Doukato essentially every night[26]; Tortuga, Nissaki, Rotonda and Barozzi a few days ahead[24]. For everything else, walk past in the afternoon and book for the next night. Accommodation is the real constraint in July–August: latest sensible booking is two weeks out, with 1–3 months recommended[33]. ⚠ On a 3–4 day trip, one mountain dinner + one producer visit + one beach lunch is the realistic ceiling. Don't try to do Apeiranthos, Halki, Melanes and Moutsouna in the same trip.
