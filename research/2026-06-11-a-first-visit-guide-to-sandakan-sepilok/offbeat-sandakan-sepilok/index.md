> **TL;DR** — Sandakan's offbeat thread is its *layered ghosts*: a bombed-flat "Little Hong Kong" port [[1]](https://sabahtourism.com/article/sandakan-little-hong-kong-of-north-borneo/)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan), a leper-island POW escape [[7]](https://kajomag.com/the-berhala-eight-the-daring-escape-from-berhala-island-during-wwii/), a colonial croquet lawn [[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Tea_House_and_Restaurant), and tarts the locals call "cow dung" [[39]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/ufo-tart-of-sandakan/). Base **in town** for the heritage + food curiosities (walkable Heritage Trail), **day-trip out** for the natural oddities (bat exodus, palm-estate monkeys, turtle hatchery, fireflies). Best window **April–June** — least rain; avoid the **Nov–Dec NE monsoon** when river and sea crossings get unreliable [[45]](https://www.climatestotravel.com/climate/malaysia/sandakan)[[46]](https://sabahguide.com/places/districts/sandakan/best-time-to-visit/).

Everything below leans offbeat by design, so each find is tagged by **where** (town / day-trip) and **when**. Nothing here is vertical, permit-expedition, or extreme — it's the weird-but-accessible layer.

## When to go (and the heat/monsoon trap)

Sandakan has no real dry season — it logs ~3,057 mm of rain a year [[45]](https://www.climatestotravel.com/climate/malaysia/sandakan). The rains *ease* April–June (your best bet), with March–May and July–August the clearest stretches for outdoor plans [[46]](https://sabahguide.com/places/districts/sandakan/best-time-to-visit/). The **northeast monsoon (Oct–Feb)** dumps >300 mm/month, peaking Nov–Dec — that's when boat trips to Berhala, the Turtle Islands and the firefly rivers are most likely to be cancelled or rough [[45]](https://www.climatestotravel.com/climate/malaysia/sandakan). Heat is constant; the genuinely cool window is dawn, which doubles as the best wildlife and fish-market hour.

## History oddities — the backstory most visitors miss

| Find | The quirk | Where / when |
|---|---|---|
| **"Little Hong Kong"** | British-encouraged Cantonese & Hakka migration built a dense shophouse port so HK-like it took the nickname — then Allied bombing flattened it in 1944–45, erasing the very thing the name described [[1]](https://sabahtourism.com/article/sandakan-little-hong-kong-of-north-borneo/)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan). Still a live 2026 tourism narrative [[5]](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/03/11/sandakan-bahyou-know-the-story-or-not). | Town / anytime |
| **The town that was twice named** | William Pryer founded the settlement on **21 June 1879** and called it *Elopura* — "beautiful city" — but locals kept the old name "Sandakan" (Suluk for "the place that was pawned") and it stuck [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Burgess_Pryer)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan). | Town / anytime |
| **Kampong German** | The *first* European settlement here was renamed Kampong German for its German trading bases — then burnt down by accident on 15 June 1879, a week before Pryer's Elopura was founded [[4]](https://mps.sabah.gov.my/sandakan.htm). | Town (Buli Sim Sim area) |

## War-history relics — rust, bullet holes & a dynamited shell

- **The Berhala Eight.** Pre-war Pulau Berhala was a quarantine station for Chinese and Filipino labourers *and* a leper colony [[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berhala_Island,_Sabah). On **4 June 1943**, eight POWs stole a boat *from the leper colony* and paddled/sailed ~250 km of open sea to Tawi-Tawi — an escape that, by luck, spared them the Sandakan Death Marches [[7]](https://kajomag.com/the-berhala-eight-the-daring-escape-from-berhala-island-during-wwii/). The island is now being developed as a recreational-forest day-trip, ~8 km from the jetty [[8]](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2025/04/16/sandakan039s-pulau-berhala-to-be-developed-as-sustainable-tourism-site). *(Day-trip island; calmest seas Mar–Oct.)*
- **[Sandakan Memorial Park](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-memorial-park/).** Built on the actual POW-camp ground where ~2,400 Australian and British prisoners died [[10]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-memorial-park/)[[9]](https://anzacportal.dva.gov.au/wars-and-missions/ww2/pows/asia/borneo). The eerie detail: a **rusting excavator, generator and boiler still sit in their original positions** in the garden [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan_Memorial_Park). From here >1,000 POWs were force-marched 260 km to Ranau in 1945; about half died on the way [[12]](https://www.dva.gov.au/recognition/commemorations/interpretive-centres-and-education/sandakan-memorial-park-malaysia). Free entry, 9–5. *(Day-trip, ~11 km out / quiet midday.)*
- **Bullet-holed mosque.** Sandakan's oldest mosque, **Masjid Jamek** (opened 1890, founded by an Indian cloth merchant named Damsah), still has **WWII bullet holes in its timber pillars** [[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan_Jamek_Mosque)[[13]](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/sandakan-39-s-masjid-jamek-hidden-heritage-under-threat-icomos/OgWBE7tGuD76Jw?hl=en). It once overlooked the sea, but land reclamation shoved the shoreline ~350 m away — leaving a "waterfront" mosque now stranded inland, and flagged as heritage under threat [[13]](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/sandakan-39-s-masjid-jamek-hidden-heritage-under-threat-icomos/OgWBE7tGuD76Jw?hl=en). *(Town / Heritage Trail start.)*
- **The dynamited stone church.** [St Michael's & All Angels](https://stmicsdk.org/about-us/our-history/) is Sabah's oldest stone church (foundation stone 1893; 30+ years to build) [[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael's_and_All_Angels_Church,_Sandakan)[[16]](https://stmicsdk.org/about-us/our-history/). Its white window stone was **imported from Hong Kong**; the Japanese dynamited it before surrendering in 1945, yet the granite shell stood through the blast [[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael's_and_All_Angels_Church,_Sandakan). *(Town / Heritage Trail.)*

## Colonial & architectural curiosities

- **A croquet lawn in Borneo.** The [English Tea House](https://www.englishteahouse.org/sandakan_index.html) keeps **1.5 acres of manicured lawn with an actual croquet pitch** on a hill over Sandakan Bay — scones, cream and a mallet game, in the tropics [[20]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Tea_House_and_Restaurant)[[21]](https://www.englishteahouse.org/sandakan_index.html). *(Town / afternoon, beside Agnes Keith House.)*
- **Agnes Keith's "Newlands".** The reconstructed villa of American writer Agnes Keith and her husband Harry, the colony's Conservator of Forests; her 1939 book gave Sabah its enduring nickname **"Land Below the Wind"** (it sits just below the typhoon belt) [[17]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/agnes-keith-house/). Now a [Sabah Museum](https://museum.sabah.gov.my/exhibition/72) house, dressed in period furniture and the family's own artefacts and photographs [[18]](https://museum.sabah.gov.my/exhibition/72)[[19]](https://www.malaysia-traveller.com/agnes-keith-house.html). *(Town / Heritage Trail.)*
- **The Hundred Steps.** The self-guided Heritage Trail starts at the ~century-old Masjid Jamik and climbs the landmark "Stairs with a Hundred Steps" to a hilltop viewpoint — the stairs once led to colonial officials' quarters that no longer exist, so you climb to a ghost address [[22]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-heritage-trail/). *(Town / cool early morning; ~1.5–2 hrs whole loop.)*

## Ritual & religious curiosities

- **Three Saints and a divination jar.** **Sam Sing Kung Temple** (1887) venerates an unusual trio — the "Three Saints" — and holds **100 pre-printed Taoist divination poems** worshippers consult; its bronze bell was donated by Sandakan's first Kapitan Cina, Fung Ming Shan, the year it was built [[23]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Sing_Kung_Temple). At 138 years it's the heritage anchor of the old "Little Hong Kong" Chinese community [[24]](https://www.asiatimes.com.my/2025/06/12/sandakans-138-year-old-sam-sing-kung-temple-steeped-in-heritage-and-memory/). *(Town / Heritage Trail.)*
- **Seven steps to enlightenment, with a sea view.** Hilltop **Puu Jih Shih Temple** (1987) at Tanah Merah has a seven-tiered pagoda (one tier per step to enlightenment) and is rated Sandakan's best **sunset** spot over the bay [[25]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puu_Jih_Shih_Temple)[[26]](https://www.mysabah.com/puu-jih/). *(Town fringe ~4 km / late afternoon.)*
- **Rattan-ladder nest harvest.** At **Gomantong**, licensed collectors scale cave roofs on nothing but **rattan ladders, ropes and bamboo poles** to gather edible swiftlet nests (the bird's-nest-soup ingredient), under strict twice-yearly Wildlife Department quotas [[27]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomantong_Caves)[[28]](https://sabahguide.com/history/gomantong-caves/). *(Day-trip en route to Kinabatangan; harvest seasons ~Feb–Apr & Jul–Sep.)*

## Nature-oddities — beyond the orangutans

| Oddity | Why it's unusual | Where / best timing |
|---|---|---|
| **2-million-bat exodus** | A swirling vortex of >2 million bats pours out of Gomantong's cave mouth nightly — often pursued by bat hawks [[29]](https://www.clladventureborneo.com/gomantong-cave-borneo/) | Day-trip / ~5:30–6:30 pm |
| **[RDC](https://www.rainforestdiscoverycentre.org/) night walk** | Guided after-dark walk (~RM30) under Sabah's longest canopy walkway (620 m) hunting tarsiers, slow loris, civets, flying squirrels [[30]](https://www.rainforestdiscoverycentre.org/)[[31]](https://www.clladventureborneo.com/rainforest-discovery-centre-sabah/) | Sepilok / dusk, pre-book |
| **World's smallest bear** | [BSBCC](https://www.bsbcc.org.my/) rehabilitates ~41 sun bears — the smallest bear on Earth, found only in SE Asia, and easy to skip next to the famous orangutans [[32]](https://www.bsbcc.org.my/)[[33]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/bornean-sun-bear-conservation-centre/) | Sepilok / 9–3:30 |
| **Monkeys saved by accident** | [Labuk Bay](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/labuk-bay-proboscis-monkey-sanctuary/): an oil-palm planter spared a 400-acre mangrove pocket after finding proboscis monkeys on it — wild big-nosed monkeys inside a working palm estate [[34]](https://travel2next.com/sandakan-borneo-labuk-bay-proboscis-monkey-sanctuary/)[[35]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/labuk-bay-proboscis-monkey-sanctuary/) | Day-trip ~38 km / feeding times |
| **Sleep beside a hatchery** | Overnight on Selingan to watch a green turtle haul ashore and lay eggs at the [Turtle Islands Park](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/turtle-island-park/) hatchery — one turtle per night, by ranger call [[36]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/turtle-island-park/) | Day/overnight 40 km N / Mar–Oct |
| **Firefly rivers** | Evening mangrove cruise where thousands of synchronous fireflies light the riverbanks, usually with seafood dinner [[37]](https://sandakanhotspot.com/mangrove-cruise-fireflies-pamaguan-island-sandakan) | Day-trip / after dusk |
| **3,000 crocs + an Amazon fish** | Malaysia's largest crocodile farm (~3,000 reptiles) tucks a 100 kg Amazonian *arapaima* into its mini-zoo, 12 km out [[38]](https://sandakan.rainforestlodge.com/pages/sandakan-crocodile-farm) | Day-trip / near Sepilok road |

## Food rituals & a stilt village

- **The "cow dung" tart.** Sandakan's signature snack is the **UFO tart** — biscuit base, custard, torched meringue — but locals proudly call it *Cow Dung Tart* (牛屎挞) for its shape and refuse to rename it [[39]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/ufo-tart-of-sandakan/). It was an *accident*: Hainanese baker Fu Ah On scorched a batch in 1955 and the burnt version was a hit; **5 May is now "UFO Tart Day"** [[40]](https://explore.airasia.com/assets/bltee0901b9fd3bec49/ufo-tarts-sandakans-iconic-accidental-confection). *(Town / coffee shops & night markets.)*
- **The dawn fish-market ritual.** Sandakan's is the **biggest, busiest fish market in Sabah** — go at first light for the landings [[42]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sandakan-central-market/). The local-breakfast move at the three-storey Central Market: **dry kway teow topped with deep-fried pork** [[41]](https://www.sabahcarrentaltour.com/travel/destinations/sandakan-central-market-a-complete-guide-for-tourists/). *(Town / before ~9 am for the catch.)*
- **Where the city began, on stilts.** **Buli Sim Sim** is the original 1879 stilt settlement Sandakan grew out of — plank walkways over the water ~3 km east, with a former fish market expanded into a **floating seafood restaurant** [[43]](https://www.bilitrainforestlodge.com/attractions/bulisimsimwatervillage.html)[[44]](https://sunleisureworld.com/destination/malaysia/sabah-sandakan/tour/top-attractions/1203/buli-sim-sim-water-village). Free to wander; a lived-in fishing community, not a built attraction. *(Town edge / morning light.)*

## A loose offbeat itinerary

- **Town day (cool morning → sunset):** Central Market dawn + dry kway teow → Heritage Trail (Masjid Jamek bullet holes → 100 Steps → Sam Sing Kung divination poems → Agnes Keith's Newlands → English Tea House croquet & scones) → UFO tart → Puu Jih Shih for sunset.
- **War + island day:** Sandakan Memorial Park (rusting relics) → boat to Pulau Berhala (the Berhala Eight story, calm-sea months only).
- **Nature-oddity day (Sepilok/Kinabatangan side):** Sun Bear Centre + RDC botanic garden by day → RDC night walk for tarsiers; or push to Gomantong for the bat exodus then a Kinabatangan firefly cruise.
