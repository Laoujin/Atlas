> **TL;DR.** Eat where the *place* tells the story. For a once-in-a-trip dinner, pick a charcoal seafood table on the **[Sim Sim](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sandakan-food-at-sim-sim-water-village/)** stilt-village boardwalk over the sea [[3]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sandakan-food-at-sim-sim-water-village/) or colonial high tea on the croquet lawn at the **[English Tea House](https://www.englishteahouse.org/)** [[4]](https://www.englishteahouse.org/sandakan_agnes_keith.html). For the local soul of Sandakan, hunt the **UFO tart** and a bowl of springy "Little-Hong-Kong" pork-and-fish noodles in a heritage kopitiam at breakfast [[1]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/ufo-tart-of-sandakan/)[[8]](https://medium.com/@sixtybolts/your-definitive-guide-to-sabah-noodle-dishes-be9466fb04a9). In **Sepilok**, your evenings belong to a single standout jungle kitchen — **[The Nest](https://www.sepilokforestedgeresort.com/eat-drink)** at Forest Edge — because choice out there is genuinely thin [[5]](https://www.sepilokforestedgeresort.com/eat-drink)[[28]](https://www.sandspice.com/sepilok-borneo/). Eat seafood at lunch/dinner, kopitiam at breakfast, and book the no-menu fishermen's table a day ahead.

## Why Sandakan eats the way it does

Sandakan was the **"Little Hong Kong of North Borneo"** — by the 1920s–30s a cosmopolitan Cantonese-merchant port whose timber and rubber money built dense shophouses [[6]](https://sabahtourism.com/article/sandakan-little-hong-kong-of-north-borneo/)[[25]](https://evendo.com/locations/malaysia/sandakan/attraction/sandakan-the-little-hong-kong). Hainanese settlers founded the kopitiam (coffee-shop) culture; Hakka is the Chinese community's internal lingua franca and shows up on the plate — pork-laced "Hakka style" added to otherwise fish-heavy noodles [[7]](https://sabahguide.com/ethnic-groups/chinese-sabah/)[[8]](https://medium.com/@sixtybolts/your-definitive-guide-to-sabah-noodle-dishes-be9466fb04a9). Layer on Kadazan-Dusun raw-fish dishes, a Sulu/Bugis Muslim street-food side, and some of Borneo's freshest seafood, and the result is a small town that punches far above its size [[12]](https://clladventureborneo.com/famous-food-in-sandakan/). **Eat the heritage, not the hotel buffet.**

> ⚠ **Timing & traps.** Breakfast legends (Sim Sim noodles, fish noodles) routinely **sell out before 10am** — go early [[3]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sandakan-food-at-sim-sim-water-village/). **Kim Fung night market is Saturday-only**; the seafront Downtown market runs Mon–Thu evenings [[24]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/pasar-malam-kim-fung-night-market/). Best weather window is roughly **Mar–Sep** (drier); the NE monsoon peaks **Nov–Feb** — fine for town kopitiams but it can chop up Sim Sim/Kinabatangan boat dining. Heat is year-round equatorial, so the breezy waterfront and rooftop tables earn their keep.

Budgets are gentle: a full local day of meals runs **RM30–50 (≈ €6–11)**, night-market plates under **RM15 (≈ €3.20)** [[11]](https://milas.travel/top-best-foods-in-sandakan/). EUR conversions below use **€1 ≈ RM 4.70** (June 2026).

## Tables with a story

### Sim Sim Water Village — eat over the sea ◦ offbeat ◦ ~3 km E of centre
The **original townsite of Sandakan (founded 1879)**: hundreds of wooden stilt houses on the sea, linked by plank walkways, over a century old [[17]](https://www.bilitrainforestlodge.com/pages/buli-sim-sim-water-village). The famous eating is on **Bridges 7 and 8** [[3]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sandakan-food-at-sim-sim-water-village/).
- **[Kau Kee (九记), G68, Bridge 7](#)** — iconic spring noodle + century-egg dumpling; 6–11am daily, sells out before 10am [[3]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sandakan-food-at-sim-sim-water-village/). (Also written up as relocated "Bridge No.7 / Lee Hao Fook" — confirm locally [[10]](https://sabaheats.com/guide-food-hunting-sandakan/)[[36]](https://pureglutton.com/sandakan-food-trail-1).)
- **[Sim-Sim 88 Seafood (森森), Bridge 8](https://www.facebook.com/seafoodsimsim88/)** — steamed fish, butter prawns, clams, fried baby squid; "they don't even need to use MSG"; 6:30am–3:30pm & 5:30–9:30pm, cards OK [[3]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sandakan-food-at-sim-sim-water-village/)[[16]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g303997-d2442815-Reviews-Sim_Sim_Seafood_Restaurant-Sandakan_Sandakan_Division_Sabah.html). Salted-fish fried rice is a local order here [[11]](https://milas.travel/top-best-foods-in-sandakan/). Chef Ah Kit "insists on only cooking with the freshest seafood" — pre-order helps [[13]](https://travelwithlens.com/sandakan-food/).

### Persatuan Perikanan Sandakan (Fishermen's Association) — no-menu legend ◦ offbeat ◦ town
A **four-table, no-menu** room: you eat whatever was freshest off the boats that day, "all you need to do is to say 'yes' to all of them." Reserve ahead [[9]](https://www.foodiestravelblog.com/post/top-10-food-in-sandakan)[[10]](https://sabaheats.com/guide-food-hunting-sandakan/). The single most "Sandakan" dinner you can book.

### English Tea House & Restaurant — colonial high tea ◦ touristy (worth it) ◦ town hill
Set in **Newlands**, the restored colonial residence beside the home of writer **Agnes Newton Keith** (*Land Below the Wind*, 1939); "sprawling green lawns" and a **croquet lawn** overlooking Sandakan Bay and the Sulu Sea [[4]](https://www.englishteahouse.org/sandakan_agnes_keith.html). High tea, scones and a colonial-garden setting are the draw — food is "mixed to positive," view and atmosphere are the reason to come [[22]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Restaurant_Review-g303997-d1166938-Reviews-English_Tea_House_Restaurant-Sandakan_Sandakan_Division_Sabah.html). Expect "a single scone larger than the palm of your hand and a pot of tea" [[2]](https://www.traveloka.com/en-sg/explore/activities/top-5-must-try-eats-in-sandakan-sabah/48440).

### Ba Lin Roof Garden — sundowner with a view ◦ mixed ◦ town
**Sandakan's only rooftop garden bar-bistro**, on top of NAK Hotel, looking over the bay; western comfort food (burgers, pizza, pasta), cocktails, vegetarian/GF options, late dessert menu [[23]](https://sabaheats.com/balin-roofgarden-coolest-rooftop-garden-bar-bistro-sandakan/)[[9]](https://www.foodiestravelblog.com/post/top-10-food-in-sandakan). Come for the **sunset over the bay**, not a culinary pilgrimage.

## Markets & street food

- **Sandakan Central Market (Pasar Umum)** — three floors; the seafood-noodle and **"original homemade kueh teow with deep-fried pork" stall (Level 3, since 1940)** are the targets; 5–11am [[10]](https://sabaheats.com/guide-food-hunting-sandakan/)[[36]](https://pureglutton.com/sandakan-food-trail-1). *Waterfront / touristy-but-real.*
- **Kim Fung Night Market** — **Saturday only, 5–10pm**, ~6.5 km from centre; "hundreds of makeshift stalls" of Chinese + halal street food (satay, grilled chicken, BBQ corn, dumplings), lion/fish dance from 8pm; "an authentic local market… not a tourist-oriented destination" [[24]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/pasar-malam-kim-fung-night-market/). *Offbeat.*
- **Sanda'Gen Downtown Night Market** — on the **seawall at Coastal Road (Tembok Bandar)**, ~36 food stalls, breezy seafront eating, evenings Mon–Thu [[37]](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/8038822/sandagen-downtown-night-market). *Seafront / offbeat.*
- **Sejati Walk Night Market (Mile 7)** — Thu–Sun 4–11pm; nasi kerabu, laksa Penang, satay, apam balik, murtabak; easy parking [[2]](https://www.traveloka.com/en-sg/explore/activities/top-5-must-try-eats-in-sandakan-sabah/48440). *Local.*
- **Pasar Kim Fung (Mile 4)** — all-day stalls: pan-fried dumplings, paus, yam puffs, tau fu fah, chicken wings [[10]](https://sabaheats.com/guide-food-hunting-sandakan/)[[36]](https://pureglutton.com/sandakan-food-trail-1). *Local.*
- **Pasir Putih food court** — grilled clams (lokan bakar) over charcoal [[12]](https://clladventureborneo.com/famous-food-in-sandakan/). *Local.*

## Day-trip dining: Kinabatangan river lodges

If you overnight on the river (Sukau/Bilit) for wildlife, meals are **buffet, included, sourced locally** — simple but good, with local beer/wine. At some lodges dinner is barefoot, sarongs offered, with raised riverside tables for sundown views [[31]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g1137932-d655181-Reviews-Kinabatangan_Riverside_Lodge-Sukau_Kinabatangan_District_Sabah.html). Atmosphere over à-la-carte; you're here for the river, and the food keeps pace.

## Food tours & cooking classes

No dedicated Sandakan-town cooking school surfaced for 2026 — the self-guided **food trail** (Sim Sim → Central Market → kopitiams) is the move [[10]](https://sabaheats.com/guide-food-hunting-sandakan/)[[36]](https://pureglutton.com/sandakan-food-trail-1). For hands-on, the best Sabah option is a **Sabah Traditional Cooking Class** (Papar, near Kota Kinabalu): market tour + family-home cooking of rendang/likku with "Mummy Halimah," min. 2 people, book a day ahead [[32]](https://www.sabahtravel.com/attraction/sabah-traditional-cooking-class). Operators such as TYH Borneo also run Sabahan cooking experiences on request [[33b]](https://www.tyhborneotours.com/Cooking-Class).

(`[33b]` TYH Borneo Tours cooking class [[33]](https://www.tyhborneotours.com/Cooking-Class).)
