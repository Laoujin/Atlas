> **TL;DR.** Sandakan rewards culture over scenery: a half-day [Heritage Trail](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-heritage-trail/) [[9]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-heritage-trail/) ties the [Agnes Keith House](https://museum.sabah.gov.my/exhibition/72) [[1]](https://museum.sabah.gov.my/exhibition/72), hilltop Chinese temples and the sobering [Sandakan Memorial Park](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-memorial-park/) [[5]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-memorial-park/) together; Sepilok adds conservation-as-culture. **Best window: late May** lands you in **Pesta Kaamatan** (30-31 May 2026) [[21]](https://publicholidays.com.my/harvest-festival/), Sabah's biggest indigenous festival, inside the dry, wildlife-friendly Mar-Oct season [[42]](https://milas.travel/sepilok-orangutan-rehabilitation-centre-sandakan/). Other anchors: **Chinese New Year 17-18 Feb** [[25]](https://cutisekolah.com.my/en/public-holidays-2026/chinese-new-year/), **ANZAC Day 25 Apr** [[8]](https://www.facebook.com/sabahforestrydepartment/posts/1407065294780325/), **Hungry Ghost late Aug** [[26]](https://kalendarmalaysia.com/hungry-ghost-festival/). Avoid Nov-Feb monsoon downpours.

## Museums & memorials

**[Agnes Keith House](https://museum.sabah.gov.my/exhibition/72)** — *town, touristy (the cultural must-do).* Restored 1930s wooden bungalow of American author Agnes Newton Keith (*Land Below the Wind*) and her forester husband, now a Sabah Museum on the Jalan Istana hill [[1]](https://museum.sabah.gov.my/exhibition/72). Daily 9am-5pm; foreigners **RM15 (~€3.20)**, under-12 free [[2]](https://www.malaysia-traveller.com/agnes-keith-house.html). Pair it with afternoon tea next door (below).

**[Sandakan Heritage Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan_Heritage_Museum)** — *town, offbeat.* Small free museum on the 1st floor of Wisma Warisan (former British admin building), covering pre-/post-war town life plus the "Safari in Sandakan" gallery on 1930s explorer-filmmakers Osa & Martin Johnson [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan_Heritage_Museum) [[4]](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/malaysia/malaysian-borneo-sabah/sandakan/attractions/sandakan-heritage-museum/a/poi-sig/1577261/356962). ⚠ Reviews report intermittent closures — verify it's open before a special trip [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan_Heritage_Museum).

**[Sandakan Memorial Park](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-memorial-park/)** — *town edge (Sibuga), touristy with AU/NZ visitors.* Landscaped park on the WWII POW camp site with an interpretive pavilion. ~2,400 Australian/British POWs were held here; the Japanese forced them on three 260km **death marches** to Ranau in 1945 — only six men survived [[6]](https://www.dva.gov.au/recognition/commemorations/interpretive-centres-and-education/sandakan-memorial-park-malaysia). The **ANZAC Day dawn service (25 Apr)** [[8]](https://www.facebook.com/sabahforestrydepartment/posts/1407065294780325/) and **Sandakan Day (15 Aug)** [[6]](https://www.dva.gov.au/recognition/commemorations/interpretive-centres-and-education/sandakan-memorial-park-malaysia) are the year's two solemn cultural set-pieces; specialist tour operators run multi-day remembrance trips around 23-25 Apr [[7]](https://www.wetravel.com/trips/anzac-day-sandakan-death-marches-3-days-sandakan-experience-historic-war-tours-03008104).

**[English Tea House & Restaurant](https://www.englishteahouse.org/sandakan_index.html)** — *town, touristy.* Not a museum but pure colonial theatre: in the Agnes Keith House grounds, 1.5 acres of lawn with an actual **croquet lawn**, scones and clotted cream, Sandakan Bay views [[38]](https://www.englishteahouse.org/sandakan_index.html). Opened 2002, modelled on the Keith bungalow [[39]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Tea_House_and_Restaurant). The natural lunch stop on the Heritage Trail.

## Heritage Trail & temples

The waymarked **[Sandakan Heritage Trail](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-heritage-trail/)** (~1 hr, red-footprint tiles, *town, touristy-but-essential*) loops from the century-old Masjid Jamik to the Pryer Memorial (honouring founder William Pryer), up the **Stairs with a Hundred Steps** — the "Staircase to Nowhere" — to Agnes Keith House, St Michael's & All Angels Church, and two temples [[9]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sandakan-heritage-trail/) [[10]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan_Heritage_Trail).

## Markets & the water village

**[Sandakan Central Market](https://www.sabahcarrentaltour.com/travel/destinations/sandakan-central-market-a-complete-guide-for-tourists/)** — *town, offbeat-authentic.* The town's largest market and Sabah's seafood haven: ground-floor wet market (fish, prawns, crabs, dried anchovies, sago, spices), textiles and clothing upstairs. Go **early morning** for the fishermen's catch [[17]](https://www.sabahcarrentaltour.com/travel/destinations/sandakan-central-market-a-complete-guide-for-tourists/).

**[Kampung Buli Sim Sim](https://sandakan.rainforestlodge.com/pages/buli-sim-sim-water-village)** — *town (~10 min), offbeat.* Stilt water-village on plank walkways, built on the **original 1879 site of Sandakan** and the cradle of the local Bajau community [[18]](https://sandakan.rainforestlodge.com/pages/buli-sim-sim-water-village). Floating seafood restaurants serve curry beancurd, prawn balls and prawn noodles — lunch with your feet over the bay. Photogenic at sunrise/sunset, like Sabah's other Bajau villages [[20]](https://mytrip.my/bajau-villages-in-sabah-explore-malaysias-sea-gypsies-and-their-floating-homes/). ⚠ A lived-in fishing community, not a built attraction — be respectful with cameras.

**Tamu (weekly open-air markets)** — *day-trip, offbeat.* Sabah's rotating village markets sell produce, handicrafts and local goods; ask your lodge which nearby tamu runs which day [[34]](https://sabahtourism.com/activity/tamu-weekly-market-in-sabah/).

## Festivals in depth

**Pesta Kaamatan** is the cultural headline. This Kadazan-Dusun harvest festival honours the rice spirit **Bambaazon**, recovered in the *magavau* ritual led by *bobohizan* priestesses; expect the **Unduk Ngadau** beauty pageant, *sumazau* dance, *sugandoi* singing and **tapai** rice wine drunk from bamboo [[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaamatan). It runs **all May 2026** with district-level events including in **Sandakan**, building to the statewide grand finale at KDCA Penampang (near Kota Kinabalu) on **30-31 May** [[21]](https://publicholidays.com.my/harvest-festival/) [[23]](https://www.sabahtravel.com/articles/post/kaamatan-guide-2026-dates-events-and-where-to-celebrate-in-sabah) [[24]](https://sabahtourism.com/event/kaamatan-festival/). For a Sandakan-based couple, the local district events are the realistic catch; the Penampang finale is a full cross-Sabah trip.

**Sandakan's own festivals** lean into the port's Chinese-Malay-Filipino-Indigenous mix: the **Sandakan Festival** stages multicultural parades, lion dances and a "Taste of Sandakan" food fair [[32]](https://uaktravel.com/cultural-festivals-in-sabah-discover-the-soul-of-borneo), and the **[Sandakan Food & Agriculture Festival](https://www.facebook.com/sandakanfoodfest/)** is a 9-day culinary showcase [[33]](https://www.facebook.com/sandakanfoodfest/) — both typically May; exact 2026 dates are released on the town's event calendar, so confirm closer to travel [[46]](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/03/11/sandakan-bahyou-know-the-story-or-not).

The **Chinese calendar** drives the rest: CNY (17-18 Feb) [[25]](https://cutisekolah.com.my/en/public-holidays-2026/chinese-new-year/), Hungry Ghost (peak 27 Aug) [[26]](https://kalendarmalaysia.com/hungry-ghost-festival/), Mid-Autumn (25 Sep, lanterns + Sandakan's famed Chung Fatt mooncakes) [[27]](https://publicholidays.com.my/mid-autumn-festival/) [[28]](https://hellosabah.com/mid-autumn-festival-mooncakes-legends-and-lantern-light/) [[29]](https://www.chinahighlights.com/festivals/mid-autumn-festival-in-malaysia.htm).

## Crafts & workshops

*Note: Sandakan itself has no headline craft-workshop scene — the hands-on options sit elsewhere in Sabah.*

- **Batik** — *day-trip / KK, hands-on.* **[Galleria Artisan](https://mypoppet.com.au/makes/artisan-batik-workshop-craft-sabah-malaysia/)** runs visitor batik workshops and trains indigenous youth in 6-12-month textile courses [[35]](https://mypoppet.com.au/makes/artisan-batik-workshop-craft-sabah-malaysia/); Batik Sabah is promoted as a distinct state craft [[36]](https://sabahtourism.com/article/batik-sabah-a-trend-and-pride/).
- **Weaving, basketry, beadwork** — *offbeat.* Kadazan-Dusun and Rungus indigenous crafts; buy at tamu markets and handicraft stalls rather than expecting Sandakan studios [[37]](https://travel.earth/exploring-the-indigenous-arts-and-crafts-of-sabah/) [[34]](https://sabahtourism.com/activity/tamu-weekly-market-in-sabah/).
- **In Sandakan**, the realistic "craft" buys are dried-seafood and local-produce souvenirs from Central Market [[17]](https://www.sabahcarrentaltour.com/travel/destinations/sandakan-central-market-a-complete-guide-for-tourists/), and mooncakes/kuih from local bakeries [[28]](https://hellosabah.com/mid-autumn-festival-mooncakes-legends-and-lantern-light/).

## Sepilok: conservation as culture

Sepilok's "culture" is conservation and education, and it's the area's signature experience.

- **[Sepilok Orangutan Rehabilitation Centre](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sepilok-orangutan-rehabilitation-centre/)** — *Sepilok, touristy (the icon).* Rehabilitating orphaned orangutans since **1964** [[40]](https://www.orangutan-appeal.org.uk/about-us/sepilok-orangutan-rehabilitation-centre); platform feedings **10:00 & 15:00**, open daily 9-4 [[41]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/sepilok-orangutan-rehabilitation-centre/); foreigner entry **RM30 (~€6.40)** [[42]](https://milas.travel/sepilok-orangutan-rehabilitation-centre-sandakan/).
- **Bornean Sun Bear Conservation Centre** — *Sepilok, touristy.* The world's only place to see sun bears in native forest; **90,760 visitors in 2025**, and the sun bear is the **Visit Malaysia 2026 mascot** [[43]](https://www.theborneopost.com/2026/01/12/new-upgraded-facilities-at-bsbcc-sepilok-to-strengthen-sabahs-ecotourism-and-conservation/).
- **[Rainforest Discovery Centre](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/rainforest-discovery-centre-rdc/)** — *Sepilok, mid.* Sabah Forestry education hub with exhibition hall and Sabah's longest **620m canopy skywalk** [[44]](https://sabahtourism.com/destination/rainforest-discovery-centre-rdc/).

## Who lives here — the peoples

Sandakan is a melting-pot port, and that *is* the culture. The town was founded by the British (1879) and built by Cantonese, Teochew, Hakka and Hainanese Chinese immigrants whose temples still anchor town life [[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Sing_Kung_Temple) [[16]](https://www.mysabah.com/wordpress/sam-sing-kung-temple-sandakan/). The coastal **Bajau** ("sea gypsies") and mixed **Bajau Suluk** (Sama-Bajau/Tausug) maritime communities settled the Sim Sim shoreline and remain a defining presence [[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama-Bajau) [[18]](https://sandakan.rainforestlodge.com/pages/buli-sim-sim-water-village). The **Kadazan-Dusun** are Sabah's largest indigenous group and own Kaamatan [[22]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaamatan). Sabah's 2026 tourism storytelling explicitly foregrounds this Suluk/Bajau/Chinese layering [[46]](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/03/11/sandakan-bahyou-know-the-story-or-not) [[45]](https://www.grazia.my/culture/heres-why-you-should-make-a-sandakan-stop-in-sabah/).
