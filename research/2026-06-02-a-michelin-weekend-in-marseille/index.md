The planning spine is simple: reserve the dinner first, then build days around it. All three 3-star tables run narrow windows — [AM par Alexandre Mazzia](https://www.alexandre-mazzia.com) (24 seats, Wed–Sat) [[1]](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_restaurants_deux_et_trois_%C3%A9toiles_du_Guide_Michelin), [Le Petit Nice Passedat](https://www.passedat.fr) (Tue–Sat, ~30 seats) [[2]](https://www.passedat.fr/en/le-petit-nice/gastronomic-michelin-star-restaurants-marseille), and [La Villa Madie](https://lavillamadie.com) in Cassis (Thu–Sun until mid-June, then adds Mon through August) [[3]](https://lavillamadie.com/en/menu/) — and each choice shapes whether a Saturday or Friday arrival makes more sense. AM's 24-seat room is the hardest constraint of the whole weekend: it books out weeks ahead and offers a sub-€295 entry only at Wednesday–Friday lunch (the €115 "Premier Pas" menu) [[4]](https://www.thefork.fr/restaurant/am-par-alexandre-mazzia-r829913/menu).

The most efficient itinerary depends on which table you choose. **If you pick La Villa Madie**, the 30-minute TER from Marseille Saint-Charles to Cassis [[5]](https://sidequestsinfrance.com/best-day-trips-from-marseille-by-train/) lets you run a single day-trip that combines dinner in a private cove with a morning hike to [Calanque d'En-Vau](https://www.calanques-parcnational.fr/en/news/sugiton-reservations) — most dramatically entered from the Cassis side — and Cap Canaille, one of Europe's highest sea cliffs [[6]](https://www.viator.com/tours/Marseille/Provence-Sightseeing-Tour-Marseille-and-Cassis-Calanques-Cruise/d485-3547MARSEILLECASSIS). That compresses the expedition's two biggest experiences into one slot and leaves the other day entirely free for Marseille's city core: MuCEM + Fort Saint-Jean + Le Panier + Notre-Dame de la Garde.

**If you keep dinner in Marseille** (AM or Le Petit Nice), the Calanques become a standalone morning — boat tour from the Vieux-Port year-round (€27–33) [[7]](https://www.icard-maritime.com/visits-to-the-calanques/?lang=en), or a bus-and-hike to Sugiton from Luminy in October through May [[8]](https://www.moonhoneytravel.com/calanques-national-park-marseille/). The afternoon then links the [Corniche Kennedy walk](https://www.marseille-tourisme.com/en/discover-marseille/nature/hiking/walking-along-the-corniche-kennedy-marseille-7eme-en-3938337/) to [Vallon des Auffes](https://www.marseilletourisme.fr/en/things-do/take-stroll/city-hikes/corniche-prophete-beach-vallon-auffes/) — the tiny fishing port both children independently flag as the city's highest-density food cluster: [L'Épuisette ★](https://www.guidedesgourmands.fr/chef-artisans/guillaume-sourrieu-epuisette-marseille/), [Auffo ★](https://presseagence.fr/marseille-gastronomie-le-restaurant-auffo-de-coline-faulquier-decroche-sa-premiere-etoile-michelin/) (Marseille's newest star, earned March 2026, chef Coline Faulquier), and [Chez Fonfon](https://www.chez-fonfon.com/en/) for casual charter bouillabaisse — all three within 200 m of each other [[9]](https://www.marseilletourisme.fr/en/things-do/take-stroll/city-hikes/corniche-prophete-beach-vallon-auffes/). Le Petit Nice is also on the Corniche; choose it and you walk to dinner from the end of the walk.

One structural note: the third research angle — IT conferences and tech events — does not contribute to a leisure weekend anchored on a Michelin dinner. Its findings belong to a different trip. The one exception worth noting: the weekly [Marseille Tech Mixer at LOCHNESS](https://www.eventbrite.com/e/marseille-tech-mixer-and-social-tech-ai-data-it-tickets-1989646081991) (every Friday evening, free, near the Vieux-Port) [[10]](https://www.eventbrite.com/e/marseille-tech-mixer-and-social-tech-ai-data-it-tickets-1989646081991) is an easy add for a tech-adjacent visitor arriving Friday afternoon before dinner plans kick in.

The open question the research leaves unresolved: **does the restaurant choice drive the Calanques strategy, or the reverse?** La Villa Madie answers both simultaneously from Cassis. AM and Le Petit Nice leave the Calanques question open — and if you're travelling in July or August, Sugiton requires a free QR-code booking capped at 400 visitors per day, made 72 hours ahead [[11]](https://www.calanques-parcnational.fr/en/news/sugiton-reservations). Pick the restaurant first; the rest follows.
