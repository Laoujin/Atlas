> **TL;DR** — Kuching is Borneo's most relaxed city and an ideal first Malaysia base. Do these five and you've done the trip right: a full day at **Bako National Park** for wild proboscis monkeys, bearded pigs and Sarawak's oldest rainforest [[1]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g298309-d10810369-Reviews-Bako_National_Park-Kuching_Sarawak.html); the **Semenggoh** orangutan feeding (9am & 3pm, RM10) for near-guaranteed great apes a half-hour from town [[2]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g19755212-d1503816-Reviews-Semenggoh_Wildlife_Centre-Siburan_Sarawak.html); a **Sarawak laksa breakfast crawl** — Choon Hui Cafe is the Bourdain-anointed bowl, open 7.30am and sold out by lunch [[3]](https://sabaheats.com/choon-hui-coffee-shop-famous-sarawak-laksa/); the **sunset river cruise** (5.30pm daily, ~RM70, with layer cake and sape music) [[4]](https://sarawakrivercruise.com/signature-sunset-cruise/); and a day at the **Annah Rais Bidayuh longhouse** — bamboo architecture, 80+ families, lunch and tuak on the tanju [[5]](https://www.amazingborneo.com/package/kuching-day-tour-annah-rais-longhouse-visit-with-ethnic-lunch).

**Best window.** Aim for **April–September**, with **June–August** the driest and the festival-heavy peak [[6]](https://www.agatetravel.com/malaysia/kuching/best-time-to-visit.html). Kuching is the wettest city in Malaysia (~247 rainy days a year; January peaks near 660 mm), so avoid the **Nov–Feb northeast monsoon** when boat trips and the open sea get unreliable [[7]](https://weather-and-climate.com/average-monthly-Rainfall-Temperature-Sunshine,Kuching,Malaysia)[[8]](https://www.mytravellane.com/best-time-to-visit-sarawak-kuching-a-weather-guide/). One caveat: the **Rainforest World Music Festival (26–28 June 2026)** fills city and Santubong beds months ahead — a draw or a deterrent depending on taste [[9]](https://rwmf.net/).

**Practical flags.** Currency is the ringgit at ~**RM5/€**; for a comfortable couple budget roughly **€90–150/day** — a characterful heritage shophouse like The Ranee runs from ~US$80/night [[10]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/HotelsList-Kuching-Boutique-Hotels-zfp148723.html), while hawker meals are a few euros and the river taxi is **RM1–2 (€0.20–0.40)** [[11]](https://welovesarawak.com/tambang-time-travel-discover-the-heart-of-kuching-through-its-legendary-river-taxi/). The old town is fully walkable; Grab covers everything else, including the **RM12–15 (~€3) airport run** [[12]](https://my.trip.com/guide/transport/kuching-airport-to-city.html). Pick up a local eSIM at the airport — city coverage is fine. Etiquette: dress modestly at mosques and temples, remove shoes in longhouses, and accept the welcome **tuak** (rice wine) graciously [[13]](https://paradesaborneo.com/blog/things-to-do-in-kuching-for-10-iconic-events-and-festivals/). No notable scams, but two cautions: **Grab thins out when you're returning** from Semenggoh, Bako village or Santubong — pre-book or ask your driver to wait [[14]](https://my.trip.com/guide/transport/getting-around-in-kuching.html); and Semenggoh sightings drop in the **Nov–Feb fruiting season** when the apes forage in the forest [[15]](https://traveloutlandish.com/blog/semenggoh-wildlife-centre-orangutans-kuching/).

**Suggested 3-night plan.** Most of Sarawak's icons orbit a single Kuching bed, so base in the Old Town and day-trip out (see `around-day-trips-kuching/` for logistics).

- **Arrival** — Fly into KCH via KL (~1h50, from ~€7) or Singapore on Scoot; Grab to a heritage shophouse on or near Carpenter Street (`sleep-kuching/`) [[16]](https://www.skyscanner.com.au/routes/kulm/kch/kuala-lumpur-to-kuching.html). Start with that **laksa breakfast** (`eat-kuching/`), then walk the waterfront.
- **Day 1 — Old Town & river.** Heritage core on foot: Main Bazaar, the Borneo Cultures Museum (Malaysia's largest, RM50) [[17]](https://service.sarawak.gov.my/web/web/home/sla_view/0/646/), a RM1 tambang across to **Fort Margherita / Brooke Gallery** (RM30) [[18]](https://kuchingborneo.info/fort-margherita-kuching/), the **Darul Hana Bridge** lit up at dusk [[19]](https://mytrip.my/darul-hana-waterfront-and-bridge-kuching-the-crown-jewel-of-sarawaks-riverside-charm/), then the **sunset cruise**. Dinner at **Top Spot**, the rooftop pick-your-own seafood court [[20]](https://mytrip.my/top-spot-food-court-kuching-the-ultimate-rooftop-food-street-experience-in-sarawak/). More sights in `see-kuching/`.
- **Day 2 — Bako.** Full day, early start: bus/Grab to Bako village, charter boat to HQ, coastal trails for proboscis monkeys and bearded pigs; last boat back ~3pm (`do-kuching/`) [[1]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g298309-d10810369-Reviews-Bako_National_Park-Kuching_Sarawak.html).
- **Day 3 — Orangutans & longhouse.** Catch the **9am Semenggoh feeding** [[2]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g19755212-d1503816-Reviews-Semenggoh_Wildlife_Centre-Siburan_Sarawak.html), then continue to **Annah Rais** for the Bidayuh longhouse and lunch (`culture-kuching/`, `offbeat-kuching/`) [[5]](https://www.amazingborneo.com/package/kuching-day-tour-annah-rais-longhouse-visit-with-ethnic-lunch). Last dinner: native Dayak cuisine at The Dyak or Lepau, with **kek lapis** layer cake to take onward [[21]](https://kajomag.com/places-to-go-if-you-are-looking-for-indigenous-food-in-kuching/).
- **Onward** — The natural next Borneo base is **Kota Kinabalu**, a ~1h25 hop from ~€19 [[22]](https://www.airasia.com/flights/from-kuching-kch-to-kota-kinabalu-bki/).

**Before you book.** Check the staggered closures: **Fairy Cave shuts Mondays, Wind Cave Tuesdays** [[23]](https://ummiaroundmalaysia.com/fairy-cave-sarawak/); the **Annah Rais hot springs have stayed closed since Covid** despite lingering in tour titles [[24]](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g298309-d654659-Reviews-Annah_Rais_Longhouse_Adventure-Kuching_Sarawak.html); and the old Sarawak Museum is only partially open after long restoration delays [[25]](https://dayakdaily.com/tourism-minister-hopes-old-sarawak-museum-will-reopen-this-year-after-extensive-restoration-delays/). Plan boat-dependent trips (Satang turtle islands, occasionally the Bako crossing) for **May–September** [[26]](https://borneoadventure.com/tours/sarawak/satang-island-day-trip/). Travel responsibly: choose Semenggoh's semi-wild rehabilitation and Bako's wild sightings over caged-animal attractions, refill rather than buy bottled water, and tread lightly on the heritage trail and in living longhouses — these are homes, not sets.
